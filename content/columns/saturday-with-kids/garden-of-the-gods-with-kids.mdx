faq:
  - q: "Is Garden of the Gods really free?"
    a: "Yes — the park is a City of Colorado Springs park, free to enter, every day of the year, per the 1909 gift that required it. The visitor center across 30th Street is also free (its Pikes Peak balcony view is the postcard); you pay only for parking nowhere, the café, and the optional jeep or trolley tours."
  - q: "Can kids climb on the rocks?"
    a: "On the low, unposted formations near the trails — the sandstone scrambles by the Central Garden are a rite of childhood here. Anything requiring hands-above-head technical climbing needs a free permit and real gear, and rangers ticket scramblers on the big fins. The rule that keeps it fun: feet stay below head height."
  - q: "When should families go?"
    a: "Before 10am or after 4pm, year-round. Midday summer brings full lots, tour buses and hot, shadeless rock; mornings bring mule deer, empty trails and soft light on the red stone. Winter after a snow is the sleeper best version — red rock, white snow, no crowds — and the paved loop gets plowed."

Every Front Range family gets photographed at Garden of the Gods
eventually; the difference between a magic morning and a hot,
parking-starved slog is entirely scheduling. The park is free
because the Perkins family gave it to the city in 1909 on the
condition it stay that way — and the family-day blueprint has not
changed much since.

**8:30am, Central Garden lots.** The paved Perkins Central Garden
loop is 1.1 flat miles through the biggest formations — Gateway
Rocks, the Kissing Camels overhead — and it is the highest
scenery-per-step walk in the state: strollers, grandparents,
everyone. Mule deer graze the edges most mornings. Let the kids
scramble the low slabs beside the path (feet below head height; the
big fins are permit-only technical terrain and rangers do enforce
it), and watch the real climbers rope up on North Gateway.

**The photo.** Drive or walk to the **Siamese Twins** trailhead: a
half-mile round trip to the eroded window that frames Pikes Peak —
the picture on half the Christmas cards in El Paso County. Then
**Balanced Rock** on the park road, where the pull-off delivers the
other famous shot for zero walking.

**The exit.** The visitor center across 30th Street: free exhibits,
a short geology film kids tolerate, bathrooms, and the balcony café
— ice cream with the whole Garden and Pikes Peak behind it. If the
crew still has legs, [Red Rock Canyon Open Space](/answers/hikes-kids-actually-finish)
across Highway 24 offers the same geology with a tenth of the
people, or [Manitou's Penny Arcade](/columns/saturday-with-kids/manitou-penny-arcade)
is six minutes west.

Summer logistics: the in-park lots fill by mid-morning June–August —
the free shuttle from the overflow lots runs on peak days, and the
1805 N 30th St visitor-center lot adds a crosswalk but usually has
space. Water for everyone (the loop is shadeless), sunscreen
year-round, and closed shoes if scrambling is on the menu; the
sandstone eats sandals.
