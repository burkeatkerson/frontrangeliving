Every metro family logs a hundred hours at DMNS before the kids age
out, and the difference between the good visits and the grim ones is
pure logistics. The museum is huge, the crowds are predictable, and
the whole thing yields to a plan.

**The open.** Online tickets for 9am, the Colorado Boulevard garage
(museum side), and straight to **Prehistoric Journey** while the
atrium is still echoing. You get the T. rex and the diplodocus wall
nearly alone for twenty minutes — the same hall is shoulder-to-
shoulder by 10:30 on any Saturday, and worse on
[snow days](/answers/rainy-day-indoor-kids-front-range). Collect the
free **Discovery Zone** timed ticket on the way up if anyone is
under 6; the 11:00–11:30 slot catches the pre-lunch slump exactly.

**The middle.** Space Odyssey's floor staff run demos mid-morning;
Expedition Health's real-measurements floor works for the 6–10
crowd; the wildlife dioramas are the calm hall when the crew needs
one, and the gem-and-mineral mine is the underrated finisher. The
fourth-floor view of the Front Range labels every peak — free
geography lesson with the lunch you carried in (allowed, and the
move: the west windows or, with a re-entry stamp, the City Park
lawn).

**The exit.** This is what City Park is for: the **H2Odyssey splash
pad** across the lot in summer, the playground south of the museum,
the lake's pedal boats, or the [zoo](/answers/which-zoo-denver-or-cheyenne-mountain)
if you are members and insane. The gift shop is survivable by prior
treaty: one item under $5, announced in the car.

The money: $25.95 / $20.95 walk-up, under 3 free — and the ~$150
family **membership breaks even on visit two**, adds member hours
and reciprocal museums, and converts DMNS from an event into
infrastructure, which is its correct role. [Free community days](/answers/scfd-free-days-memberships)
exist and are mob scenes; members skip exactly that. Full context in
[the museum rankings](/answers/museums-with-kids-front-range).
