Every metro area needs the place where a two-hour toddler morning
reliably works; on the Springs' west side it is Bear Creek. The
county built a small, genuinely good nature center into the scrub-oak
foothills below Gold Camp Road, staffed it with naturalists who like
children, and made the whole thing free.

Inside: a kid-scaled exhibit room — crawl-through log tunnel, puppet
corner, track-matching games, live reptile or two — that absorbs the
under-6 crowd while the desk hands bigger kids a scavenger-hunt
sheet. The program calendar (story hours, creek-critter days,
full-moon walks, the summer camps that book out in
[the January rush](/answers/summer-camps-front-range)) is the quiet
best value in Springs parenting.

Outside: the **Songbird Trail** loop, under a mile of gentle gravel
through Gambel oak with the creek at the bottom — the canonical
[first kid hike](/answers/hikes-kids-actually-finish) on this side of
town — with longer loops up the regional park's ridges when the crew
graduates. Deer are near-guaranteed at the edges of the day; the
scrub jays run a protection racket at the picnic tables.

The pairing: mornings here, then ten minutes down 21st Street to
[Old Colorado City](/neighborhoods/colorado-springs/old-colorado-city)
for lunch, or up the hill for the [Cheyenne Cañon](/neighborhoods/colorado-springs/cheyenne-canon)
waterfalls when the kids reach the Seven Bridges age. Its sibling
facility — [Fountain Creek Nature Center](/columns/saturday-with-kids/fountain-creek-nature-center)
— runs the wetland version of the same morning on the south side,
and doing both in a season is a complete free curriculum in Front
Range ecology.

Practical: Tuesday–Saturday for the building (trails daily), real
bathrooms, shaded parking, strollers fine on Songbird if the
jogger-wheeled kind, rattlesnake signs honored by staying on trail.
The donation box deserves your five dollars; it is the cheapest
membership in town.
