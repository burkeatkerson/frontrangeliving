Bear Creek gets the foothills; Fountain Creek got the water — and
with small children, water wins. The county's second nature center
sits in the cattail sloughs where Fountain Creek spreads out south of
the Springs, and it delivers the thing toddler nature walks usually
only promise: visible animals, every single time. Turtles stacked on
logs, great blue herons stalking the shallows, frogs announcing
themselves, red-winged blackbirds at eye level, deer in the
cottonwoods at the edges of the day.

The loops are flat boardwalk and fine gravel — genuinely
stroller-proof, which makes this the default outing for the
two-kids-under-four stage and the visiting-grandparents Saturday.
The exhibit room is smaller than Bear Creek's but has the bird
window (feeders swarming, a spotting sheet, small chairs) that can
absorb a three-year-old for a suspicious length of time, and the
desk lends exploration backpacks and runs pond-dipping programs in
season — a net full of aquatic bugs being the single most reliable
astonishment in early childhood.

The naturalist calendar mirrors Bear Creek's: story hours, critter
days, the festival weekends, camps that fill in
[the January registration rush](/answers/summer-camps-front-range).
Between the two centers, the county runs a complete free
introduction to Front Range ecology — prairie wetland here,
foothills scrub [there](/columns/saturday-with-kids/bear-creek-nature-center)
— and a family that works both program calendars for a year has
out-educated most paid memberships.

Pairings: the [Fountain Creek Regional Trail](/answers/biking-with-kids-front-range)
runs right past for the bike crowd; [Fountain](/places/fountain)'s
Metcalfe Park splash pad is the summer add-on; and the return drive
through Security-Widefield hits Sonic exactly when the backseat
requires it. Practical: building Tuesday–Saturday, trails daily
dawn–dusk, real bathrooms, mosquito repellent June–September (it is
a wetland; that is the deal), and the donation box again worth your
five.
