The Penny Arcade is not a building so much as an accumulation: a
century of coin-operated machines packed into a series of open-air
passages and rooms off Manitou Avenue, run by the same family for
generations, and priced as if inflation were a rumor. Kiddie rides
from the 1950s: a quarter. Fortune tellers and strength testers from
the 1930s: a penny or a nickel. Pinball from every decade: quarters.

The genius of the place with mixed-age kids is that nobody strikes
out. The two-year-old rides a fifty-year-old rocket for a quarter
while the ten-year-old works the pinball rows, and the budget
conversation — here is your bag of quarters, when it's gone it's
gone — does the discipline for you.

The Saturday plan: arrive at 10 when it opens (summer weekends jam
by noon), spend the bag, then walk the avenue — the [springs-water
fountains](/answers/free-things-kids-front-range) with paper cups,
the candy shop, and lunch. If legs remain, the Barr Trail lot's
shuttle situation is a warning sign, not an invitation: with kids,
the [Garden of the Gods loop](/columns/saturday-with-kids/garden-of-the-gods-with-kids)
or the arcade's own second lap beats the Incline every time.

Bring: a sweatshirt (the passages are shaded and Manitou runs cool),
small bills for the change machines, and hand sanitizer. Parking is
the hard part — the Wichita/Cañon lots fill by 10:30 in summer; the
free shuttle from the edge-of-town lots is honestly easier, and to a
[transit-phase kid](/answers/train-obsessed-kid-colorado) it counts
as a second ride.
