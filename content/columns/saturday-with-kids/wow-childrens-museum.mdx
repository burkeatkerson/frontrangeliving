Boulder County's contribution to the children's-museum genre is
small, local and exactly right: two floors of a converted old-town
building where everything is scaled to people under four feet tall,
run by a nonprofit that clearly watches how children actually play.
For the east-county family — Lafayette, Louisville, Erie,
Broomfield, east Boulder — it is the rainy-day anchor and the
standing playdate venue of the 1-to-7 years.

The floor plan is the curriculum: the **water table** (smocks
provided, sleeves soaked regardless — pack the spare shirt),
the toddler-only zone for the crawlers, the **pretend town**
upstairs — vet clinic with a waiting room of stuffed patients,
market with scanners, stage with costumes — the climber, and a
real **art studio** with a staffed project that sends everyone home
with something drippy. Rotating exhibits keep the fourth visit as
good as the first, which matters, because this is a membership-or-
library-pass venue, not a one-off.

On that: the open secret is the **library pass** — Lafayette's
library (two blocks away) and several other Boulder County systems
lend free WOW! family passes exactly like books. The passes go like
bestseller holds; the app on Sunday night is the technique. Failing
that, **after 3pm is discounted**, and the 3–5pm window is also the
calmest the water table ever gets. Full price is $15 a kid / $9 an
adult, which for a three-hour toddler morning still beats every
paid alternative north of Denver — see [the museum rankings](/answers/museums-with-kids-front-range)
for how it slots against the Children's Museum of Denver (bigger,
further, $18 everyone).

The pairing writes itself: Old Town Lafayette outside the door —
lunch on Public Road, the WOW-to-ice-cream corridor — with
**Waneka Lake's** playground and [pond fishing](/answers/fishing-with-kids-colorado)
ten minutes west for the day the weather turns out fine after all.
