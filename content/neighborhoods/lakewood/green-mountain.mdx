Green Mountain is a 1960s and 70s neighborhood on the slopes below 2,400 acres
of open space, with the Front Range out the window and trails at the end of the
street.

## Where Green Mountain actually is

West Lakewood between Alameda and Morrison Road, west of Union Boulevard, on and
below the mountain itself.

## The park

**William Frederick Hayden Park** covers about 2,400 acres of Green Mountain with
an extensive trail network. It is among the largest open spaces contained inside
a Front Range suburb, and it is the neighborhood's defining feature.

**Bear Creek Lake Park** is a short drive south.

## The housing

1960s–70s ranches, tri-levels and split-levels, many with mountain views. Solid
construction, generally well maintained, and priced around or below the Denver
metro median.

## Who Green Mountain suits

Hikers and runners, families who want open space at the door, and anyone
splitting time between Denver and the mountains.

## What to check before you offer

**Wildfire exposure** on the upper slopes — this is grassland interface.

**Slope, drainage and retaining walls** on hillside lots.

**1970s systems** approaching replacement.

Prices here move with the wider market rather than independently of it. The
Denver metro single-family median was $650,000 in June 2026, with condos and
townhomes at $395,000 — useful as a baseline, but a neighborhood this specific
deserves a look at genuinely comparable sales rather than a city average. The
[property tax](https://dpt.colorado.gov/residential-local-government-assessment-rate) picture follows the same statewide formula
everywhere: assessment rate against actual value, multiplied by the local mill
levy.
