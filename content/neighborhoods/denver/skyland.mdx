Skyland is a handful of quiet brick blocks wrapped around the northern edge of
City Park Golf Course — small, easy to miss, and with better open space access
than its price suggests.

## Where Skyland actually is

North of [City Park](/neighborhoods/denver/city-park/) between York and Colorado
Boulevard, from 26th to 32nd Avenue, south of
[Clayton](/neighborhoods/denver/clayton/).

## The golf course

City Park Golf Course was rebuilt in recent years alongside major stormwater
detention work for the Platte to Park Hill drainage project. The result is a
better course and a large piece of engineered open space on the neighborhood's
doorstep.

## The housing

1900s–1950s brick — bungalows, squares and some ranches on the later blocks.

## Who Skyland suits

Golfers, park users, and buyers who want City Park adjacency without Park Hill
or Congress Park pricing.

## What to check before you offer

**Stormwater history** — this area was part of the drainage problem the
detention project was built to solve.

**Pre-1955 systems.**

Prices here move with the wider market rather than independently of it. The
Denver metro single-family median was $650,000 in June 2026, with condos and
townhomes at $395,000 — useful as a baseline, but a neighborhood this specific
deserves a look at genuinely comparable sales rather than a city average. The
[property tax](https://dpt.colorado.gov/residential-local-government-assessment-rate) picture follows the same statewide formula
everywhere: assessment rate against actual value, multiplied by the local mill
levy.
