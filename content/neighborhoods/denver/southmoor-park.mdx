Southmoor Park is a quiet pocket of 1960s and 70s houses at Denver's southern
edge, with a light rail station, canal trail frontage and a ten-minute run to
the Tech Center.

## Where it actually is

South-east Denver near the city limits, between Quebec and Ulster, south of
Hampden Avenue.

## The housing

1960s–70s ranches and two-storeys on standard suburban lots, plus some condo
development. Quieter and more owner-occupied than
[Hampden South](/neighborhoods/denver/hampden-south/) next door.

## Transit

Southmoor station is the practical draw, along with immediate I-25 access.

## Who Southmoor Park suits

Tech Center commuters who want a detached house and rail, and buyers who want
quiet at the city edge.

## What to check before you offer

**I-25 noise** on the western blocks.

**1970s systems** — panels, furnaces, roofs.

Prices here move with the wider market rather than independently of it. The
Denver metro single-family median was $650,000 in June 2026, with condos and
townhomes at $395,000 — useful as a baseline, but a neighborhood this specific
deserves a look at genuinely comparable sales rather than a city average. The
[property tax](https://dpt.colorado.gov/residential-local-government-assessment-rate) picture follows the same statewide formula
everywhere: assessment rate against actual value, multiplied by the local mill
levy.
