Denver's two rental ordinances are the most consequential local rules on
the Front Range because Denver is where most out-of-state investors start,
and because both are enforced by a department with a compliance budget.
Neither is complicated. Both get missed.

## The Residential Rental Property licence

**Who:** anyone offering a residential unit for rent for 30 consecutive
days or more — single-family, condo, each unit of a duplex or small
building (multi-unit buildings license the building), and ADUs.

**What:** a licence application, proof of ownership and a local contact, a
fee (modest, scaled to unit count), and a **passed inspection by a
city-approved third-party inspector**. The inspection covers the minimum
housing standards — smoke and CO alarms, egress, heating, electrical panel
and GFCI protection, plumbing, handrails, pest and moisture conditions —
and is closer to a rental-licence checklist than a buyer's inspection.
Expect $150–300 for the inspection and a short list of fixes on anything
built before 1980.

**Term:** four years. Renewal requires a new inspection.

**Transfer:** none. The licence belongs to the owner. When a licensed
rental sells, the buyer must inspect and apply afresh. The practical
consequence: schedule the licence inspection during your contract inspection
period, and make the seller's licence status part of your due diligence.

**Enforcement:** complaint-driven and by cross-checking listings against the
licence database. Fines escalate per day of unlicensed operation; the
ordinance authorises up to $5,000 a day for the most serious violations.
Unlicensed status also weakens your position in an eviction.

## The short-term rental licence

In force since 2017 and, since the 2019–21 enforcement cases, actually
enforced. The rule is simple: a short-term rental (under 30 consecutive
nights) is lawful only in the **licensee's primary residence** — the place
where they live most of the year, register to vote, hold a driver's licence
and receive mail. One person, one primary residence, one STR licence. An
LLC cannot hold one. A second home cannot be one.

Permitted within that rule: renting your whole home while you travel,
renting a room or basement while you are there, and renting a legal ADU on
the lot you live on. The licence requires a business file number, proof of
residence, liability insurance, and payment of Denver's lodger's tax.

Not permitted: an investor-owned unit operated nightly, full stop. Denver
revoked its first licences for primary-residence fraud in 2019 and has
pursued criminal charges for false statements on applications.

**The lawful alternative** for an investor is the 30-day-plus furnished
rental, which is a long-term rental under the code and needs only the
residential licence. See [the mid-term rental guide](/investing/mid-term-rentals-hospital-corridors).

## How the rules change the underwriting

1. **A licence line.** Small — about $10 a month amortised — but the
   inspection's fix list on a 1920s Whittier duplex can be $2,000–5,000 the
   first time through (panel, GFCIs, egress, handrails).
2. **No STR exit.** A Denver pro forma is a long-term-rental pro forma. If
   your model needs nightly income to carry, it does not carry.
3. **House-hacking is favoured.** The primary-residence STR rule and the
   owner-occupied exemption from HB24-1098 both reward living in the
   building. See [the house-hacking worksheet](/investing/house-hacking-denver-duplex-math).

## Other Denver provisions worth knowing

- **Tenant right to counsel** in eviction, funded by the city since 2022;
  contested evictions take longer here than in surrounding counties.
- **Denver's Expanding Housing Affordability ordinance** (2022) applies
  linkage fees and inclusionary requirements to new development of ten or
  more units, not to existing rentals.
- **ADUs** are allowed citywide after the 2024–25 rezoning; see [the ADU
  guide](/investing/adu-math-colorado).
- **Group living** (2021): up to five unrelated adults per household were
  allowed citywide before the state ended family-based limits altogether.
- **Lead-based paint** and **radon** disclosures apply as elsewhere; Denver
  also requires a lead-safe work permit for renovation of pre-1978 housing.
