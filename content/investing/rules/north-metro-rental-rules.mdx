The north metro is the metro's value tier for houses: 1960s–70s ranches in
Northglenn, Thornton and Federal Heights under $500,000, 1990s–2010s
subdivisions in Broomfield and Brighton, and the Reunion/Buffalo Run growth
belt in Commerce City. Regulation is light on long-term rentals and
scattered on short-term ones.

## Long-term rentals

No city in the north metro licenses or registers long-term rentals as of
August 2026. State law governs — see [the statewide page](/investing/colorado-landlord-law-2026).
Westminster and Thornton both considered rental registration in 2024–25
without adopting it.

## Short-term rentals, city by city

### Northglenn

Municipal Code Article 18-18. A licence is required for any rental under 30
consecutive days. The licence **does not require primary residence** and
the code does not cap nights per year, which makes Northglenn the north
metro's one investor-open STR jurisdiction. Requirements: business licence
(~$15), STR licence (~$175 new / $150 renewal), property inspection
affidavit, licence-holder affidavit, and the state's public-benefits
affidavit. Annual, non-transferable, surrendered on sale. Occupancy and
parking standards apply.

### Thornton

STR allowed only in the host's **primary residence, occupied at least 50%
of the year**; annual licence (~$180); city lodging tax. Investor units do
not qualify.

### Westminster

Regulations adopted May 22, 2023. A city STR licence plus business and sales
tax licences; accommodations tax; **no applicant may operate more than one
licensed STR premises** in the city at a time. Primary-residence
requirements apply in residential zones; check the current ordinance text
for the zone in question.

### Broomfield

City and County of Broomfield licenses STRs with a primary-residence
requirement in residential zones and a lodging tax. Much of Broomfield's
post-1995 housing also sits under HOA covenants that prohibit rentals under
30 days independently of the city.

### Brighton and Commerce City

Both license STRs; both restrict whole-home non-primary STRs in
single-family zones; both collect lodging tax. Commerce City's Reunion and
Buffalo Run HOAs add their own minimum-lease covenants. Call the clerk for
current text.

### Federal Heights

Tiny (about two square miles), largely 1960s–70s housing and mobile-home
parks; STRs are a licence-and-tax matter with few applicants. Federal
Heights is a long-term yield market — see [the north metro brief](/investing/arvada-westminster-northglenn-thornton).

## The rule that costs more than any STR rule

Mill levies. The north metro's school districts — Adams 12 Five Star
(Thornton, Northglenn, Westminster east of Sheridan, Federal Heights),
Westminster Public Schools, 27J (Brighton, north Commerce City, Thornton
east), Adams 14 (Commerce City south) — levy more mills than Denver Public
Schools, and the post-1995 subdivisions sit in metro districts on top.
Effective tax rates run 0.7–1.1% of value against Denver's 0.5%. On a
$500,000 house that is $100–250 a month of carry. Pull the parcel; see [the
property-tax guide](/investing/property-tax-for-colorado-investors).

27J also runs a four-day school week, which has been true since 2018 and
affects which tenants with children choose Brighton.

## ADUs

Every north-metro city amended its code for HB24-1152 by June 30, 2025.
The convertible stock is the 1960s–70s ranches with detached garages in
Northglenn, Thornton south of 104th, and old Westminster; the 1990s-and-later
subdivisions mostly carry covenants that prohibit them.

## How this changes the underwriting

1. **No long-term licence line** anywhere in the tier.
2. **Northglenn is the north metro's one non-primary STR option.** It is
   also a 1960s ranch market with modest nightly demand; underwrite it as a
   long-term rental with STR optionality, not the reverse.
3. **The tax line is 30–60% higher than Denver's** for the same value.
   This is the number that makes a $480,000 Thornton house carry like a
   $550,000 Denver one.

<Note label="Verify before you write">
  Westminster and Thornton have both revised STR rules since 2023 and the
  smaller cities publish ordinances rather than guides. Confirm with the
  city clerk's office before underwriting any short-term income.
</Note>
