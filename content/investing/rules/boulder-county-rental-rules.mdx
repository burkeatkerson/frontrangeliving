Boulder County is the most regulated rental environment on the Front Range,
and the regulation is old enough to be stable: Boulder has licensed rentals
since the 1970s and required energy compliance since 2010. The surprise for
buyers is not that the rules exist but what they cost to meet on a
1960s house.

## City of Boulder

### Long-term rental licence

Every dwelling rented for 30 days or more needs a rental licence. Two
inspections sit behind it, both by city-approved private inspectors who set
their own fees:

- **Rental licence (safety) inspection**, every four years, on new and
  renewal licences: alarms, egress, electrical, heating, plumbing, guards
  and rails, general condition.
- **SmartRegs compliance**, one-time per unit: a points-based energy
  checklist (insulation, windows, furnace efficiency, air sealing, water
  fixtures) or a HERS index score. A 1960s Martin Acres ranch that has
  never been touched typically needs $5,000–20,000 of attic insulation,
  furnace or water-heater replacement, and air sealing to pass. A
  post-2000 house usually passes as is.

The city fee is about $70 per building; the inspectors' fees are separate
and run $150–400 each. Renewal without a passed inspection is not possible.

### Short-term rental licence

Principal residence only, defined as the dwelling where the owner resides
more than half the year, proven by a Colorado driver's licence at that
address. The licensee must be a natural person, trust or nonprofit with at
least 50% ownership; the name on the licence must match the deed. Four-year
term with an annual affidavit and fee. Owner-occupied STRs are not
inspected; the owner self-certifies alarms.

Unlicensed STR operation is enforced through platform data and neighbour
complaints, and Boulder's code allows for licence denial on the next
application.

### Occupancy

Boulder's long-standing limits of three or four unrelated occupants were
superseded by HB24-1007 on July 1, 2024. Occupancy is now governed by
building and fire code only. See [the student rental guide](/investing/student-rentals-front-range)
for what that did to the Hill.

## Longmont

No long-term rental licence. Short-term rentals require a city STR licence
plus a sales and use tax licence, and **only City of Longmont residents are
eligible**. A resident may licence their own residence and at most one
investment dwelling; the entire dwelling may be rented to one party at a
time; individual rooms may be rented only with the owner or manager
present. Occupancy is two per bedroom plus two. Annual renewal.

For an investor who does not live in Longmont, that means long-term only —
which is how [Longmont](/places/longmont) is best bought anyway (see [the
Boulder County brief](/investing/boulder-county-longmont-lafayette)).

## Louisville, Lafayette, Superior, Erie

None licenses long-term rentals. All four license STRs, and all four restrict
whole-home non-primary STRs: Louisville and Lafayette require the unit to be
the operator's primary residence in residential zones; Superior allows
primary-residence STRs with a licence; Erie (straddling Boulder and Weld
counties) licenses STRs with a primary-residence requirement in residential
zones. Each charges a lodging or accommodations tax. Verify with the town
clerk: these four changed their ordinances between 2019 and 2023 and
Lafayette revisited its rules in 2025.

## Unincorporated Boulder County

The county licenses both **short-term dwelling rentals** (in a primary
residence) and **vacation rentals** (non-primary), with the vacation-rental
licence subject to zoning limits, septic capacity, a wildfire-mitigation
requirement in the mountain zones, and a neighbour-notice process. Mountain
parcels west of Boulder — Sugarloaf, Magnolia, Gold Hill — are where
investors have asked about these, and the septic and wildfire conditions are
where the applications fail. Nederland has its own STR permit with a cap.

## Niwot, Lyons, Nederland

Niwot is unincorporated (county rules). Lyons licenses STRs with a
primary-residence requirement in most residential zones. Nederland caps STR
permits; see [the mountain-town STR page](/investing/mountain-town-str-caps).

## How this changes the underwriting

1. **Boulder SmartRegs** is a capital line on any pre-1990 purchase:
   $5,000–20,000 before the first tenant.
2. **No STR story anywhere in the county** unless you live in the unit.
   Boulder County pro formas are long-term pro formas.
3. **Boulder's rental inspection every four years** is a recurring
   $300–600 and occasionally a panel or a handrail.

<Note label="Verify before you write">
  Boulder publishes its licensing rules clearly and changes them rarely;
  the smaller towns do neither. Call the clerk in Louisville, Lafayette,
  Superior or Erie before you underwrite anything other than a long-term
  lease.
</Note>
