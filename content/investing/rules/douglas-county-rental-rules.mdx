Douglas County is the metro's newest housing — most of it built since 1990,
almost all of it in master-planned communities with an HOA and a metro
district — and its rental rules reflect that. The cities differ sharply on
short-term rentals and agree on everything else: no long-term licence, and
the covenants do the regulating.

## Parker

Short-term rentals are **not permitted**. Parker's code classifies them as
"tourist homes," a use not allowed in residential zones, and the town has
pursued listings. Rentals of 30 days or more are ordinary residential uses.
No long-term licence. Parker's HOAs — Stonegate, Canterberry, the Pinery,
Stroh Ranch — generally add minimum-lease covenants of six or twelve months.

## Castle Rock

The town has **no STR ordinance**. An operator needs a town business licence
and must collect and remit lodging and sales tax. Council considered rules
in 2020 and again later and declined both times, saying complaints did not
warrant it. The Meadows, Founders Village, Crystal Valley and Terrain are all
HOA-governed, and their covenants typically restrict rentals under 30 days;
pull them. No long-term licence.

## Unincorporated Douglas County — Highlands Ranch, Roxborough, the Pinery, Franktown, Sedalia

Since 2023 the county requires an **annual STR licence** for any rental under
30 consecutive days. Key provisions:

- A dwelling in a **planned development zone district** is not licensed
  unless the PD expressly allows STRs — which excludes most of Highlands
  Ranch and the large master-planned areas.
- Occupancy, parking, septic and wildfire conditions in the rural zones.
- County lodging tax.

Highlands Ranch is additionally governed by the HRCA covenants, which
restrict short-term rentals. The county's licence is relevant mainly to
rural parcels in Franktown, Sedalia and the Perry Park area.

## Lone Tree and Castle Pines

Both license STRs with primary-residence requirements in residential zones
and both are HOA-dense enough that covenants govern. Neither licenses
long-term rentals. Lone Tree's RidgeGate has its own district rules.

## Larkspur

Small, rural, STR-tolerant with a town licence; outside the scope of most
investors.

## The rule that governs the whole county: the metro district

Almost every Douglas County home built since 1990 sits in a metropolitan
district that repays its infrastructure bonds through a debt-service mill
levy. Total levies of 120–160 mills are common; the Meadows in Castle Rock
alone carries hundreds of millions in district debt. On a $600,000 Parker
house that is $1,500–2,500 a year more tax than an equivalent Denver house,
for thirty years. See [the property-tax guide](/investing/property-tax-for-colorado-investors)
and [the new-build versus resale comparison](/investing/new-build-vs-resale-front-range).

Douglas County School District's levy is lower than most metro districts',
which partly offsets this — and the district's schools are a primary reason
tenants with children choose the county.

## ADUs

HB24-1152 applies to Parker, Castle Rock, Lone Tree and Castle Pines, and
each amended code by June 30, 2025. It does not override HOA covenants
recorded before the law, and nearly every Douglas County subdivision has
them. ADUs here are a rural-parcel question.

## How this changes the underwriting

1. **No STR income anywhere most people buy** — Parker by law, Highlands
   Ranch and Castle Rock by covenant.
2. **No long-term licence line**, anywhere in the county.
3. **The tax line is the story.** Every Douglas County pro forma needs the
   parcel's actual total mill levy and the metro district's service plan,
   not the county average. See [the Douglas County brief](/investing/douglas-county-parker-castle-rock).

<Note label="Verify before you write">
  Castle Rock and Parker have both said they would revisit STR rules;
  Douglas County's licence was adopted in 2023 and has been amended. The
  HOA covenants, which you pull during the contract's HOA period, are
  binding regardless of what the city does.
</Note>
