Colorado's statutes were quiet for thirty years and then were not. What
follows is every provision that touches a Front Range rental in 2026,
organised the way it reaches you: at the application, in the lease, during
the tenancy, at the end. Bill numbers are given so you can read the source;
this is a summary, not legal advice, and the 2027 session starts in January.

<Note label="Read this first">
  Several of these statutes carry a per-violation statutory penalty, a cure
  period, and attorney-fee shifting to a prevailing tenant. The cheapest
  compliance is a current lease form from your property manager or the
  Colorado Apartment Association, not a national template.
</Note>

## At the application

**Application fees** (HB19-1106): may not exceed the actual cost of
screening, must be the same for every applicant, and an itemised receipt is
required. A tenant may provide a **portable screening report** (HB23-1099)
from the last 30 days, and if they do you may not charge an application fee.

**Source of income** (HB20-1332): housing vouchers, disability income and
any lawful source count as income. You may not advertise "no Section 8" and
you may not set income-to-rent standards that effectively exclude voucher
holders (the standard applies to the tenant's share of rent).

**Criminal history** (HB19-1106): no consideration of arrests that did not
lead to conviction, or of most convictions older than five years.

**Credit** (SB23-184): for tenants with a subsidy, rental history or
income over 200% of rent may not be required; generally, income above 200%
of rent cannot be demanded. Eviction records older than seven years are
off-limits.

## In the lease

**Late fees** (SB21-173, C.R.S. 38-12-105): no late fee until rent is seven
days late; capped at the greater of $50 or 5% of the overdue amount; must be
in the lease; may not be charged twice for the same payment; cannot be
grounds for eviction by itself; cannot be deducted from a subsequent rent
payment. Violation: $50 statutory penalty per occurrence, plus fee shifting.

**Fee disclosure** (HB25-1090, in effect from 2026): advertised rent must be
the total mandatory monthly cost; every fee must be itemised and disclosed
before the lease is signed. Mandatory "amenity" or "administrative" fees that
are not disclosed are unenforceable.

**Prohibited clauses** (HB23-1095, SB21-173): no waiver of jury trial or
class action, no one-way attorney-fee clauses, no penalty for reporting code
violations, no automatic renewal to a longer term, no markup on utilities
beyond actual cost plus a capped administrative fee, no clause making the
tenant liable for the landlord's attorney fees in an eviction.

**Security deposit** (HB25-1249, effective January 1, 2026): capped at one
month's rent; installment option over at least six months on request; no
non-refundable "deposits" of any kind; pet deposits count toward the cap
(pet rent is separately capped by HB23-1068 at $35 a month or 1.5% of rent).

**Lease term and renewal** (HB24-1098): see below; the practical effect is
that a twelve-month lease no longer simply ends.

**Immigration status** (SB20-224): no inquiry, no disclosure.

**Bed bugs** (HB19-1328): landlord responsibility to inspect within 96 hours
of a report and treat.

## During the tenancy

**Warranty of habitability** (HB19-1170, SB24-094): the landlord must begin
repairs within 24 hours for conditions that threaten life, health or safety
(heat, water, sewage, electrical, mould, a broken lock) and within 72 hours
for others; the tenant may withhold rent, repair and deduct, or terminate if
the landlord fails. SB24-094 added a presumption of breach if the landlord
did not respond in time and a damages formula.

**Entry:** no statute sets a notice period, but the lease should; 24 hours
is the norm.

**Rent increases:** no cap. One increase per twelve months (HB21-1121),
with 60 days' written notice for most tenancies. For a tenancy past twelve
months, a rent increase that a court finds was intended to force the tenant
out can be treated as a constructive non-renewal under HB24-1098.

**Occupancy** (HB24-1007, July 1, 2024): no local limit on unrelated
occupants; only building and fire code standards apply. See [the student
rental guide](/investing/student-rentals-front-range).

**Utilities** (HB23-1095): pass-throughs must reflect actual cost with a
formula in the lease; the administrative fee is capped.

**Mobile home parks** have their own extensive statute and are not covered
here.

## At the end

**For-cause eviction and non-renewal** (HB24-1098, signed April 19, 2024):
after twelve months of tenancy, a landlord may decline to renew only for:

- nonpayment of rent;
- a substantial lease violation not cured after notice;
- refusal to sign a reasonable new lease on similar terms;
- no-fault grounds: demolition or conversion, substantial repairs that
  require vacancy, occupancy by the owner or a family member, or withdrawal
  from the rental market to sell. Each requires 90 days' notice.

Exempt: owner-occupied single-family homes, duplexes and triplexes;
tenancies under twelve months; certain employer-provided and transitional
housing. A tenant evicted without cause may recover damages and fees.

**Eviction procedure** (HB23-1120, HB24-1098): a 10-day demand for
nonpayment or cure (longer under some city ordinances); mandatory mediation
before filing when the tenant receives certain public benefits; a hearing in
7–14 days; appeal in 48 hours; sheriff execution. Denver provides counsel to
tenants in eviction. Uncontested, 3–6 weeks; contested, 2–4 months. Details
in [the remote-owner playbook](/investing/remote-investor-playbook-colorado).

**Deposit return** (HB25-1249): within 30 days (60 if the lease says so),
with an itemised statement; the tenant may request a joint move-out
walk-through and the landlord must itemise in writing anything beyond
normal wear. Withholding more than 125% of actual damages is presumed
wrongful; wrongful withholding is treble damages plus fees.

## What the state did not do

- **No rent control.** The 1981 prohibition stands.
- **No statewide rental licence.** Denver, Boulder and Aurora license; the
  rest do not, as of August 2026.
- **No statewide STR rule.** Entirely local; see the city pages.
- **No commercial property-tax rate on STRs.** Proposed and defeated in
  2023, 2024 and 2025.
- **No statewide just-cause for tenancies under twelve months.**

## What is coming

The 2026 session was the quietest for housing in five years. Bills
introduced and not passed — eviction-record sealing, a right to counsel
statewide, a cap on application fees — will return in January 2027. Watch
[the revision date on this page](/investing/colorado-landlord-law-2026).
