North of the Boulder County line and outside Fort Collins, the Front Range
gets simpler. Loveland, Greeley and the Weld County towns license and tax
short-term rentals without Fort Collins's zone map or Boulder's inspections,
and none of them licenses a long-term rental. What they have instead is the
cheapest housing north of Pueblo and, in Weld County, the lowest mill levies
in the northern corridor.

## Long-term rentals

No licence or registration anywhere in the tier. State law applies in
full; see [the statewide page](/investing/colorado-landlord-law-2026).

## Loveland

An STR requires a licence from the city's **Planning Division** — application,
proof of ownership or authorisation, fee — plus city sales and lodging tax
accounts. Loveland has not adopted a blanket primary-residence requirement;
the parcel's zoning and any HOA covenants are the limits. This makes
[Loveland](/places/loveland) the most STR-open city of its size on the
northern Front Range, and [Downtown Loveland](/neighborhoods/loveland/downtown-loveland)
and the Lake Loveland blocks are where the nightly demand sits. Confirm
current requirements with Planning; the programme has been revised since its
adoption.

## Greeley

Greeley's licence separates:

- **Accessory STRs** — part of an owner-occupied dwelling — permitted in all
  zoning districts with occupancy and frequency limits.
- **Primary STRs** — a whole dwelling as lodging — permitted only in
  commercial and industrial zones.

A city business licence and lodging tax apply; the licence number must
appear in all advertising. The practical effect for an investor is that a
whole-home STR in a Greeley residential neighborhood is not lawful. Greeley
is a long-term yield market — see [the Greeley / Evans brief](/investing/greeley-evans).
Greeley's former occupancy rule went with HB24-1007 in 2024.

## Windsor, Severance, Timnath, Johnstown, Berthoud, Milliken, Evans, Eaton, Wellington, Mead

Each licenses STRs and collects lodging tax; owner-occupancy rules vary by
town and by zone, and most were written between 2019 and 2024. Windsor and
Timnath, with the most new construction, rely heavily on metro-district and
HOA covenants. Evans, adjoining Greeley and UNC, is long-term territory.
Call the clerk for each.

## Unincorporated Larimer and Weld counties

Larimer County regulates STRs as "lodging facilities in residential
dwellings" with a permit in most zones, septic and access conditions, and
stricter review in the Estes Valley (see [the mountain-town STR page](/investing/mountain-town-str-caps)).
Weld County permits STRs in its residential and agricultural zones with a
use-by-special-review process in some.

## The two facts that matter more than the STR rules

**Weld County's mill levies are low.** Greeley-Evans District 6 and the
Weld RE districts levy less than Denver-metro school districts, and most
of Greeley's housing predates metro districts. Effective tax rates of
0.5–0.6% on the older stock; 0.9–1.1% on new-build in Windsor, Severance
and Johnstown metro districts. Pull the parcel.

**Water.** Every Northern Colorado town prices water tap fees and raw-water
dedication into new construction at $20,000–60,000 per unit, which is why
an ADU in Windsor costs more than one in Denver and why older stock with
existing taps carries a hidden premium. Irrigation restrictions in dry years
are a lease clause, not a surprise.

## How this changes the underwriting

1. **No licence lines** on long-term rentals anywhere.
2. **Loveland is the northern corridor's STR-open city**; Greeley and the
   residential zones of the small towns are effectively long-term only.
3. **Weld's tax line is the lowest north of Pueblo** on resale housing,
   and the highest in new-build metro districts. The parcel decides.

<Note label="Verify before you write">
  These cities publish ordinances, not guides, and several revised them
  in 2024 and 2025. A call to the clerk or planning counter before you
  write an offer is the due diligence.
</Note>
