Pueblo is the lightest-touch rental jurisdiction on the Front Range and the
one where an absentee owner most often gets into trouble anyway — not with
the licensing department, but with code enforcement and the tenant pool.

## Short-term rentals

An annual city STR licence is required for any rental under 30 days:
application, proof of ownership (or a lease with the owner's authorisation),
proof of liability insurance, city sales and lodging tax accounts, and a
state sales tax licence (renewed every two years). The city does not impose
a Denver-style primary-residence requirement; zoning and HOA covenants
apply. Enforcement is complaint-driven and reasonably firm.

The nightly market is small: the Riverwalk and Union Avenue district, the
Colorado State Fair in late August, and Lake Pueblo's summer season. A
[Downtown or North Side](/neighborhoods/pueblo/pueblo-north-side) house can
do well for ten weeks a year and sit for twenty. Underwrite the long-term
lease and treat nightly income as upside.

## Long-term rentals

No licence, no registration, no inspection programme. State law applies,
and two parts of it land harder in Pueblo than elsewhere:

- **HB25-1249's one-month deposit cap** (from January 1, 2026) in a market
  where turnover costs on older houses are high and tenant incomes are
  thinner. Screen for rental history and price the wear into rent.
- **HB24-1098's for-cause requirement** after twelve months, in a market
  where owners have historically used non-renewal as the cheap exit from a
  problem tenancy. Document lease violations from the first month. See [the
  statewide page](/investing/colorado-landlord-law-2026).

## The codes that bite

Pueblo's code enforcement division is active, and citations attach to the
owner:

- **Weeds and grass over six inches** — cited from May through September,
  abated by the city at the owner's cost if not cured, and liened.
- **Trash, debris, inoperable vehicles** in yards and alleys.
- **Exterior property maintenance** — peeling paint, broken windows,
  failing roofs and fences — under the International Property Maintenance
  Code.
- **Sidewalk snow** within 24 hours.
- **Vacant building registration** for properties empty over a set period.

A manager who drives the property monthly and a lease that assigns yard
and snow duty to the tenant are the compliance programme.

## Taxes

Pueblo's city mill levy runs around 95, against Denver's high 70s, because
District 60's levy is high and the city's own levy is not small; Pueblo West
(unincorporated, District 70, its own metro district) is similar. Low values
keep the bills small — roughly $2,000 a year on a $300,000 house — but the
effective rate of about 0.65% is above the Denver metro's. See [the
property-tax guide](/investing/property-tax-for-colorado-investors).

## ADUs

Pueblo is a subject jurisdiction under HB24-1152 and amended its code by the
deadline; the city's older neighborhoods have alley-loaded lots and
detached garages that convert cheaply. At Pueblo rents ($1,000–1,200 for an
ADU) the build math works only on conversions. See [the ADU guide](/investing/adu-math-colorado).

## How this changes the underwriting

1. **No licence lines.**
2. **A real code-enforcement line** — $50–100 a month for a local manager's
   drive-by and yard service is not optional for an absentee owner.
3. **Nightly income is seasonal upside**, not the base case.

The [Pueblo worksheet](/investing/rent-vs-carry-pueblo) and [the Pueblo
brief](/investing/pueblo-for-yield) carry the numbers.
