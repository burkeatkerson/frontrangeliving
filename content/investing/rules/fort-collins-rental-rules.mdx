Fort Collins is a clean regulatory environment for a long-term landlord and
a mapped one for a short-term operator. The city never adopted a rental
licence, lost its famous occupancy rule to state law in 2024, and regulates
STRs by drawing lines on a map that you can look up by address. Most of the
surprises here come from not looking.

## Short-term rentals

**Definition:** a stay of fewer than 30 consecutive days.

**Two licence types:**

- **Primary STR** — the operator's primary residence (or a unit on the same
  lot). Allowed in most residential zones.
- **Non-primary STR** — an investor-owned unit. Allowed only in zones the
  city's map shows as permitting both types: chiefly the Downtown, Community
  Commercial, and some mixed-use and older core zones. Not allowed in the
  Low Density Residential and most Medium Density zones that make up the
  city's subdivisions.

**Building type:** single-family and two-unit buildings only. A condo or
apartment in a building of three or more units cannot be an STR regardless
of zone.

**Licence:** $150 initial, $100 annual renewal, due by June 30 each year;
proof of liability insurance, off-street parking, a city sales and lodging
tax account, and a local contact. Occupancy limits follow bedrooms. The city
applies a dispersion standard in residential zones to keep STRs from
clustering — check the current distance on the city's page, as it has been
revised.

**Enforcement:** platform monitoring plus complaints; operating without a
licence or outside the mapped zones is a municipal offence with per-day
fines.

Practical effect: an investor who wants nightly income in Fort Collins is
buying in or near Old Town and the Downtown zone, where the prices start
around $600,000 for anything with two bedrooms and the competition is
hotels. See [the Fort Collins / Loveland / Windsor brief](/investing/fort-collins-loveland-windsor).

## Long-term rentals

No licence, no registration, no inspection programme as of August 2026.
Council has studied rental registration twice; watch the revision date.

What does apply:

- **State law** in full — see [the statewide page](/investing/colorado-landlord-law-2026).
- **Occupancy by building code**: since the July 2024 repeal of U+2, the
  limit is the number of legal bedrooms (egress, size, separation), not the
  number of unrelated people. Fort Collins also still enforces its
  **extra-occupancy rental house** land-use process for houses that want to
  add bedrooms beyond what the zone's parking standard supports — a permit
  and a parking plan, not a ban.
- **Property maintenance and nuisance codes**, with a three-strikes public
  nuisance ordinance that lands on the owner.
- **Snow removal** within 24 hours on adjacent sidewalks; the city fines the
  owner, and the lease should pass it to the tenant.

## What the end of U+2 did

For twenty years, a four-bedroom house near CSU rented three bedrooms
because the fourth tenant was illegal. From August 2024 it rents four. Rents
on the Campus West and Sheely blocks moved up by roughly one bedroom's worth
over the 2024–25 leasing cycle, and four- and five-bedroom houses re-priced
relative to threes. The [student rental guide](/investing/student-rentals-front-range)
has the numbers.

## ADUs

Fort Collins complied with HB24-1152 by the June 30, 2025 deadline: one ADU
per single-family lot, administrative review, no owner-occupancy
requirement. The city's water plant-investment fee and the Fort
Collins–Loveland Water District fees outside the city's own service area
add $10,000–25,000 to a detached ADU compared with Denver. See [the ADU
guide](/investing/adu-math-colorado).

## How this changes the underwriting

1. **Long-term is frictionless.** No licence line, no inspection line.
2. **STR only where mapped.** Pull the parcel on the map before you
   underwrite a nightly rate; most subdivisions are primary-only.
3. **Bedrooms are rent.** Since 2024 a legal fifth bedroom near CSU is worth
   $900–1,100 a month. An egress window costs about $4,000.

<Note label="Verify before you write">
  The STR map is the authority, and the city redraws zones. Look up the
  address on the city's STR page the week you write the offer, not the week
  you first saw the listing.
</Note>
