Aurora is the metro's most important rental submarket outside Denver —
roughly 400,000 people, the Anschutz Medical Campus, Buckley Space Force
Base, and the largest stock of 1970s–2000s attached housing in the state —
and it regulates the small landlord more than anyone but Denver and Boulder.
The rules are workable. They need to be in the file.

## The residential rental licence

Adopted in 2023, Aurora's programme licenses residential rentals of one to
four units. The elements:

- **Application and fee**, annual or multi-year depending on the current
  schedule.
- **Inspection** against the city's minimum housing standards on a periodic
  cycle, with re-inspection on complaint.
- **The crime-free lease addendum** as a licence condition (below).
- **A local responsible party** who can respond to the city.

Larger buildings fall under the city's existing multifamily inspection
regime. Enforcement is complaint-driven and through code enforcement; the
penalty for operating unlicensed is a municipal citation with daily fines and
the inability to use the city's eviction-support processes.

Because the programme has been adjusted since adoption — fees, cycle, and
which units are exempt — confirm the current requirements on the city's
landlord-resources pages before you close.

## Short-term rentals

Under Chapter 26, Article V of the city code:

- An STR is any residential unit offered for fewer than 30 consecutive days.
- It requires a **general business licence** and a **lodger's tax licence**
  before it is advertised. Advertising without both is itself an offence.
- It must be the operator's **primary residence**. Non-primary units cannot
  be licensed.
- An **entire dwelling** may be rented short-term for no more than **180
  nights in any 365-day period**. Room rentals with the host present are not
  capped.
- The operator collects Aurora's lodger's tax on every stay.

Aurora enforces through platform data; § 26-219 lists the unlawful acts
(operating without licences, exceeding 180 nights, advertising without a
licence number) and each carries a fine.

For an investor, the STR question in Aurora is closed. The mid-term (30+
day) furnished rental to Anschutz staff is lawful, needs only the
residential rental licence, and is the better business anyway — see [the
mid-term rental guide](/investing/mid-term-rentals-hospital-corridors).

## The crime-free programme

Aurora's police department runs one of the country's older crime-free
multi-housing programmes. For a licensed small landlord it means: the
addendum in every lease, access to training, and a clear lease ground when
a tenant or guest is involved in criminal activity. Under HB24-1098 a
substantial lease violation remains a for-cause ground, and the addendum is
how Aurora landlords document one.

## Other Aurora specifics

- **Two school districts, three counties.** Aurora spans Adams, Arapahoe
  and a sliver of Douglas; Cherry Creek and Aurora Public Schools split the
  city along a line that matters to tenants with children and to mill levies.
  See [the Aurora brief](/investing/aurora-anschutz-corridor).
- **HOA-heavy attached stock.** Much of Aurora's 1990s–2010s townhome and
  condo inventory carries minimum-lease-term and rental-cap covenants. Pull
  them before your HOA deadline.
- **ADUs** are allowed under the city's HB24-1152 compliance, one per
  single-family lot, though covenants in the newer subdivisions often
  prohibit them.
- **Metro districts** in the east — Southlands, the E-470 corridor, Murphy
  Creek, Green Valley Ranch-adjacent — add 40–70 mills. See [the property-tax
  guide](/investing/property-tax-for-colorado-investors).

## How this changes the underwriting

1. **A licence line and an inspection line**, like Denver's.
2. **No nightly rental income** on an investor-owned unit; 30-day furnished
   is the lawful ceiling.
3. **The addendum is a strength** in an eviction under HB24-1098; use the
   city's form.
