The insurance line on a Front Range pro forma has doubled in six years, and
most out-of-state investors are still carrying the number from their home
state. Colorado's average homeowners premium reached about $6,630 in 2026 —
fourth highest in the country — after rising roughly 100% since 2020. The
state's own analysis found that hail accounts for 26–54% of premiums
depending on county and about half along the Front Range and eastern
plains, where most of the population lives. Wildfire is a smaller share of
the average and a much larger share of the problem in the specific places
it applies.

## What a landlord policy costs, by submarket

A DP-3 (dwelling fire, special form) landlord policy on a $500,000 house
with $450,000 of dwelling coverage, $500,000 liability, loss of rent, and a
1% wind/hail deductible, August 2026:

| Submarket | Annual premium | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Denver, Lakewood, Arvada, Wheat Ridge, Englewood | $2,400–3,600 | Hail regular; 2017 storm history on the west side |
| Boulder, Longmont, Louisville, Lafayette | $2,600–4,000 | Marshall Fire loaded the county; the western edges higher |
| Fort Collins, Loveland, Greeley | $2,400–3,800 | Hail; the 2018 Fort Collins storm |
| Aurora, Commerce City, Brighton, Thornton | $3,000–4,500 | The hail plain; 2023–24 storm losses |
| Parker, Castle Rock, Highlands Ranch | $2,800–4,200 | Hail; wildfire on the west edge |
| Colorado Springs, east and south | $3,000–4,800 | Hail; the 2018 Fountain storm |
| Colorado Springs, north and west foothills | $3,500–6,000 | Waldo Canyon and Black Forest history; WUI surcharges |
| Pueblo, Pueblo West | $2,600–4,000 | Hail; Pueblo West's wildland edge |
| Evergreen, Conifer, Nederland, Estes Park | $4,000–8,000 or unavailable | WUI; several carriers have exited; FAIR Plan as backstop |

Condos: the unit-owner HO-6 is cheap ($400–900) and the association's
master policy is where the cost landed — see [condo versus townhome versus
house](/investing/condo-vs-townhome-vs-house-investing).

## The deductible is the story

Between 2022 and 2025 most Front Range policies moved from a flat $1,000–
2,500 wind/hail deductible to a **percentage of dwelling coverage**: 1%,
increasingly 2%, sometimes 5% in the hardest-hit zip codes. On a $450,000
dwelling limit that is $4,500, $9,000 or $22,500 per hail claim. A roof that
costs $22,000 to replace after a storm, on a 2% deductible, nets $13,000
from the carrier.

Two consequences:

1. **The deductible belongs in the reserve.** Four months of carry plus the
   wind/hail deductible is the number we give remote owners.
2. **Roof age is an underwriting line.** Carriers write roofs over 10–15
   years at actual cash value (depreciated), not replacement cost, and
   non-renew at 20. A Class 4 impact-rated roof earns a 10–30% premium
   discount and sometimes a lower deductible. See [the inspection
   notes](/investing/what-inspectors-find-front-range).

## Wildfire: the WUI

Colorado's wildland-urban interface runs the length of the Front Range
foothills, and after the 2012 Waldo Canyon and 2013 Black Forest fires in
the Springs, the 2020 Cameron Peak and East Troublesome fires in the north,
and the 2021 Marshall Fire in Boulder County — a grass fire that burned
1,000 suburban homes — carriers have re-rated or left the affected zip codes.
Evergreen, Conifer, Genesee, Nederland, the Boulder mountain parcels, the
Springs' Mountain Shadows and Peregrine, Roxborough, and Estes Park are
where a standard-market quote may not exist.

What works: a wildfire mitigation assessment (Colorado State Forest Service
or the county), documented defensible space, a Class A roof, ember-resistant
vents, and shopping surplus-lines and regional carriers. The Colorado FAIR
Plan (operational since 2025) is the backstop — bare coverage at a high
price. For an investor, a house that only the FAIR Plan will write is a
house to reconsider.

## What else is on the policy

- **Loss of rent** (fair rental value): 12 months minimum. A hail roof or a
  kitchen fire is three months of vacancy.
- **Liability:** $500,000 on the policy and a $1 million umbrella ($300–500
  a year) over it. Colorado juries are not shy.
- **Water backup:** sewer and drain backup is excluded by default and is the
  Front Range's most common non-hail claim. Add it.
- **Ordinance or law:** 10–25%; a 1958 house rebuilt after a fire must meet
  2024 code.
- **Vacancy clause:** most DP-3s suspend coverage after 30–60 days vacant.
  Tell the carrier during a long make-ready.

## The 2026 law

SB26-155, signed June 4, 2026, created the Strengthen Colorado Homes
enterprise inside the Division of Insurance to run a grant programme for
hail-resistant roofs on the Alabama FORTIFIED model, after a 2025 attempt
failed. It is funded by a small insurer assessment and prioritises
owner-occupants; in El Paso County the state estimated a fortified roof
could save about $390 a year in premium. For landlords the near-term
benefit is indirect — carriers already discount Class 4 roofs — but the
programme is the state's first structural answer to hail, and it is the one
to watch.

## What we do on every purchase

Bind the quote before the inspection objection deadline, not after. Ask for
the wind/hail deductible in dollars. Pull the CLUE report. Price a Class 4
roof into the first replacement. Put the deductible in the reserve. And on
anything west of Highway 93 or Highway 115, get the quote before the
inspection at all.
