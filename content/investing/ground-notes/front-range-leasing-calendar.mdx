A Colorado rental runs on a calendar that is not the one in the lease
template. The leasing market has a season as sharp as a ski resort's; the
military, the universities and the legislature each add a date; and the
house itself has four jobs a year that somebody has to be assigned in
writing. This is the year, month by month, for a Front Range landlord.

## The leasing season

Single-family turnover on the Front Range clusters hard: roughly 70% of
annual move-ins happen between May and August, driven by school calendars,
the weather, and the fact that nobody wants to move a couch in a January
wind. Days on market for a three-bed house in 2026 ran about two weeks in
June and five to six in December. Rents follow: a house listed in November
typically leases for $100–200 less than the same house in June, and to a
thinner applicant pool.

The practical rule: **write every lease to expire between May 1 and August
31**, even if the first term is 14 or 16 months to get there. A tenant who
wants to leave in October is a tenant you offer a month-to-month at a
premium until spring.

## The cycles that shift it

**Colorado Springs — PCS season.** Military moves peak from May to August
and again, smaller, in December–January. BAH resets on January 1, and
landlords who raise rent on renewals do it then. See [the military rental
worksheet](/investing/rent-vs-carry-colorado-springs-military-rental).

**College towns — August 1.** CSU, CU, UNC, DU, UCCS and Mines houses turn
on a single week. A student house that is vacant on September 1 is vacant
until May. See [the student rental guide](/investing/student-rentals-front-range).

**Hospital corridors — 13 weeks.** Travel-nurse contracts start on a rolling
basis with peaks in January and July; mid-term furnished units turn on
that rhythm. See [the mid-term rental guide](/investing/mid-term-rentals-hospital-corridors).

**The legislature — January 1.** New statutes take effect at the new year
(HB25-1249's deposit rules did on January 1, 2026); the lease form changes
annually. See [the statewide rules](/investing/colorado-landlord-law-2026).

## The four jobs the lease must assign

**Sprinklers.** Front Range irrigation systems are blown out with compressed
air before the first hard freeze — by mid-October — and started up after the
last one in April, with an annual backflow-preventer test that most cities
require and many landlords forget. A missed blow-out is a $1,500–4,000
repair in spring. Assign it to a contractor and bill it, or assign it to the
tenant with a date; do not leave it to memory.

**Furnace.** Service in October. Colorado's habitability statute treats loss
of heat as a 24-hour repair item, and the first cold snap is when every
furnace in the metro fails at once. A $150 service call in October is a
$600 emergency call in December.

**Snow.** Denver requires sidewalks cleared within 24 hours after a storm
ends (business districts sooner); Boulder, Fort Collins, Lakewood and most
others have equivalent ordinances, and the fine goes to the owner. On a
single-family house, assign it to the tenant in the lease and provide a
shovel; on anything with shared walks, hire a service at $30–60 per event.

**Hail.** After any storm with hail over an inch, the roof should be
photographed within a week and any damage reported within the policy's
window (often one year, sometimes less). Tenants do not look at roofs. A
manager or a roofer on a standing arrangement does. See [the insurance
notes](/investing/front-range-insurance-hail-wildfire).

## Smaller things the calendar catches

- **Evaporative coolers** (common in Pueblo, the Springs and older Denver)
  need a spring start-up and a fall drain-and-cover; assign both.
- **Irrigation restrictions** — many Front Range utilities limit watering
  days in summer and ban it in drought years; put the utility's schedule in
  the lease so a brown lawn is not a habitability dispute.
- **Radon fans** run continuously and fail silently; check the manometer at
  every inspection.
- **Wind.** Chinook events take fences and shingles in January and February
  along the foothills; Boulder County and the west metro see 80–100 mph
  gusts most winters.
- **Water heaters** in Colorado's hard water last 8–12 years; flush annually.

## The one rule

Put a name next to every seasonal task in the lease or the management
agreement — tenant, manager, contractor — with a date. The Front Range does
not forgive an unassigned sprinkler system, and the city does not care
whose turn it was to shovel.
