Drive north-east out of Denver on I-76 or Peña Boulevard and the city thins
into the airport's hinterland: the Suncor refinery and old Commerce City,
then Reunion and Buffalo Run, then Brighton's subdivisions against the
farmland. This is where the metro's new-construction price floor is, where
its tenant base works at DIA, and where two disclosures do most of the
underwriting.

## The tenant base

Denver International Airport employs about 40,000 people on site — airlines,
ground handling, TSA, concessions — and a logistics belt along E-470 and
I-76 employs tens of thousands more in warehousing. Oil and gas field offices
sit in Brighton and Fort Lupton. These households want a 2005-or-newer
three- or four-bed with a two-car garage within 25 minutes of the terminal,
and they rent them at $2,600–2,950. Turnover is moderate; shift work makes
the garage and the quiet street matter more than the school.

## The two disclosures

**The metro district.** Every house in Reunion, Buffalo Run, Brighton
Crossing and GVR sits in one or more metropolitan districts repaying
infrastructure bonds through a debt-service levy. Total levies of 130–170
mills are normal; Denver's is in the high 70s. On a $500,000 house that is
$4,000–5,300 a year in tax, or $330–440 a month. It is the largest single
difference between this belt and the rest of the metro and it is in the
title commitment, not the listing. Pull the service plan: authorised debt,
maximum mills, term. See [the property-tax guide](/investing/property-tax-for-colorado-investors).

**27J.** School District 27J — Brighton, Commerce City north of roughly
96th, eastern Thornton — has run a four-day, Tuesday–Friday week since
2018. It is a known quantity and some families prefer it; others will not
rent in the district. Four-bedroom houses rent better than three-bedrooms
here partly because of the households who have organised around it.

## What is specific to the belt

- **Builder inventory.** Reunion, Brighton Crossing and the new phases are
  still delivering in 2026; builders are carrying standing inventory with
  incentives, which competes with your resale and your re-let. See [new-build
  versus resale](/investing/new-build-vs-resale-front-range).
- **Environmental.** Old Commerce City has the Suncor refinery, the Rocky
  Mountain Arsenal's legacy (now a wildlife refuge), and rail yards; read the
  disclosures and the CDPHE records on anything south of 80th.
- **Wind and hail.** The north-east is the metro's most exposed plain;
  Class 4 roofs earn a real premium discount here.
- **Water.** South Adams County Water and the Brighton system; tap fees
  on new construction and irrigation restrictions in dry years.
- **No rental licence** in Commerce City or Brighton; GVR and Montbello
  need Denver's. STRs restricted in all three; see [the north metro
  rules](/investing/north-metro-rental-rules) and [Denver's](/investing/denver-rental-rules).

## Where we would look first

A 2005–2012 four-bed in Reunion or Brighton Crossing bought from a builder's
standing inventory or a 2021–22 buyer who overpaid, with the metro
district's service plan read and the levy at its authorised maximum already.
Old-town Brighton's pre-1980 houses for the buyer who wants the belt's
tenant base without the district debt.
