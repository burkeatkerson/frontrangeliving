Denver built small multifamily housing for forty years and then zoned it
out for seventy. Almost every duplex, triplex and fourplex in the city sits
in the ring that was developed between the 1890s streetcar boom and the
Depression, and the 2010 zoning code confirmed most of it as two-unit or
row-house districts. This is the stock that [house-hackers](/investing/house-hacking-denver-duplex-math)
and small investors compete for, and it has three very different kinds of
problem.

## The three problems

**The conversion.** Thousands of Capitol Hill and Whittier houses were cut
into units in the 1940s–70s without permits. The zoning may allow two units
and the house may have three; the assessor may call it single-family and it
may have two kitchens. An illegal unit cannot be counted for rent on an
appraisal, cannot be licensed under Denver's rental ordinance, and can be
ordered removed. Check zoning, the assessor's property type, and the permit
record; if they disagree, price the property as what the record says it is.

**The systems.** Pre-1930 brick is structurally excellent and mechanically
original. Expect to find some of: a clay or Orangeburg sewer line with
offsets ($8,000–18,000 in Denver with the right-of-way work), a Federal
Pacific or Zinsco panel, knob-and-tube in the walls, galvanised supply,
single-pane windows, and a boiler. Denver's rental-licence inspection will
flag the panel and any missing GFCI or egress. See [the inspection ground
notes](/investing/what-inspectors-find-front-range).

**The fee-simple half.** Since about 2012 most "duplexes" built in Jefferson
Park, Sunnyside, Berkeley and Sloan Lake are two separate parcels sharing a
party wall. You buy one half, and it is a townhome with a neighbour, not an
income property with two rents. Excellent houses; not this asset class.

## Where we would look first

Whittier and Cole between Downing and York, for the densest legal duplex
fabric and the best price per unit in the pre-war ring. Harvey Park's
side-by-sides for the post-war version with garages and separate meters.
Baker for the buyer who wants the rent premium of walkability and can
handle a historic-district review on the exterior.

## Where we would be careful

Capitol Hill triplexes and larger, where Denver County's 8.3% apartment
vacancy in 2026 is felt first and the unit count is most often wrong on the
listing. Anything on the new-build side of Highland sold as a "duplex" at a
per-half price.
