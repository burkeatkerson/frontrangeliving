Take I-25 south from Colorado Springs to the Academy Boulevard exit and turn
east. The 1960s ranches of Security give way to the 1970s–80s split-levels of
Widefield, then the 1990s–2020s subdivisions of Fountain — Countryside,
Cross Creek, Mesa Ridge, Lorson Ranch — against the creek. Fort Carson's
Gate 20 is the reason all of it exists, and an E-5 with a family and $2,358
a month in housing allowance is the tenant all of it is priced for.

## The market

Fort Carson is home to the 4th Infantry Division and about 25,000 soldiers,
plus contractors and civilian employees. Roughly half the post lives off
base, and the closest off-base housing is here. The result is a rental
market with three unusual properties: demand is steady and federally
funded, the rent ceiling is published every January, and turnover happens
on a two-to-three-year PCS cycle regardless of how good the house is.

| Cross Creek, Mesa Ridge, Heritage | 1995–2015 | $380–440K | $2,200–2,400 | Two-car garages; D8; HOAs modest; some metro districts |
| Lorson Ranch (Fountain / unincorporated) | 2005–2026 | $400–480K | $2,300–2,550 | Newest; metro district 40–70 mills; builder inventory in 2026 |

Gross yields of 6.5–7% on the 1970s–2000s stock. Mill levies: 60–75 in the
older areas, 110–140 in Lorson Ranch and the newer metro districts. See [the
property-tax guide](/investing/property-tax-for-colorado-investors).

## The numbers

[The military rental worksheet](/investing/rent-vs-carry-colorado-springs-military-rental)
runs a $400,000 house here at a gap of about −$750 a month at 25% down
with a manager, closing in four to five years at BAH growth rates. Self-
managed with a local handyman, the gap is about −$650.

## What the tenant wants

In order: a two-car garage, a fenced yard, a gate within ten minutes, a
school in D3 or D8 that their neighbour on post recommended, and central air.
Houses with all five rent in a week at the top of the range. Houses with an
evaporative cooler and a carport rent in a month at the bottom. The $15,000
to add central air to a 1970s split-level pays back in rent and in days on
market.

## What is specific to the corridor

- **The SCRA.** Orders end leases on 30 days' notice. Underwrite an extra
  turnover every three years.
- **Single employer.** The corridor's economy is the post. A realignment is
  unlikely; a shutdown delays BAH. Hold four months of reserve.
- **PFAS.** Aqueous film-forming foam from Peterson Space Force Base
  contaminated the Widefield aquifer; the contamination was identified in
  2016, the water districts switched sources and installed treatment, and
  tap water has met federal standards since 2018. The Air Force funds
  long-term treatment. It is a disclosure on every sale, and some tenants
  ask; it has not measurably moved rents. Private wells are a different
  matter — avoid them.
- **Fountain Creek.** The 2013 and 2015 floods re-drew the floodplain;
  check the FEMA map on anything near the creek or the Jimmy Camp Creek
  confluence.
- **Hail.** The corridor is on the plain. Class 4 roofs and a known wind/hail
  deductible.
- **No rental licence**, city or county. STRs: Fountain licenses with owner-
  occupancy rules; El Paso County permits them in unincorporated areas with
  conditions. Nobody buys here for nightly income.

## Where we would look first

A 1978–1995 three-bed with a two-car garage and central air in Widefield or
Cross Creek, under $400,000, with a tenant already on a lease that runs past
next summer's PCS season. Skip Lorson Ranch until you have read the metro
district's service plan.
