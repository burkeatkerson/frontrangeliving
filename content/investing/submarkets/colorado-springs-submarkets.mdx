Colorado Springs is 195 square miles, half a million people, five military
installations, three school districts that matter, and a single-family median
of $499,900 in June 2026. It is not one market. Read it as four, by quadrant,
each with its own tenant.

## North: D20 and the family tenant

Academy School District 20 is the region's most sought-after, and the
1980s–2010s subdivisions inside it rent to families who chose the district
before they chose the house.

| Area | Stock | 3–4 bed, 2026 | Rent | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Briargate](/neighborhoods/colorado-springs/briargate) | 1985–2010 | $500–620K | $2,500–2,900 | The D20 heartland; HOAs modest; yields near 5.8% |
| [Rockrimmon](/neighborhoods/colorado-springs/rockrimmon) | 1970s–90s on the bluffs | $520–650K | $2,600–3,000 | Views, trees, older; D20 |
| [Peregrine](/neighborhoods/colorado-springs/peregrine), [Mountain Shadows](/neighborhoods/colorado-springs/mountain-shadows) | 1980s–2010s foothills | $600–800K | $2,900–3,400 | Waldo Canyon (2012) rebuild; wildfire insurance is the underwriting line |
| Northgate, Flying Horse, Wolf Ranch | 2005–2026 | $550–750K | $2,800–3,300 | Metro districts; builder inventory in 2026; Air Force Academy adjacency |

Gross yields of 5.5–6%. Long-term only — all single-family zones, no
non-owner STRs. [Monument](/places/monument) and [Palmer Lake](/places/palmer-lake)
continue the D38 version north.

## West: the only STR-viable blocks

[Old Colorado City](/neighborhoods/colorado-springs/old-colorado-city) (1880s–
1920s cottages, the Colorado Avenue strip), [Pleasant Valley](/neighborhoods/colorado-springs/pleasant-valley),
and the blocks toward [Manitou Springs](/places/manitou-springs) and Garden
of the Gods. $420,000–600,000, renting $2,300–2,800 long-term. This is
where the city's non-owner-occupied STR permits cluster — the zoning allows
them on some corridors and the 500-foot separation has largely been claimed.
Buying an existing permitted STR here is possible; creating a new one is a
map exercise. See [the Springs STR page](/investing/short-term-rental-colorado-springs).
Appreciation has been strong; the stock is old and the lots are small.

## South and southeast: BAH and yield

[The Broadmoor](/neighborhoods/colorado-springs/broadmoor),
[Broadmoor Bluffs](/neighborhoods/colorado-springs/broadmoor-bluffs),
[Cheyenne Cañon](/neighborhoods/colorado-springs/cheyenne-canon) and
[Skyway](/neighborhoods/colorado-springs/skyway) are the D12 premium
southwest — $700,000 and up, yields under 5%, not a rental market.

East of Nevada Avenue and south of Platte the city becomes the metro's
military rental market:

| Area | Stock | 3-bed, 2026 | Rent | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Southeast (Academy–Hancock–Circle) | 1960s–80s ranches | $330–400K | $2,000–2,250 | Cheapest in the city; D2/D11; vacancy higher; block by block |
| Stetson Hills, Powers corridor | 1990s–2010s | $420–520K | $2,300–2,600 | Peterson/Schriever tenants; D49; metro districts post-2005 |
| [Security-Widefield](/places/security-widefield), [Fountain](/places/fountain) | 1960s–2010s | $340–430K | $2,100–2,350 | Fort Carson's Gate 20; E-5 with dependents BAH $2,358; see [the Fountain brief](/investing/fountain-security-widefield) |
| Lorson Ranch, Mesa Ridge (Fountain) | 2005–2026 | $400–480K | $2,300–2,550 | New-build; metro districts; builder inventory |

Gross yields of 6.5–7%. The [military rental worksheet](/investing/rent-vs-carry-colorado-springs-military-rental)
runs the numbers on a $400,000 house here.

## What is specific to the Springs

- **Five installations:** Fort Carson (south), Peterson and Schriever Space
  Force Bases (east), the Air Force Academy (north), Cheyenne Mountain
  (southwest). About 40,000 active duty plus a large retired and contractor
  population. BAH resets each January.
- **No long-term rental licence.** STRs permitted; see the rules page.
- **Hail and wildfire both.** The north and west foothills are WUI; the
  east is hail plain. Insurance quotes vary by $1,500 a year across the city.
- **Mill levies are the lowest of any Front Range city** — 60–70 in older
  D11 areas — which is worth half a point of yield against Denver.
- **Water.** Colorado Springs Utilities' tap fees and the city's ban on
  non-functional turf in new development; irrigation restrictions in
  drought years.

## Where we would look first

Patty Jewett and Shooks Run for the balanced central buy; a Stetson Hills
four-bed for the Peterson/Schriever family tenant at a yield near 6.5%;
Security-Widefield for the Fort Carson BAH play. The north for the buyer
who wants D20 appreciation and will accept a 5.5% yield for it.
