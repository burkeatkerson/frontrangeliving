Boulder County is the Front Range's appreciation story — the city of
Boulder's open-space ring, purchased from 1967 onward, made it the first
Front Range city to run out of land — and the numbers on a rental here
reflect that. Almost nothing in the county carries. Almost everything in
the county has doubled twice since 2000. The question is which kind of buyer
you are, and within the county, Longmont is the answer for the one who
wants a rental rather than a land bank.

## Boulder: the land

110,000 people, CU's 37,000 students, the county's jobs, and a median for a
detached house that crossed $1 million in 2021 and has stayed there.

| The Hill, Goss-Grove | 1900s–60s near CU | $1.2–1.6M (5-bed) | $5,800–7,000 by the room | 5% | Student houses; see [the student guide](/investing/student-rentals-front-range) |
| [Table Mesa](/neighborhoods/boulder/table-mesa), Martin Acres | 1950s–70s ranches | $950K–1.2M | $3,800–4,500 | 4.6% | South Boulder; NCAR, NIST commuters; SmartRegs work on every one |
| [North Boulder](/neighborhoods/boulder/north-boulder) | 1950s–2010s mixed | $900K–1.4M | $3,600–4,800 | 4.5% | Holiday neighborhood; the 2000s infill |
| [Gunbarrel](/neighborhoods/boulder/gunbarrel) | 1970s–2000s condos and townhomes | $420–650K | $2,300–3,000 | 5.5% | The county's condo stock; HOA insurance problem applies; unincorporated in parts |

Every long-term rental needs a city licence, a safety inspection every four
years, and one-time SmartRegs energy compliance ($5,000–20,000 on a 1960s
house). STRs: principal residence only. See [Boulder County's rules](/investing/boulder-county-rental-rules).
Mill levies around 90–100.

A $1.1 million Table Mesa ranch at 25% down and 7.25% carries at about
$6,400 a month against $4,200 in rent. Nobody buys that for the rent.

## Longmont: the yield

100,000 people, the county's second city and its only one with a median
near the metro's. Employment is real and diversified — Seagate, the
aerospace and photonics cluster, UCHealth Longs Peak, Smucker's, the
county's northern offices — and the housing is 1880s downtown, 1950s–70s
ranches, and 1990s–2010s subdivisions at $520,000–600,000.

| Southwest and Clover Basin | 1990s–2010s | $560–680K | $2,900–3,200 | 5.8% | St. Vrain Valley schools; HOAs; close to Boulder commute |
| Northeast Longmont | 1990s–2020s | $520–620K | $2,700–3,000 | 6.0% | Newest; some metro districts; Union Reservoir |

No long-term licence. STR licences are available **only to Longmont
residents**, so for an investor Longmont is long-term. Mill levies around
85–95. [The Longmont-or-Pueblo comparison](/investing/longmont-vs-pueblo)
puts it against the other end of the yield spectrum.

All four license STRs with primary-residence rules; none licenses long-term
rentals. Gross yields of 5–5.5% and the county's strongest family-tenant
demand, because of the schools and the Boulder commute.

## The Marshall Fire and insurance

The December 30, 2021 fire destroyed more than 1,000 homes in Louisville,
Superior and unincorporated Boulder County in a grass-fire driven by
100-mph winds across suburban subdivisions, not forest. The rebuild is
largely complete; those blocks now carry 2023–26 construction built to the
county's new wildfire and energy codes, and the highest homeowners
premiums in the county. For a landlord the lessons are two: insurance
availability and the wind/hail/wildfire deductible are underwriting lines
on the western side of every Boulder County city, and a rebuilt house's
insurance is cheaper than the 1978 house next to it. See [the insurance
ground notes](/investing/front-range-insurance-hail-wildfire).

## What is specific to the county

- **Boulder's licence and SmartRegs** — see the rules page.
- **No investor STRs anywhere.** Boulder County pro formas are long-term.
- **Boulder Valley and St. Vrain Valley schools** both draw family tenants;
  the line between them (roughly Highway 287 and the Lafayette–Erie
  boundary) matters to rents.
- **The open-space ring** is why Boulder appreciates and why Longmont,
  Lafayette and Erie absorbed the growth.
- **Oil and gas** in Erie's Weld County half and east Longmont; check the
  ECMC map.
- **Water.** Every city here owns its system; tap fees on new construction
  are among the highest on the Front Range.

## Where we would look first

A 1960s–70s Longmont ranch under $560,000 for the balanced buy — the only
Boulder County purchase that reaches break-even in under a decade. Lafayette
for the buyer who wants Boulder Valley schools at the lowest entry. Boulder
itself only with capital that is buying land and is content to be paid in
appreciation.
