Greeley is the northern Front Range's working city — 110,000 people, the
University of Northern Colorado, the JBS beef plant, two hospitals, and
the field offices of the oil and gas companies that drill the Wattenberg —
and the only city in the corridor where a three-bed house still sells for
under $400,000 on a normal block. The Weld County median was $499,000 in
June 2026; Greeley and [Evans](/places/evans) sit well under it.

## The tenant base

Unusually diversified for a city its size, which is the investment case:

- **UNC**: about 9,000 students, a quarter of them off campus in houses.
- **Healthcare**: Banner North Colorado Medical Center and UCHealth Greeley
  Hospital (opened 2019), together roughly 4,000 jobs.
- **JBS**: the beef plant employs about 4,500, largely immigrant households
  who rent two- and three-bed houses long-term and pay reliably.
- **Oil and gas**: Weld County produces most of Colorado's oil; the field
  and service workforce rents newer houses in west Greeley, Evans and
  Windsor, and leaves when the rig count falls.
- **Agriculture and food processing**: Leprino, the dairies, the feedlots.
- **Government and schools**: Weld County's seat; District 6 is the
  county's largest employer after JBS.

At 3.5% rent growth the gap closes in about four years, the fastest in
northern Colorado. Note the 8% vacancy: the oil cycle earns it.

## What is specific to Greeley

- **No rental licence.** Greeley's STR rules put whole-home non-primary
  STRs in commercial zones only; see [the northern Colorado rules](/investing/northern-colorado-rental-rules).
  This is a long-term market.
- **Low mill levies.** D6 and Weld County levy less than the Denver metro's
  districts; 75–90 mills on resale housing, 110–130 in the west Greeley and
  Evans metro districts.
- **The smell.** The JBS plant and the feedlots are real on an east wind.
  Tenants in east and central Greeley know; tenants from elsewhere ask.
  West of 35th Avenue it is rarely an issue.
- **Oil and gas setbacks.** Active well pads sit inside the city; the
  state's 2,000-foot setback applies to new wells, not existing ones. Check
  the ECMC map for anything east of 47th Avenue or in Evans.
- **Water.** Greeley's own system is strong; tap fees on new construction
  are high. Evans is separate.
- **Hail and wind.** The plain. Class 4 roofs.
- **The 2013 flood** put parts of Evans and east Greeley under water; FEMA
  maps were redrawn. Check the parcel.

## Where we would look first

A 1970s–90s three-bed between 23rd and 47th Avenues, under $400,000, with a
garage and central air, for a family or hospital tenant. A UNC-block
four-bed for the buyer who wants 9% gross and will run it as a student house
on an August cycle. Evans for the cheapest entry with D6 schools, away from
the floodplain.
