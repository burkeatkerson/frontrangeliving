Pueblo is 110 miles south of Denver, 111,000 people, and the only Front
Range city where the June 2026 single-family median ($324,000) is under
half the metro's. The [rent-vs-carry worksheet](/investing/rent-vs-carry-pueblo)
shows what that does to a leveraged purchase. This brief is about *where* in
Pueblo, because the city's neighborhoods differ more than Denver's do and
the difference is mostly risk.

## The economy, briefly

Pueblo was a steel town (CF&I, now EVRAZ) and is now a healthcare,
government and education town with a steel mill. Parkview and St.
Mary-Corwin are the largest employers; the state mental health institute,
three prisons in the county, two school districts and CSU Pueblo follow.
Vestas builds wind-turbine towers; the Chemical Depot's demilitarisation
mission wound down in 2023 and its redevelopment is slow. Median household
income is roughly 70% of Colorado's. Rents track that: $1,650 for a
three-bed house, $1,200 for a two-bed apartment.

## The three disciplines

**Leverage.** Pueblo at 25% down and 7.25% is cash-negative. At 50% down it
carries; with cash it yields 5% net. Buy it with the equity that makes it
carry, or buy it somewhere else. [The leverage table](/investing/rent-vs-carry-pueblo)
shows the steps.

**Management.** A Pueblo-based manager who drives the property monthly,
knows the code enforcement officers, and screens for rental history over
credit score. The fee is 10%; the alternative is a Springs firm that finds
out about the weed citation when the lien shows up. See [Pueblo's rules](/investing/pueblo-rental-rules).

**Product.** A 1960s–80s three-bed ranch with a garage, central air or a
good swamp cooler, and a roof under ten years old, on the North Side or in
Belmont. Not a 1905 East Side house with a $160,000 price tag and a 10%
yield. Earn your way east with the second or third property and a manager
who has operated there.

## What is specific to Pueblo

- **Rents are soft.** Down about 1% over the year to July 2026 and more on
  apartments; 2023–25 deliveries on the north side are still leasing up.
  Underwrite flat rent for two years.
- **Resale is thin.** A few hundred houses a year above $300,000. Selling
  takes months, not days, and a price cut is common.
- **Code enforcement** is the city's main landlord regulation: weeds, trash,
  exterior condition, and the vacant building registry. It attaches to the
  owner.
- **Mill levies** around 95 in the city (D60) and similar in Pueblo West
  (D70 plus the district) — higher than Denver's, on lower values.
- **Water.** Pueblo Board of Water Works is a strong system; Pueblo West's
  district has its own restrictions and the older acre lots are on wells
  and septic, which a rental needs inspected.
- **Heat.** Pueblo is 1,000 feet lower and several degrees hotter than
  Denver; central air is a rent line, not a luxury.

## Where we would look first

A North Side or Belmont ranch under $340,000 with a lease in place, bought
with 40–50% down, managed by a Pueblo firm. [The Longmont-or-Pueblo
comparison](/investing/longmont-vs-pueblo) frames where it sits in a
portfolio: the income leg, not the only leg.
