Aurora is 160 square miles, 400,000 people, three counties and two school
districts, and describing it as one market is how buyers end up with the
wrong house. It is four markets. All four share a median ($515,000 in June
2026) a fifth below the Denver metro's and rents only about a tenth below,
which is the arithmetic that makes Aurora the metro's best balance of yield
and liquidity.

## 1. The Anschutz corridor: Fitzsimons, Havana, the R Line

The University of Colorado Anschutz Medical Campus — UCHealth University of
Colorado Hospital, Children's Colorado, the VA, the medical school — employs
about 20,000 people and runs 13-week travel contracts year round. The R
Line light rail runs from the campus south along I-225 to Nine Mile and the
Southeast line. Within two miles of the campus:

- **[Havana District](/neighborhoods/aurora/havana-district)**: 1960s–80s
  apartments and condos, 1950s–60s ranches west of Havana Street, the pan-
  Asian and Ethiopian commercial corridor. Two-bed condos $260,000–340,000,
  renting $1,800–2,000 unfurnished and $2,700–3,100 furnished mid-term. See
  [the mid-term rental guide](/investing/mid-term-rentals-hospital-corridors).
- **Fitzsimons-adjacent 1950s ranches** (Morris Heights, the blocks north of
  Colfax to Montview): $380,000–450,000, renting $2,300–2,600. Yields near
  7%. Condition varies block by block.
- **Del Mar Park and the 1960s–70s subdivisions** between Colfax and 6th:
  $400,000–480,000, renting $2,400–2,700.

Vacancy in the corridor's apartments is higher than the metro's (Aurora
delivered heavily in 2023–25), but houses and small condos near the campus
lease in days.

## 2. Original Aurora: the appreciation play

The 1920s–50s grid between Yosemite and Peoria, Colfax to 6th Avenue — the
old town, the Colfax streetcar suburb. Bungalows and small brick houses at
$380,000–480,000, renting $2,200–2,500. Aurora's cultural-arts district
investment, the Stanley Marketplace effect from the north, and the campus a
mile east have moved prices here faster than anywhere in the city. The
stock has Denver-ring problems (sewer, panel, windows) at lower prices.
The Colfax BRT project, under construction through 2027, runs its eastern
terminus here.

## 3. The 1970s–2000s subdivisions: yield and tenant depth

South of Mississippi and east of I-225 is the Aurora most tenants actually
rent: Seven Hills, Pheasant Run, Meadow Hills, Mission Viejo, Saddle Rock,
Tollgate, the Quincy Reservoir blocks. Three- and four-bed houses from 1975
to 2005 at $460,000–580,000, renting $2,600–3,000. Many are in Cherry Creek
Schools, which adds $150–250 a month of rent. Gross yields of 6.2–6.8%.
HOAs are modest where they exist; metro districts are rare before 2000.

This is where we would send a first-time Aurora investor.

## 4. The east: Southlands, Murphy Creek, the E-470 corridor

[Southlands](/neighborhoods/aurora/southlands) and the master-planned
communities east of E-470 are 2005–2026 construction, $520,000–680,000,
renting $3,000–3,400. Cherry Creek Schools. Metro districts on every parcel
adding 40–70 mills; HOAs with minimum-lease covenants. Yields of 5.8–6.2%
before the tax drag. Newer-house maintenance, faster tenant turnover, and
competition from builders' standing inventory in 2026 — see [new-build
versus resale](/investing/new-build-vs-resale-front-range).

## The numbers

A 1985 three-bed, two-bath in Seven Hills at $500,000, Cherry Creek
Schools, renting $2,800:

| | Monthly |
| --- | --- |
| P&I (25% down, 7.25%) | $2,558 |
| Tax (Arapahoe/Cherry Creek, ~100 mills on $450K net) | $255 |
| Insurance | $265 |
| Management, vacancy (9%, 5%) | $392 |
| Maintenance, reserve | $350 |
| Aurora rental licence | $10 |
| **Carry** | **$3,830** |
| **Rent** | **$2,800** |
| **Gap** | **−$1,030** |

At 3.5% rent growth the gap closes in about five years — a year sooner than
the Denver ranch, on a house that cost $50,000 less.

## What is specific to Aurora

- **The rental licence and the STR rule.** Licence with inspection; nightly
  rentals only in a primary residence and capped at 180 nights. See [Aurora's
  rules](/investing/aurora-rental-rules).
- **Three counties.** Adams (north of roughly 6th Avenue), Arapahoe (the
  bulk), Douglas (a sliver at the south). Mill levies and assessor
  timelines differ.
- **Buckley Space Force Base** in the east adds a BAH-paid tenant base;
  Denver-area BAH for an E-5 with dependents is above $2,600.
- **Colfax.** The Colfax BRT will change the north side's bus access by
  2027; the construction is a nuisance now.

## Where we would look first

The Seven Hills–Pheasant Run–Meadow Hills band for the first purchase; a
Havana-corridor condo for a furnished mid-term play; Original Aurora for
the buyer with a ten-year horizon who wants the campus's appreciation at a
Denver-ring discount.
