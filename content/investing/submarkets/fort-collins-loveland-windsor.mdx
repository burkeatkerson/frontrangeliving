The northern corridor is usually sold as one thing — "NoCo" — and it is
three. Fort Collins is the constrained, appreciating university city.
Loveland, ten miles south, is the older, cheaper, more open market that
investors pass on the way. Windsor, Timnath and Severance are what was
built in the fields between them after 2000.

## Fort Collins: appreciation

170,000 people, CSU's 33,000 students, a growth-management area that has
held the city's edges since the 1990s, and the Front Range's best downtown
outside Denver. The single-family figure CAR reported for June 2026 was
$650,000; the true city-wide median for a three-bed is nearer $600,000.

| Area | Stock | 3-bed, 2026 | Rent | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Old Town](/neighborhoods/fort-collins/old-town-fort-collins) and the historic grid | 1880s–1930s | $700K–1.1M | $3,200–3,900 | The one zone where non-primary STRs are mapped; yields under 5% |
| [Campus West](/neighborhoods/fort-collins/campus-west), Sheely, Avery Park | 1950s–70s ranches | $520–620K | $2,800–3,100 (or $3,800–4,200 by the room) | The student belt; see [the student guide](/investing/student-rentals-front-range) |
| [Midtown](/neighborhoods/fort-collins/midtown-fort-collins) (College Ave, Drake to Harmony) | 1960s–90s | $500–600K | $2,700–3,000 | UCHealth Poudre Valley; MAX bus rapid transit; the balanced Fort Collins buy |
| [Harmony corridor](/neighborhoods/fort-collins/harmony-corridor) | 1990s–2010s, townhomes | $480–650K | $2,600–3,200 | The employment corridor (tech, healthcare); fee-simple townhomes work here |
| Southeast (Fossil Creek, Timberline) | 1995–2020 | $580–720K | $3,000–3,400 | Metro districts in the newest phases; Poudre School District's south schools |

Gross yields of 5.5–6%. No long-term licence; STRs mapped by zone — see
[Fort Collins's rules](/investing/fort-collins-rental-rules). Larimer/PSD
mill levies around 85–95; higher in the southeast districts.

## Loveland: balance

80,000 people, the corridor's older manufacturing and arts town, UCHealth
Medical Center of the Rockies and the Centerra employment area on I-25,
Lake Loveland and the Big Thompson. Houses $100,000–150,000 under Fort
Collins for the same vintage.

| Area | Stock | 3-bed, 2026 | Rent | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Downtown Loveland](/neighborhoods/loveland/downtown-loveland) and the 1900s grid | Bungalows, Victorians | $450–600K | $2,400–2,800 | The Foundry redevelopment; the corridor's most open STR rule |
| 1950s–70s Loveland (Lake Loveland, Garfield, Taft) | Ranches | $460–540K | $2,400–2,650 | The balanced buy; Thompson School District |
| West Loveland (Namaqua, Mariana Butte) | 1980s–2010s | $560–700K | $2,800–3,200 | Foothills adjacency; wildfire insurance edge |
| Centerra, east of I-25 | 2005–2026 | $520–640K | $2,700–3,100 | Metro districts; the medical campus; builder inventory |

Gross yields around 6%. No long-term licence; STR licence through Planning
without a blanket primary-residence rule — see [the northern Colorado
rules](/investing/northern-colorado-rental-rules). Loveland is where we would
send a first-time northern Colorado investor.

## Windsor, Timnath, Severance: new-build

Between Fort Collins and Greeley along Highway 392 and the Poudre, three
towns that roughly doubled between 2010 and 2024. Almost everything is
2000–2026 and in a metro district.

| Town | Stock | 3–4 bed, 2026 | Rent | Total mills | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Windsor](/places/windsor) | 1995–2026; older core on Main St | $520–650K | $2,700–3,100 | 100–140 | Water Valley, Highpointe; Weld RE-4 schools; the 2008 tornado's rebuild zone is now the older part |
| [Timnath](/places/timnath) | 2005–2026 | $580–720K | $2,900–3,300 | 120–150 | Fort Collins commute; PSD schools; Costco and the Harmony Road corridor |
| [Severance](/places/severance) | 2010–2026 | $480–580K | $2,500–2,800 | 110–140 | The cheapest new-build in the corridor; Weld RE-4; farthest from employment |

Yields of 5.8–6.2% before the metro-district tax drag of $150–250 a month.
See [new-build versus resale](/investing/new-build-vs-resale-front-range)
and [the I-25 growth belt](/investing/i-25-north-growth-belt).

The Windsor house is newest and carries worst, because the metro district
takes back what the lower maintenance saves.

## What is specific to the corridor

- **Water.** Tap fees and raw-water dedication of $20,000–60,000 per new
  unit; older houses with existing taps carry a hidden premium. The
  Northern Integrated Supply Project and Thornton's pipeline are the
  decade's water politics.
- **CSU's calendar** moves Fort Collins rents in August.
- **Wildfire** on the west edges of Fort Collins and Loveland (Cameron Peak
  in 2020 burned to the edge of the foothills subdivisions); **hail** on
  the east.
- **I-25 construction** between Fort Collins and Johnstown through 2027.
- **No rental licence** anywhere in the three tiers.

## Where we would look first

A 1960s–70s Loveland ranch within a mile of Lake Loveland for the balanced
buy. A Midtown Fort Collins ranch near the MAX line for the appreciation
buy with a hospital-staff tenant. Windsor only from builder standing
inventory, with the service plan read.
