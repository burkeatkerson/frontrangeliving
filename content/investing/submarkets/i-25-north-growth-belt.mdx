Drive I-25 north from the Thornton line and for thirty miles every exit has
a town that was a few hundred people in 2000 and is ten thousand now:
Dacono, Frederick, Firestone, Mead, Berthoud, Johnstown, and east along
Highway 60 to Milliken. They grew because a four-bed house here cost what a
three-bed condo cost in Boulder County, and they still do. They are
investor-friendly on price, on regulation, and on base taxes, and they carry
one shared cost that the listing does not mention.

## The shared cost

Every subdivision built here since 2000 is in a metropolitan district, and
several are in two or three stacked districts. Weld County's and the towns'
own levies are low — 40–55 mills before schools — and the St. Vrain Valley
and Thompson school levies are moderate. The districts add 40–70. Total
levies of 110–150 mills on a $500,000 house are $3,400–4,700 a year,
against about $2,500 in Denver. That is $75–180 a month of the yield
advantage handed back. The [property-tax guide](/investing/property-tax-for-colorado-investors)
explains how to read a district's service plan; the short version is that
the 2005–2012 phases have amortised a third of their debt and the 2022–26
phases have amortised none.

## What is specific to the belt

- **The commute.** I-25 between Highway 7 and Highway 66 is the metro's
  worst northern bottleneck; the state's express-lane project through
  Berthoud finished in 2023 and the segment south of Highway 66 remains
  congested. Tenants price it.
- **Oil and gas.** The Wattenberg field. The 2017 Firestone explosion
  produced new flowline rules; active pads and the state's 2,000-foot
  setback for *new* wells apply. Check the parcel on the ECMC map; tenants
  ask.
- **Hail and wind.** The plain. Class 4 roofs; a written deductible.
- **Water.** Central Weld and Little Thompson water districts; tap fees of
  $30,000–50,000 per new unit, which is why the towns' metro districts are
  large and why resale houses with existing taps carry value.
- **Builder inventory.** Barefoot Lakes, Wyndham Hill's last phases,
  Johnstown's Thompson River Ranch and Milliken's newest subdivisions all
  had standing inventory with incentives in August 2026. See [new-build
  versus resale](/investing/new-build-vs-resale-front-range).
- **No rental licence** in any of the seven towns; STRs licensed with
  owner-occupancy rules in most. Long-term market.
- **Schools.** St. Vrain Valley (Frederick, Firestone, Dacono, Mead),
  Thompson (Berthoud, west Johnstown) and Weld RE-5J (Milliken, east
  Johnstown) — the boundary runs through Johnstown and matters to tenants.

## Where we would look first

A 2005–2012 four-bed in Frederick or Firestone, in a district whose debt is
partly amortised, bought from an owner rather than a builder, with a two-car
garage and a yard. Berthoud for the buyer who wants the belt's one real
downtown and Larimer County. Skip the 2024–26 phases unless the service plan
shows the levy already at its maximum and the builder's incentive covers two
years of the difference.
