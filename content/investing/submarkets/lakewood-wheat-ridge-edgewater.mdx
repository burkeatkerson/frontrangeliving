The west side is where Denver's post-war ring continues across the county
line, at a discount, with Jeffco's lower-drama regulation. Three cities,
three characters.

## Lakewood

Colorado's fifth-largest city, 1950s–70s almost entirely, with the W Line
running east–west through the middle and Belmar — the 2004 redevelopment of
Villa Italia mall — as a downtown it never had before.

| Area | Stock | 3-bed, 2026 | Rent | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Belmar](/neighborhoods/lakewood/belmar) and the Alameda corridor | 2000s townhomes and condos; 1950s ranches around them | $450–600K | $2,600–3,000 | Walkable; townhome HOAs $200–350; the only true mixed-use node on the west side |
| W Line stations (Lamar, Wadsworth, Garrison, Oak) | 1950s ranches within a half mile; new apartments at the stations | $480–580K | $2,750–3,000 | The 40 West arts district on Colfax; station premium real but modest |
| [Green Mountain](/neighborhoods/lakewood/green-mountain) | 1960s–80s split-levels and ranches on the slope | $560–680K | $3,000–3,300 | Trail access, views, Jeffco schools; the west side's Virginia Village |
| Applewood (shared with Wheat Ridge/Golden) | 1950s–70s on larger lots | $650–850K | $3,200–3,600 | Priced off Golden; yields under 5.5% |
| South Lakewood (Bear Creek, Kendrick Lake) | 1970s–80s | $520–620K | $2,850–3,100 | Quiet; Marston/Bear Valley tenant pool |
| Lakewood near St. Anthony (Union corridor) | 1970s condos and apartments | $280–380K (condos) | $1,800–2,100 | Mid-term furnished demand from the hospital |

Lakewood has no long-term rental licence. Its 2023 STR ordinance allows a
primary-residence STR plus one non-owner-occupied STR per licensee — the
most investor-tolerant rule of any large metro city — and the Belmar and
W Line blocks have enough nightly demand to make the second licence worth
something. See [the Jeffco rules page](/investing/jeffco-rental-rules).

Mill levies: 85–100 depending on fire district and whether the parcel is in
one of the older 1980s metro districts in the south.

## Wheat Ridge

Nine square miles of 1940s–60s ranches on unusually large lots — Wheat Ridge
was a greenhouse and orchard town and the lots show it — between Denver's
[Berkeley](/neighborhoods/denver/berkeley) and Arvada. $500,000–620,000 for
a three-bed on a 9,000–12,000 square foot lot, renting $2,800–3,000.

The investor case is the lot and the zoning. Wheat Ridge's R-2 and R-3
districts allow duplexes and small multi-units on lots that most single-
family owners never used that way, and the city's 2019-onward ADU and
mixed-use code along 38th and 44th Avenues is the friendliest in Jeffco.
The 38th Avenue corridor between Sheridan and Wadsworth has become the
west side's restaurant row, and the houses a block off it have re-priced
toward Berkeley.

Whole-home STR licences are capped at 2% per council district and the
eastern districts are full; partial-home is uncapped. No long-term licence.

## Edgewater

Less than a square mile, wedged between Sloan's Lake and Lakewood, 1920s–50s
bungalows and cottages on small lots. $520,000–680,000, renting $2,800–3,200.
It is [Sloan Lake](/neighborhoods/denver/sloan-lake) pricing minus 15% with
Denver's lake, Edgewater Public Market, and a 25th Avenue main street. No
long-term licence; STRs require primary residence. Yields under 5.5%; this
is an appreciation buy, and it has appreciated faster than any Jeffco city
over the last decade.

## What is specific to the west side

- **Jeffco schools** are a tenant draw across the whole area; Lakewood's
  Green Mountain and Bear Creek articulation areas and Wheat Ridge's
  Prospect Valley blocks rent faster with families.
- **The Federal Center** (6,000 federal employees) and **St. Anthony
  Hospital** are the employment anchors besides downtown.
- **Hail.** The west side takes it regularly; 2017's storm was the costliest
  in Colorado history and hit Lakewood and Wheat Ridge directly. Roof age
  and the insurer's deductible are underwriting lines, not afterthoughts.
- **Expansive soils** along the Green Mountain slope; see [the inspection
  notes](/investing/what-inspectors-find-front-range).

## Where we would look first

A 1950s–60s ranch within a half mile of a W Line station between Lamar and
Garrison, or a block off 38th Avenue in Wheat Ridge on an R-2 lot. Both
carry at a 6% gross yield, both have a value-add path (basement, ADU, or on
the Wheat Ridge lot a second unit), and both have the deepest resale demand
in Jeffco.
