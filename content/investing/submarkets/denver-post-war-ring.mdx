Between 1950 and 1970 Denver annexed and built the ring that now holds most
of its rental houses: brick ranches and split-levels on 6,000–9,000 square
foot lots, three bedrooms and a bath up, a basement that may or may not be
finished, a one-car garage, a silver maple in the front yard. This is the
house in [the brick-ranch worksheet](/investing/rent-vs-carry-denver-brick-ranch).
The worksheet treats the ring as one thing; it is not.

The southeast ring carries about half a point less yield than the southwest
and has appreciated slightly faster, because of Cherry Creek, the light-rail
stations on the Southeast line, and the DTC commute.

## North and east: the rest of the ring

[Montbello](/neighborhoods/denver/montbello) and [Gateway–Green Valley
Ranch](/neighborhoods/denver/gateway-green-valley-ranch) are 1970s–2000s, not
post-war, and are covered in spirit by [the 27J belt brief](/investing/commerce-city-brighton-27j):
newer houses, metro districts in GVR, DIA commuters, gross yields around
6.5%. [Regis](/neighborhoods/denver/regis) and [Chaffee Park](/neighborhoods/denver/chaffee-park)
in the northwest are small 1940s–50s pockets priced off Berkeley and carry
yields under 5.5%.

## The six things a 1958 house is hiding

1. **The sewer line.** Clay, sometimes Orangeburg, often with roots from
   that silver maple. Scope it; a replacement in Denver is $8,000–18,000.
2. **The panel.** Federal Pacific Stab-Lok and Zinsco panels are common in
   1960s–70s houses; many insurers will not bind until replaced ($2,500–4,000).
3. **The furnace and water heater.** Original 1990s replacements are now at
   end of life. $6,000–9,000 for a furnace; Denver's electrification rules
   apply when you replace with gas versus a heat pump.
4. **The roof.** Age matters less than the last hail event. Ask for the
   insurance claim history (a CLUE report) and check whether the roof is
   Class 4 impact-rated, which earns a premium discount.
5. **The basement.** Expansive soils and 1950s drainage mean most have taken
   water. Look for efflorescence, a sump, and grading that slopes toward
   the house.
6. **The windows.** Single-pane aluminium sliders. $12,000–20,000 to
   replace; tenants notice the draft more than the look.

Full detail in [the inspection ground notes](/investing/what-inspectors-find-front-range).

## The value-add that works

The unfinished or badly finished basement. A conforming egress window
($3,500–5,000), a bathroom, and a proper bedroom turn a three-bed/one-bath
at $2,950 into a four-bed/two-bath at $3,400–3,500 for $35,000–55,000. That
is a 12–15% return on the improvement and it lifts the resale comp set.
The detached ADU, by contrast, [rarely pencils](/investing/adu-math-colorado).

## Where we would look first

Harvey Park and Virginia Village, for the two best combinations of yield,
resale depth and tenant demand in the ring. Athmar Park and Ruby Hill for
the buyer who wants the southwest yield with the shortest commute to
downtown. Anything in the ring with a garage, a finished basement, and a
sewer line already replaced is worth paying $20,000 more for.
