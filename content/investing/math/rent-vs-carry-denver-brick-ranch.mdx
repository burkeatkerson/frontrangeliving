This is the rental out-of-state buyers picture when they picture Denver: three
bedrooms, one bath up, a partly finished basement, a one-car garage, a 7,000
square foot lot, and brick that has not needed anything in seventy years. They
sell for $500,000 to $620,000 across [Harvey
Park](/neighborhoods/denver/harvey-park), [Virginia
Village](/neighborhoods/denver/virginia-village), [Athmar
Park](/neighborhoods/denver/athmar-park), [Bear
Valley](/neighborhoods/denver/bear-valley), and [Mar
Lee](/neighborhoods/denver/mar-lee). We underwrite the middle of that range.

## The assumptions

- **Purchase price:** $550,000, under the seven-county metro single-family median of $650,000 (CAR, June 2026) because these are smaller houses.
- **Financing:** 25% down, $412,500 loan, 30-year fixed at 7.25% — roughly the investor rate in a week when conforming owner-occupied averaged 6.67%.
- **Rent:** $2,950, from leases signed this summer on comparable three-bed ranches, not from listing estimates.
- **Condition:** original kitchen, updated electrical panel, sewer line scoped clean. If the sewer line has not been scoped, stop and scope it.

## The monthly worksheet

| Line | Monthly |
| --- | --- |
| Principal and interest (7.25%, 30 yr) | $2,814 |
| Property tax (Denver, ~79 mills on $495K net of the 10% reduction, 6.8% rate) | $222 |
| Insurance (landlord policy, 1% wind/hail deductible) | $275 |
| Management (9% of rent) | $266 |
| Vacancy (5%) | $148 |
| Maintenance (8%, 1954 house) | $236 |
| Capital reserve | $200 |
| Denver rental licence, amortised | $10 |
| **Total carry** | **$4,171** |
| **Rent** | **$2,950** |
| **Monthly gap** | **−$1,221** |

Strip out management and self-manage and the gap falls to about −$955. Put
35% down instead of 25% and it falls to about −$830. The headline figure we
quote, −$1,040, is the middle of those cases: a lightly involved owner with a
manager who charges 8% and no leasing fee in year one.

## What the gap buys

Three things, and you should want all three before you accept the loss.

**Principal paydown.** About $340 a month in year one on this loan, rising
every year. The true economic loss is closer to $700 than $1,040.

**Rent growth against a fixed payment.** Denver single-family rents were
flat to slightly down in 2025–26 after the apartment glut. The long-run figure
is closer to 3–4% a year. At 3%, rent passes the unlevered carry around year
six; at 4%, year five.

**Appreciation.** The seven-county metro median has roughly doubled since
2013. Nobody should underwrite a repeat, but 3% a year on $550,000 is $16,500
in year one, which is more than the annual cash loss.

## What would make us walk

- A sewer line that scopes with offsets or bellies: $8,000–18,000 in Denver
  because the line crosses the city's right-of-way and needs a licensed
  contractor and a street cut.
- A roof older than the last big hail year for that part of town; insurers
  will write it with an actual-cash-value roof endorsement that leaves you
  paying most of the replacement.
- A basement bedroom without an egress window. It cannot be counted as a
  bedroom for rent or for the rental-licence inspection.
- Anything in the [two-to-four-unit belt](/investing/denver-two-to-four-unit-belt)
  priced as if it were a house; that stock is underwritten differently.

## Where this math gets better

Same house, same block, but a finished basement with a conforming bedroom and
second bath, rented as a whole: $3,400. Or, under Denver's post-HB24-1152 ADU
rules, the garage site holding a 600-square-foot detached unit at $1,700 —
see [the ADU guide](/investing/adu-math-colorado) for why that rarely pencils
at today's build costs, and the one case where it does.
