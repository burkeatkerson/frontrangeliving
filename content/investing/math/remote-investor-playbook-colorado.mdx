A decade ago Colorado was a simple state to be a landlord in: no rent control,
fast evictions, minimal statute. The first two are still true. The third is
not. Between 2021 and 2026 the General Assembly passed late-fee caps,
habitability expansions, source-of-income protection, for-cause eviction,
lease-term restrictions, deposit limits, and fee-disclosure rules, most with
per-violation penalties and attorney-fee shifting. None of it makes Colorado
a bad place to own a rental. All of it makes it a bad place to own one
casually from Phoenix.

## The five things to have in place

### 1. A licensed manager, chosen for the submarket

Colorado requires anyone managing property for others to hold a real estate
broker's licence, so the field is regulated. Pick by submarket, not by
brand: a Denver firm will take a Pueblo house and visit it twice a year. Ask
three questions — what did you *sign* last month on a comparable unit, what
is your average days-on-market for a house in this zip, and how many
evictions did you file in 2025 — and check the answers against each other.
Fees: 8–10% of collected rent, a half- to full-month leasing fee, and a
markup on maintenance that you should cap in the agreement at 10%.

### 2. A lease written for 2026

The lease your manager used in 2023 is non-compliant. It needs: the total
monthly cost disclosed up front (base rent plus every mandatory fee), a late
fee no greater than $50 or 5% of rent and not charged until day eight, a
security deposit of no more than one month's rent with an installment
option, no waiver of the tenant's right to a jury or to attorney's fees, no
penalty for a tenant calling code enforcement, and the statutory
habitability language. The [statewide rules page](/investing/colorado-landlord-law-2026)
lists every provision. Use the Colorado Apartment Association's or your
manager's 2026 form, not a national template.

### 3. The city licence

- **Denver:** residential rental licence, third-party inspection, four-year
  term, non-transferable on sale.
- **Boulder:** rental licence, inspection every four years, SmartRegs
  one-time.
- **Aurora:** residential rental licence with periodic inspection.
- **Everyone else on the Front Range:** no long-term licence as of August
  2026, though Fort Collins and Lakewood have studied one. STRs are licensed
  almost everywhere.

See the city pages under [the rules, city by city](/investing).

### 4. Insurance you have actually read

Colorado's average premium reached about $6,630 in 2026, fourth highest in
the country, and hail is roughly half of it on the Front Range. A landlord
(DP-3) policy with loss-of-rent coverage, liability of at least $500,000, and
a wind/hail deductible you have written down. Add a $1 million umbrella; it
costs $300–500 a year. The [insurance ground notes](/investing/front-range-insurance-hail-wildfire)
go deeper.

### 5. The reserve

Four months of full carry, plus the hail deductible. The Front Range's big
capital hits — roof, sewer line, furnace in January — do not wait for your
cash flow to catch up, and a contested eviction in Denver is two to four
months of no rent.

## The calendar

| When | What |
| --- | --- |
| January | BAH resets (Springs); new statutes take effect; property tax bills issue. Review rent against new comps. |
| February–March | Lease renewals go out for May–August expirations; 60 days' notice of any rent increase is the safe practice. |
| April 30 / June 15 | Property tax due (full, or second half). |
| May–August | Leasing season. 70% of Front Range single-family turnover happens here; a house that is vacant in October waits. |
| May–September | Hail season. Your manager should photograph the roof after every storm over one inch and file within the policy's window. |
| June 30 | Reappraisal valuation date in even years; protest window for new values runs May–June in odd years. |
| August | Student leases turn (CSU, CU, UNC, DU, UCCS). |
| October | Furnace service, sprinkler blow-out, gutter clean. Colorado law holds you to working heat. |
| November–December | Slowest leasing weeks; avoid expirations here. |

## Evictions, realistically

Colorado evictions are still faster than most blue states. Nonpayment: a
10-day demand, then filing (about $85–135), a hearing in 7–14 days, a 48-hour
appeal window, then the sheriff posts and executes within roughly a week.
Uncontested, 3–6 weeks door to door. Contested — and in Denver, where
tenants have a right to counsel, more are contested — 2–4 months. After
twelve months of tenancy HB24-1098 requires cause for non-renewal; the
permitted no-fault grounds (owner move-in, substantial renovation, sale,
withdrawal from the market) need 90 days' notice and, for renovation,
relocation assistance in some cities.

The practical response is what good managers already do: screen hard within
the law (source-of-income discrimination is prohibited; credit and income
standards applied evenly are not), and keep tenancies under twelve months
only when you have a real reason to.

## What would make us walk

A manager who says the 2024–26 laws "don't really change much." They
changed the lease, the deposit, the renewal, the fee schedule, and the
penalty for getting any of those wrong.
