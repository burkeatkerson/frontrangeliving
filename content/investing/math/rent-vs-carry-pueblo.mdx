Pueblo County's June 2026 single-family median was $324,000 — half the
Denver metro's — and rents are not half of Denver's. That arithmetic is the
entire investment case, and it is a real one. What it is not is a leveraged
cash-flow machine at today's rates, and the worksheet shows why.

## The assumptions

- **Purchase:** $300,000, a 1960s–80s three-bed ranch on the [North
  Side](/neighborhoods/pueblo/pueblo-north-side), in Belmont, or in [Pueblo
  West](/places/pueblo-west).
- **Financing:** 25% down, $225,000 loan at 7.25%.
- **Rent:** $1,650 from this summer's leases. The citywide all-types average
  is about $1,295; houses run well above it.
- **Condition:** evaporative cooler, older furnace, roof within ten years,
  sewer scoped.

## The monthly worksheet

| Line | Monthly |
| --- | --- |
| Principal and interest | $1,535 |
| Property tax (Pueblo, ~95 mills on $270K net, 6.8%) | $145 |
| Insurance | $190 |
| Management (10% — Pueblo managers charge more on smaller rents) | $165 |
| Vacancy (7%) | $116 |
| Maintenance (10%, older stock) | $165 |
| Capital reserve | $150 |
| **Total carry** | **$2,466** |
| **Rent** | **$1,650** |
| **Monthly gap** | **−$816** |

That is the fully loaded, third-party-managed figure. Take the reserve and
maintenance down to the minimum on a renovated house and self-manage — which
out-of-state owners should not do here — and the gap is nearer −$400. We quote
−$550 as the realistic middle.

## The leverage table

Because the yield is high and the price is low, the down payment moves the
answer faster here than anywhere else on the Front Range:

| Down payment | Loan | P&I | Monthly gap |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 25% | $225,000 | $1,535 | about −$550 |
| 40% | $180,000 | $1,228 | about −$240 |
| 50% | $150,000 | $1,023 | roughly break-even |
| Cash | — | — | about +$1,000 (≈5% net yield) |

A 50% down payment on a Pueblo house is $150,000. That is roughly the 25%
down payment on a Denver ranch, and it produces a property that carries
itself from day one. That is how we would buy Pueblo: as the low-leverage,
income leg of a portfolio that has its appreciation leg somewhere north.

## What is different about operating here

- **Managers.** Use one based in Pueblo. Colorado Springs managers will take
  the listing and drive down twice a year.
- **Tenant screening.** Pueblo's median household income is roughly 70% of
  the state's. Income-to-rent ratios of 3× are met by fewer applicants; 2.5×
  with a strong rental history is common practice.
- **Turnover costs.** Older stock, swamp coolers, and detached garages mean
  make-ready runs $2,000–4,000 rather than the $1,500 you would budget in a
  2005 house.
- **The East Side and [Bessemer](/neighborhoods/pueblo/bessemer).** Gross yields
  of 9–10% exist there, on $150,000–200,000 houses. So do 12% vacancy and
  collection problems. First-time Pueblo buyers should start on the North
  Side or in Pueblo West and earn their way east.

## What would make us walk

A seller's pro forma at $1,900 rent. Pueblo rents slipped roughly 1% over the
year to July 2026 and were down more than that on apartments; the market is
soft, not rising. Underwrite the lease in hand, and if there is no lease in
hand, underwrite $1,600.
