For twenty years the Front Range student-rental business was a zoning
business. Fort Collins's "U+2" rule capped a house at three unrelated adults;
Boulder's caps were three or four depending on the zone; Greeley and Golden
had their own versions. Investors bought four- and five-bedroom houses and
rented three bedrooms. On July 1, 2024, HB24-1007 made every one of those
rules unenforceable, and by that August the cities had repealed them. A
five-bedroom house near CSU is now a five-bedroom rental, legally, for the
first time.

## What the law left standing

Cities may still limit occupancy on demonstrated health and safety grounds:
International Building Code minimums (roughly 70 square feet for the first
occupant of a bedroom and 50 for each additional), egress windows in every
sleeping room, fire separation, and septic capacity outside sewer service.
In practice that caps a house at about one occupant per legal bedroom plus
one or two, which is what the market was doing anyway.

What is not coming back: any rule that counts whether tenants are related.

## Campus by campus

### CSU, Fort Collins (about 33,000 students)

The student belt runs west and south of campus: [Campus
West](/neighborhoods/fort-collins/campus-west) along Elizabeth and Plum,
the 1960s–70s ranches of Sheely and Avery Park, and the 1950s bungalows
between Laurel and Prospect east of Shields. A four-bed, two-bath ranch at
$560,000 rents by the bedroom at $900–1,100 per room for a total of
$3,800–4,200 on a 12-month lease with a parental guarantor. Gross yield
roughly 8% on rent, about 6% after the heavier wear and a summer vacancy
month. Fort Collins has no long-term rental licence. This is the best
combination of yield and resale liquidity on the list.

### CU Boulder (about 37,000 students)

The Hill, Goss-Grove, and the blocks of Martin Acres and
[Table Mesa](/neighborhoods/boulder/table-mesa) near the South Boulder bus
routes. A five-bed on the Hill is $1.2–1.6 million and rents at $5,800–7,000
by the bedroom; a four-bed in Martin Acres is $850,000–1 million at
$4,200–4,800. Gross yields of 4.5–5.5%. Every long-term rental needs a city
rental licence, a safety inspection every four years, and one-time SmartRegs
energy compliance — see [Boulder County's rules](/investing/boulder-county-rental-rules).
You buy Boulder student housing because it is Boulder land with a tenant on
it, not for the yield.

### UNC, Greeley (about 9,000 students)

The blocks between 8th and 20th Avenues south of the campus, and the 1970s
fourplexes along 20th Street. A four-bed 1910s house at $320,000 rents by
the bedroom at $600–750 for $2,500–2,900; gross yield 9–10%. The tenant pool
is thin, the summer vacancy is real, and Greeley's own 2020s apartment
deliveries near campus compete with you. Still the highest cash yield on this
page. See the [Greeley / Evans brief](/investing/greeley-evans).

### UCCS, Colorado Springs (about 11,000 students)

[Cragmor](/neighborhoods/colorado-springs/cragmor) and the Austin Bluffs
corridor north of campus; 1960s–80s ranches at $400,000–480,000 renting at
$2,600–3,200 by the room. Gross yields around 7%. UCCS is a growing,
commuter-heavy campus and the on-campus housing expansion has kept
off-campus rents soft.

### DU, Denver (about 13,000 students)

[University](/neighborhoods/denver/university) and
[University Park](/neighborhoods/denver/university-park) east of campus and the
blocks toward Evans and Downing. $800,000–1.1 million for a four-bed; rents
$4,400–5,200. Denver's residential rental licence applies. Gross yields
under 6%, bought for appreciation in a neighborhood where the land value is
most of the price.

### Colorado School of Mines, Golden (about 7,500 students)

Tiny market, tight supply, very high parental-guarantor quality. Houses in
[Downtown Golden](/neighborhoods/golden/downtown-golden) and on the 1950s
blocks south of campus at $750,000–950,000 rent at $4,200–5,000. Golden's
STR rules are strict (primary residence only); long-term rentals are
unlicensed. Yields under 6%, near-zero vacancy.

## How to operate one

- **Lease the house, not the rooms**, on one joint-and-several 12-month lease
  with a parental guaranty on each tenant. Individual room leases hand you
  five tenancies and five potential HB24-1098 cases.
- **August 1 to July 31** is the cycle at CSU, CU and UNC. A house that
  turns in October sits until May.
- **Security deposits** are capped at one month's rent under HB25-1249 from
  January 1, 2026, and normal wear and tear — which in a student house is
  considerable — cannot be deducted. Price the wear into the rent.
- **Furnish nothing.** Unfurnished student houses turn faster and the
  deposit law makes furniture damage hard to recover.

## What would make us walk

- A house with three legal bedrooms and two "bonus rooms" without egress. You
  can rent what the fire code says is a bedroom and nothing else, and the
  Boulder inspector will count.
- A Boulder house that has never had a SmartRegs inspection. Budget
  $5,000–20,000 of insulation and furnace work before you can licence it.
- Buying in Greeley or Cragmor for appreciation. They are yield markets.
