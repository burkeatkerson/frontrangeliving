Colorado's property tax is the line out-of-state investors get most wrong,
in both directions. Texans and Floridians assume it is high and over-reserve.
Californians assume it is low and fixed and get a $7,000 bill on a Parker
townhome. The truth is that it is low on average and has the widest
parcel-to-parcel variance of any state we work in, because of the mill levy
and the metro district.

## The 2026 formula

```
taxable value = (actual value − 10% of the first $700,000) × assessment rate
tax           = taxable value × mill levy ÷ 1,000
```

For tax year 2026 the residential assessment rate is **6.8%** for local
government levies (county, city, fire, library, metro district) and
**7.05%** for school district levies. The 10% reduction is capped at $70,000
of value. These are the figures set by the 2024 special session (HB24B-1001,
building on SB24-233); they were designed to hold revenue roughly flat and
are scheduled to stay near these levels, but the assembly has changed them
in four of the last five sessions, so check the Division of Property Taxation
page before you underwrite.

The *actual value* is what the county assessor says the house was worth on
June 30 of the prior even-numbered year. Colorado reappraises every odd year;
the 2025 reappraisal values, based on June 30, 2024 sales, govern 2025 and
2026 bills.

## The mill levy is the variable

| Place | Typical total mills, 2026 | Tax on a $500,000 house |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Colorado Springs, older D11 neighborhoods | 60–70 | $1,900–2,200 |
| Pueblo (city, D60) | 90–100 | $2,800–3,100 |
| Denver | 75–80 | $2,400–2,550 |
| Lakewood, Arvada, Wheat Ridge (Jeffco) | 85–100 | $2,700–3,100 |
| Boulder | 90–100 | $2,800–3,100 |
| Fort Collins | 85–95 | $2,700–2,900 |
| Thornton, Westminster, Northglenn (Adams 12 schools) | 100–120 | $3,200–3,700 |
| Aurora (Cherry Creek or APS schools) | 90–110 | $2,900–3,400 |
| Parker, Castle Rock, new-build with metro district | 120–160 | $3,800–5,000 |
| Commerce City Reunion, Brighton new-build with metro district | 130–170 | $4,100–5,300 |

Those are ranges; the specific parcel is on the county assessor's site with
its full levy breakdown, and you should pull it for every property before you
write an offer. Every county assessor on the Front Range publishes it.

## The metro district

Since the 1990s almost every new subdivision on the Front Range has been
built by a metropolitan district: the developer forms a special district,
the district sells bonds to pay for the streets, water, sewer and parks, and
the homeowners repay the bonds through a debt-service mill levy for 30 to 40
years. Statute caps the debt levy at 50 mills, but there is no cap on how
many overlapping districts a parcel can sit in, and the operations levy is
separate.

What it means for an investor:

- A $500,000 new build in a metro district can carry a tax bill $2,000–2,500
  a year higher than an identical-value resale two miles away. That is
  $170–210 a month out of your rent.
- The levy does not fall as the house appreciates; most districts are
  authorised to raise mills to hold debt service flat when values drop.
- Colorado requires a metro district disclosure in new-construction sales
  contracts and the district's debt is a public record at the Division of
  Local Government. Read the service plan: the authorised debt, the maximum
  mill levy, and the term.

The [new-build versus resale guide](/investing/new-build-vs-resale-front-range)
walks through the carry difference on a real pair.

## Three things that are not true

**"Investors pay commercial rates."** They do not. Colorado's residential
rate applies to any residential property regardless of who lives in it.
Proposals to classify short-term rentals as commercial (a 27–29% assessment
rate) have failed in every session since 2023, though they keep coming back.

**"Taxes are locked at purchase."** They are not. Colorado has no Prop 13.
The assessor revalues every two years from sales, and a 2027 reappraisal
will use June 30, 2026 values.

**"The seller's tax bill is what I will pay."** Usually close, because the
value does not reset on sale — but the seller may have a senior homestead
exemption (50% of the first $200,000 exempt), which disappears the day a
rental owner takes title.

## How to underwrite it

Pull the parcel on the assessor's site. Take the current actual value, apply
the formula above with the parcel's actual total levy, and add 5% a year for
the next reappraisal. If the parcel is in a metro district, pull the service
plan and check whether the debt levy is at its authorised maximum; if it is
not, assume it will be.
