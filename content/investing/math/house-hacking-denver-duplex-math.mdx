At a 7% investor rate with 25% down, a Denver rental loses money every month
— see [the brick ranch worksheet](/investing/rent-vs-carry-denver-brick-ranch).
The house-hack changes two variables at once: an owner-occupied loan at the
lower rate with a tiny down payment, and a tenant who pays for half the
building. It is the strategy we recommend most often to people under forty
who want to own a Denver rental and do not have $140,000.

## The assumptions

- **Purchase:** $750,000 for a side-by-side or up/down duplex in [Whittier](/neighborhoods/denver/whittier), [Cole](/neighborhoods/denver/cole), [Baker](/neighborhoods/denver/baker) or the Harvey Parks — two two-bed, one-bath units of roughly 900 square feet each.
- **Financing:** FHA, 3.5% down ($26,250), 30-year fixed at 6.75% (owner-occupied, slightly above the 6.67% conforming average because FHA pricing runs a touch higher), with upfront MIP financed and annual MIP of 0.55%.
- **Rent on the second unit:** $2,200 for a renovated two-bed in these neighborhoods; $1,900 for an original one.
- **You live in the other unit** for at least twelve months, which FHA requires.

## The monthly worksheet

| Line | Monthly |
| --- | --- |
| Principal and interest ($736,400 incl. upfront MIP, 6.75%) | $4,776 |
| Annual MIP (0.55%) | $337 |
| Property tax (Denver, ~79 mills on $680K net, 6.8%) | $305 |
| Insurance (two-unit landlord policy) | $320 |
| Maintenance and reserve on the rented unit | $250 |
| Vacancy on the rented unit (5%) | $110 |
| Denver rental licence (rented unit only), amortised | $10 |
| **Total carry** | **$6,108** |
| **Rent, second unit** | **$2,200** |
| **Your net housing cost** | **about $3,900** |

We quote "$3,400 to $3,900" because most people who do this self-manage and
skip the reserve line in year one, and because the tax deduction on the
rented half is real. Both are fair; neither should be counted on.

## What you are comparing it to

The unit you live in would rent for $2,200. So the house-hack costs you
$1,200–1,700 a month more than renting the same unit. For that premium you
get: principal paydown of about $700 a month in year one, the rented half's
depreciation, all the appreciation on a $750,000 asset with $26,000 in, and
the right to move out in year two and rent both sides at roughly $4,400 on a
loan you could never get as an investor.

That last point is the whole strategy. Do it twice in four years and you own
two Denver duplexes on owner-occupied financing.

## The rules that make this easier in Colorado

**HB24-1098.** Colorado's for-cause eviction law does not apply to
owner-occupied duplexes and triplexes while the owner lives there. You can
decline to renew a tenant in your own building without stating cause. The
moment you move out, that exemption ends and the [statewide
rules](/investing/colorado-landlord-law-2026) apply in full.

**Denver's rental licence** applies to the unit you rent, not the unit you
live in. One inspection, four-year term.

**Denver's STR rule** — primary residence only — actually helps here. You can
licence a short-term rental in the building you live in; investors who do not
live there cannot. See [Denver's rental rules](/investing/denver-rental-rules).

## Where the duplexes actually are

The [two-to-four-unit brief](/investing/denver-two-to-four-unit-belt) has the
block-level detail. In short:

| Area | Era and type | Typical 2026 price | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Capitol Hill, North Cap Hill, Cheesman | 1890–1930 brick, often converted | $700K–1.1M | Many are illegal conversions; check the zoning certificate |
| Whittier, Cole, Five Points, Clayton | 1890–1920 side-by-sides and up/downs | $650–900K | The densest duplex stock in the city |
| Baker, Lincoln Park | 1890–1910 | $700–950K | Walkable, strong rents, old sewer lines |
| Jefferson Park, Sunnyside, Berkeley | 1900–1930 plus 2010s new-build duplexes | $750K–1.4M | New-build halves sell as separate fee-simple townhomes |
| Barnum, Villa Park, Westwood | 1950s–70s side-by-sides | $550–700K | Cheapest entry; original condition |
| Harvey Park, Harvey Park South | 1950s–60s side-by-sides | $600–750K | Good bones, garages, yards |

## Three- and four-unit buildings

FHA's self-sufficiency test — 75% of total market rent must cover the whole
payment — kills most Denver fourplexes at 2026 prices. A $1.2 million
four-unit with $8,400 in total rent needs a payment under $6,300, and the
payment is about $9,500. The exception is a building you can buy under
$900,000, which now means Aurora, Englewood, or a rough block of East Colfax.

## What would make us walk

- An "up/down duplex" that the assessor lists as a single-family house. That
  is an unpermitted conversion; the city can make you remove the second
  kitchen, and FHA will not lend on it as a duplex.
- Shared utilities with no sub-metering. You will be paying the tenant's gas
  bill all winter, and Colorado law now restricts how utility costs can be
  passed through.
- Knob-and-tube, a Federal Pacific panel, or galvanised supply — all common
  in the 1900–1930 belt and all flagged on the rental-licence inspection.
