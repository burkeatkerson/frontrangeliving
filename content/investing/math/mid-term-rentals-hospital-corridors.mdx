Every Front Range city that has restricted short-term rentals drew the line
at the same place: fewer than 30 consecutive nights. A furnished unit leased
for 30, 60 or 90 days is a long-term rental everywhere from Fort Collins to
Pueblo. That regulatory gap is the mid-term rental business, and the Front
Range has an unusual amount of the demand that fills it.

## Who rents for 30 to 90 days here

**Travel nurses and allied health.** The big teaching and trauma campuses run
13-week contracts year round: the Anschutz Medical Campus in Aurora
(University of Colorado Hospital and Children's Colorado, roughly 20,000
employees), the Swedish–Craig–Porter cluster in Englewood, Saint Joseph and
Presbyterian/St. Luke's in Denver's Uptown, St. Anthony in Lakewood, UCHealth
Poudre Valley in Fort Collins, UCHealth Memorial in Colorado Springs, and
Parkview in Pueblo.

**Corporate relocations.** Thirty- to ninety-day furnished stays while a
family looks for a house, paid by the employer. Broomfield's corporate
campuses, the DTC, and downtown Fort Collins generate these steadily.

**Insurance displacement.** After a hail or fire loss, carriers pay
additional living expense for a furnished rental near the home. On the Front
Range this is a real and recurring source: the Marshall Fire put hundreds of
families into furnished housing for a year, and every summer hail season
does the same in smaller numbers.

**Military TDY and PCS gaps** in Colorado Springs, and visiting faculty at
CU, CSU and Mines.

## The worked case: a two-bed near Anschutz

A 2004 two-bed, two-bath condo in the [Havana
District](/neighborhoods/aurora/havana-district) side of Aurora, ten minutes
to the campus, $330,000.

| | Unfurnished, 12-month | Furnished, 30–90 day |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rent | $1,900 | $2,900 all-in |
| Utilities and internet paid by owner | $0 | $280 |
| Furnishing, amortised over 5 years ($15,000) | $0 | $250 |
| Cleaning and turnover (6 turns/yr at $180) | $0 | $90 |
| Vacancy | 5% ($95) | 12% ($348) |
| Management | 9% ($171) | 15% ($435) |
| HOA, tax, insurance | $640 | $660 |
| P&I (25% down, 7.25%) | $1,689 | $1,689 |
| **Net before debt** | **$994** | **$848** |
| **Net after debt** | **−$695** | **−$841** |

Read that again: with third-party management at 15%, the furnished version
nets *less*. The mid-term premium goes to the manager. Self-managed — which is
feasible for mid-term in a way it is not for nightly — the furnished unit
nets about $1,283 before debt, a 29% improvement on unfurnished, and the gap
after debt narrows to about −$400.

premiums there rarely clear the extra cost.

## The rules that still apply

- **Denver's residential rental licence** is required for any rental of 30
  days or more. Mid-term does not escape it.
- **Aurora's rental licence** applies likewise; see [Aurora's
  rules](/investing/aurora-rental-rules).
- **Lodger's tax** stops at 30 days in every Front Range city; at 29 you owe
  it and you are an unlicensed STR.
- **HB24-1098** attaches to a tenant after twelve months. Most operators cap
  any single tenant at about nine months for this reason.
- **Security deposits** under HB25-1249 are capped at one month's rent from
  January 1, 2026, and the furnished-damage exposure is higher. Furnished
  operators lean on a documented move-in inventory and the mandatory
  walk-through.

## What would make us walk

- A condo HOA with a six-month minimum lease term. Many post-2000 associations
  in Aurora and Centennial have exactly that.
- A pro forma that assumes 95% occupancy. Mid-term runs 85–90% for good
  operators and the gaps cluster in December and January.
- Any unit more than 20 minutes from the anchor hospital. Nurses coming off a
  twelve-hour shift do not drive 30.
