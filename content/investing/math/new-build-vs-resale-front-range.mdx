Every Front Range production builder is carrying standing inventory in
August 2026 — the 2025 delivery wave in the south metro and northern Colorado
coincided with the slowest resale market since 2014 — and the incentive
sheets have become generous: 2-1 rate buy-downs, permanent buy-downs to the
high 5s, $15,000–30,000 in closing credits, free appliance and blind
packages. Investors are being invited in. The question is what the
incentives are paying you to accept.

## The worked pair

Both in Parker, both four-bed, three-bath, two-car, about 2,200 square feet.

**A:** 2026 new build in a master-planned community south of Hess Road.
$560,000 list, $20,000 closing credit, builder lender offers a permanent
buy-down to 6.0% on a 25% down investor loan.

**B:** 1998 resale in a Stonegate-type 1990s subdivision, updated kitchen,
newer roof. $545,000, no incentives, 7.25% investor rate.

| | A: new build | B: 1998 resale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Effective price after credits | $540,000 | $545,000 |
| Loan (25% down) | $405,000 at 6.0% | $408,750 at 7.25% |
| Principal and interest | $2,428 | $2,789 |
| Property tax (A: 145 mills incl. metro district; B: 95 mills) | $476 | $319 |
| HOA | $95 | $65 |
| Insurance | $230 | $260 |
| Maintenance + reserve | $150 | $350 |
| Management, vacancy (5%, 8%) | $403 | $390 |
| **Total carry** | **$3,782** | **$4,173** |
| **Rent** | **$3,150** | **$3,000** |
| **Gap** | **−$632** | **−$1,173** |

The new build wins year one by about $540 a month. That is real. Now look at
what produced it: $360 of the $540 is the buy-down, which is the builder's
money and is already in the price; $100 is lower maintenance, which is
honest for five years and then is not; and the metro district took $157 back.

## What the metro district does over time

The Parker build sits in a district with an authorised 50-mill debt levy plus
operations, on bonds with 30 years to run. Over a ten-year hold that is
roughly $19,000 of additional tax against the resale, and it does not
decline. The resale's 1990s district is usually paid off or close to it —
check, because some 1990s Douglas County districts refinanced and extended.
[The property-tax guide](/investing/property-tax-for-colorado-investors)
covers how to read the service plan.

## What happens when you sell or re-let

The new build's tenant leaves in year three. You are now advertising a
three-year-old house two streets from the builder's model home, where the
builder is renting and selling the same floorplan with incentives. New
subdivisions also rent to households who are waiting to buy, and those
households leave faster.

The 1998 resale sits in a neighborhood with mature trees, no competing
builder, and a tenant pool that chose the school and the location rather
than the granite. Turnover is lower and the resale buyer pool is wider.

## What the new build is genuinely better at

- **Warranty.** One year on everything, two on systems, ten on structure
  from most production builders. HB25-1272 (2025) added an opt-in programme
  for attached housing with a warranty and third-party inspection in exchange
  for defect-litigation protections; on single-family the builder warranty
  stands alone.
- **Insurance.** New roof, new wiring, new plumbing: the cheapest landlord
  policy you will find on the Front Range.
- **Capital expenditure.** Nothing for five years, realistically ten on the
  big items.
- **Tenant appeal.** Everything rents faster new.

## Where the new-build math is best right now

Standing inventory, not pre-sales. Builders discount completed houses they
are paying to carry, not houses they have not started. In August 2026 the
deepest incentives we are seeing are in [Castle Rock](/places/castle-rock)
and [Parker](/places/parker) south of Hess, [Erie](/places/erie) and
[Firestone](/places/firestone), [Windsor](/places/windsor),
[Timnath](/places/timnath) and [Severance](/places/severance), and the
Reunion side of [Commerce City](/places/commerce-city) — see the
[I-25 growth belt](/investing/i-25-north-growth-belt) and [Douglas
County](/investing/douglas-county-parker-castle-rock) briefs.

## What would make us walk

- A metro district service plan with debt authorised but not yet issued. The
  mills you see today are not the mills you will pay.
- A builder lender whose "permanent buy-down" is a 7/6 ARM.
- A community where more than a quarter of completed homes are already
  rentals. The HOA will cap rentals next, and your exit buyers are other
  investors.
