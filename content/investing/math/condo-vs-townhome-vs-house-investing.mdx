The cheapest front door in Denver is a $280,000 one-bed condo in
[Capitol Hill](/neighborhoods/denver/capitol-hill). The cheapest front door in
Boulder is a $420,000 two-bed in [Gunbarrel](/neighborhoods/boulder/gunbarrel).
Both rent quickly. Both have HOA dues that have risen 40–80% since 2022,
and in both cases the dues are now the second-largest line in the worksheet
after the mortgage. This is why.

## What happened to the HOA

Between mid-2022 and mid-2023, insurance premiums for Colorado condominium
associations roughly doubled. Hail is the reason — the Front Range is the
second-most hail-prone metro corridor in the country — and several carriers
left the Colorado association market entirely. The policies that remain
carry wind/hail deductibles of 2–5% of insured building value. On a
$30 million building that is a $600,000–1.5 million deductible, which the
association passes to owners as a special assessment the first time a storm
hits.

A standard HO-6 unit-owner policy includes about $1,000 of loss-assessment
coverage. Raise it to $50,000; it costs a few dollars a month.

## The carry comparison

Three Denver-area properties, each renting for roughly $2,400 a month in
2026:

| | Condo, 2-bed, Capitol Hill, 1970s | Fee-simple townhome, 2-bed, Arvada, 2005 | House, 3-bed, Lakewood, 1962 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Price | $385,000 | $445,000 | $520,000 |
| P&I (25% down, 7.25%) | $1,970 | $2,277 | $2,661 |
| HOA | $520 | $180 | $0 |
| Property tax | $175 | $230 | $285 |
| Insurance (HO-6 / landlord) | $60 | $140 | $250 |
| Maintenance + reserve | $120 | $220 | $400 |
| Management + vacancy (13%) | $312 | $312 | $312 |
| **Total carry** | **$3,157** | **$3,359** | **$3,908** |
| **Gap** | **−$757** | **−$959** | **−$1,508** |
| **Expense ratio ex-debt** | **49%** | **45%** | **52%** |

The condo has the smallest gap because it has the smallest loan. Its
operating expense ratio is no better than the house's, and every line the
association controls — dues, assessments, rental caps, pet rules — can change
by a board vote you do not attend.

## What to pull on a condo before you offer

Colorado's Common Interest Ownership Act gives buyers a right to the
association documents; your contract's HOA deadline is when you read them.

1. **The master insurance policy declarations page.** Premium, carrier,
   wind/hail deductible as a percentage, and whether the roof is covered at
   replacement cost or actual cash value.
2. **The reserve study**, and the reserve balance against it. Under 30%
   funded is a special assessment waiting to happen.
3. **Three years of budgets and minutes.** Look for the word "assessment",
   the word "litigation", and the word "roof".
4. **Rental restrictions.** Caps, minimum lease terms, and whether the cap
   is reached. Colorado law limits an HOA's ability to bar rentals outright,
   but caps and waiting lists are lawful and common in post-2010 buildings.
5. **Owner-occupancy ratio.** Under 50% and most conventional lenders will
   not finance your buyer when you sell.

## Why there are so few new condos, and why that may change

After 2008, construction-defect litigation under Colorado's CDARA made
for-sale condos nearly uninsurable to build; attached product on the Front
Range has been overwhelmingly rental apartments or fee-simple townhomes
since. HB25-1272, signed May 2025, created a programme from January 1,
2026 where a builder who offers a warranty and submits to third-party
inspections throughout construction receives protection from most defect
suits, with a six-year window for owners to claim. Builders are enrolling
projects now; the first deliveries are likely 2027–28. For investors, that
means the 1970s–2000s condo stock you can buy today will face new
competition, and it means new-build condos will finally exist as an asset
class here again.

## The attached product that still works

**Fee-simple townhomes in small associations** — eight to forty units, a
light HOA that covers exterior paint, snow and landscaping, each owner
insuring their own structure. The 1990s–2010s stock in
[Arvada](/places/arvada), [Westminster](/places/westminster),
[Centennial](/places/centennial), [Broomfield](/places/broomfield), and the
[Harmony corridor](/neighborhoods/fort-collins/harmony-corridor) in Fort
Collins. The HOA risk is a fraction of a condominium's, the insurance is a
landlord policy you control, and the rent is within $200 of a small house.

**Paired homes and duplex halves** in older Denver, Englewood, and Lakewood:
a party wall and a shared-maintenance agreement, no association at all.

## What would make us walk

- A master policy with an actual-cash-value roof endorsement on a building
  with a roof over twelve years old. The next hailstorm is your assessment.
- A building where the minutes mention a defect claim. The litigation
  suspends most financing and can run five years.
- A rental cap already reached. You are buying a unit you cannot rent until
  somebody on the waiting list sells.
