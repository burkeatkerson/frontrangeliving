Every Front Range submarket sits somewhere on one line. At one end, Boulder:
a 4.5% gross yield, a resale market that clears in days, and a forty-year
appreciation record nobody else in the state can match. At the other,
Pueblo's East Side: 9% gross, a resale market that can go quiet for a
quarter, and flat real prices over the same forty years. The strategy is
knowing which end you are paying for.

## The table

Gross yield is annual rent over price on a typical three-bed house in the
submarket, using this summer's leases. Break-even is the number of years of
3.5% rent growth before a 25%-down, 7.25% purchase with a manager covers its
carry. Liquidity is our read of the resale market's depth.

## How to read it

**Appreciation markets** lose money every month at today's rates and will
keep doing so for most of a decade. You buy them with capital you do not need
back, you hold them for ten years, and the return comes from the land. See
[the brick ranch worksheet](/investing/rent-vs-carry-denver-brick-ranch) for
what that feels like month to month.

**Yield markets** get to break-even in two to five years and then pay you,
modestly, for as long as you want to own them. You buy them with more equity
— [Pueblo's leverage table](/investing/rent-vs-carry-pueblo) shows how much
more — and you accept that selling takes months, not days.

**Balanced markets** are where most people's first Colorado rental belongs.
Aurora, Lakewood, Arvada, Westminster, Loveland, central Colorado Springs:
a 6% gross yield, a resale market deep enough to exit in any season, and
enough rent growth that the carry gap closes inside five or six years. The
[Aurora](/investing/aurora-anschutz-corridor),
[west side](/investing/lakewood-wheat-ridge-edgewater) and
[north metro](/investing/arvada-westminster-northglenn-thornton) briefs cover
the block-level detail.

## Three that are neither

**Highlands Ranch and Lone Tree.** Prices near Denver's, yields near
Boulder's, and an HOA on every parcel. Fine places to live; weak places to
rent out.

**Estes Park, Manitou Springs, Nederland.** Mountain-town STR economics with
STR licence caps already full. Without the nightly rental, the long-term
yield is under 4%. See [the mountain-town STR page](/investing/mountain-town-str-caps).

**Downtown Denver condos.** A 2026 vacancy rate of 8.3% in Denver County
apartments, an HOA that doubled, and a resale market that has not recovered
from 2020. Cheap for a reason.

## What changes this map

Rates, first. At 6% instead of 7.25%, every break-even column shortens by
about two years and the balanced tier starts carrying on day one with
self-management. Apartment supply, second: the 2024–25 delivery wave that
pushed metro vacancy to 6.84% in Q1 2026 is over — completions fell 43% in the
year to Q2 — and single-family rents typically follow apartment rents up
with a lag of a year. Regulation, third, and city by city: see [the rules
pages](/investing) for what each has done since 2024.
