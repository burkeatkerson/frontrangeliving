The ADU pitch arrives in every investor's inbox: buy a house with a deep lot,
drop a unit in the backyard, collect two rents. HB24-1152 made it legal
almost everywhere on the Front Range. It did not make it cheap.

## What the law did

From June 30, 2025, every Colorado municipality with more than 1,000
residents inside a metropolitan planning organisation — the whole Front Range
urban corridor — must allow one ADU on any lot where a single-family house is
allowed. The law strips out the tools cities used to block them: no
owner-occupancy requirement, no extra off-street parking requirement in most
cases, no design standards stricter than for the main house, and
administrative rather than public-hearing approval.

What it left in place: building and fire codes, setbacks, height limits,
utility connection fees, and — importantly — HOA covenants recorded before the
law. Most post-1990 subdivisions in Douglas County, Broomfield and the Powers
corridor have those covenants. Most pre-1960 Denver, Lakewood, Arvada, Fort
Collins and Colorado Springs neighborhoods do not.

## What a detached ADU costs in 2026

| Item | Denver, typical |
| --- | --- |
| Design and engineering | $12,000–25,000 |
| Permits and plan review | $5,000–8,000 |
| Denver Water system development charge | $2,170 (from July 1, 2026) |
| Sewer tap, electrical service upgrade | $8,000–20,000 |
| Construction, 500–650 sq ft, stick-built | $200,000–260,000 |
| Construction, modular, delivered and set | $180,000–230,000 |
| **All-in** | **$240,000–310,000** |

Builders quoting $150,000 for a detached unit are quoting a shell. The number
that keeps coming in from completed Denver projects is $400–500 per square
foot all-in, and the permit timeline runs eight to twelve months from first
drawing to certificate of occupancy despite the administrative-review
mandate.

## The return on a detached unit

$270,000 to build, $1,750 rent: gross return on cost of 7.8%. Net of
vacancy, maintenance, added insurance and the extra property tax the
assessor will add when the improvement is picked up, about 5.5–6%. Financed
with a HELOC or construction loan at 8–9%, it is cash-negative. Paid for in
cash, it is an OK bond with a lot of execution risk.

The appraisal problem makes it worse. Denver appraisers are still inconsistent
about crediting ADU value; completed projects typically appraise at 50–70% of
build cost on a refinance. You cannot reliably build one and pull the money
back out.

## The versions that pencil

**The basement ADU.** A Denver brick ranch with a full basement, seven-foot
ceilings, and a side or rear entrance becomes a legal one-bed unit for
$90,000–150,000: egress windows, a second kitchen, fire separation, a separate
electrical sub-panel. Rent $1,300–1,500. Return on cost 12–18%. This is the
ADU we recommend to people who own the house already.

**The garage conversion.** Detached two-car garages on Denver's west side
and in old Lakewood and Arvada convert for $120,000–180,000 if the slab and
framing are usable. Rent $1,500–1,700. Return on cost 10–14%. Lose the garage
and the main house rents for about $100 less.

**The existing, legal ADU.** The best ADU deal is one somebody else built.
Denver has a few thousand permitted ADUs; they sell at a modest premium to
the house alone, far below build cost. Check the permit history on the city's
portal and confirm the certificate of occupancy exists.

## City by city

- **Denver:** ADUs allowed citywide since the 2024–25 rezoning, detached and
  attached, up to 1,000 square feet or 50% of the main house in most zones.
  The ADU can be your STR if you live on the lot; it cannot be one if you do
  not. See [Denver's rental rules](/investing/denver-rental-rules).
- **Colorado Springs:** ordinance 25-45 implements the state law and removed
  the ability to hold an STR permit on any property with an ADU after June
  30, 2025 (existing legal combinations are grandfathered).
- **Fort Collins and Boulder:** allowed; both have water-tap and
  plant-investment fees that add $10,000–25,000 to the Denver numbers.
- **Aurora, Lakewood, Arvada, Westminster, Thornton:** all amended code by
  the deadline. Lakewood and Arvada have the most convertible detached-garage
  stock on the west side.
- **Douglas County towns:** legal, but most subdivisions carry covenants that
  prohibit them, and the law does not override covenants recorded before it.

## What would make us walk

A purchase price that already includes the ADU you have not built. If the
seller's agent is pricing a $600,000 house at $650,000 "because you can add
an ADU", the $50,000 is paying for a right you already have on every other
lot in the city.
