The Front Range housing market has a seasonality as sharp as its weather,
and it has held through the 2022–26 slowdown: buyers appear in February,
peak in April and May, thin out in the July heat, return after Labor Day,
and vanish from mid-October to late January. The best time to sell is when
the most of them are looking at the fewest listings.

## What 2026 looks like

The metro came into 2026 with more inventory than any spring since 2014:
months of supply above two, a median of 14 days on market in May for the
houses that sold, an average of 45–56 days across all listings by mid-year,
sale-to-list ratios just under 99%, and a third or more of listings taking
at least one price cut. Prices were roughly flat year over year in the
Denver metro and Boulder County, slightly up in the Springs and Fort
Collins, slightly down in Pueblo. Mortgage rates held in the mid-to-high
6s. The Colorado Association of Realtors' read in April was "finding
balance," which is the polite phrase for a market where the seller no
longer sets the terms.

What that means for timing: the season still matters, but the first two
weeks on market matter more. A house priced at the comps in March sells in
two weeks; the same house 5% over the comps in March sits, takes a
reduction in April, and sells in June for less than the March number.

## The windows in detail

**Late February to mid-May.** Buyers who got pre-approved in January and
have been watching for six weeks. New listings ramp through March; by the
third week of April the metro is at peak inventory and peak showings. List
in the first half of the window if the house is ready, so it is not
competing with the April flood. Spring snow is the risk: a March listing
photographed under a foot of snow looks like a March listing.

**June to August.** Family buyers tied to school calendars keep Cherry
Creek, Douglas County, D20 and Boulder Valley houses moving; everywhere
else slows, and afternoon hail and 95°F showings do not help. Listings
that launched in April and did not sell are taking reductions now, which
is the competition.

**Labor Day to mid-October.** The second-best window. The summer
non-sellers have either cut or withdrawn; serious buyers who lost out in
spring are back; new listings are fewer. A contract by October 15 closes
before Thanksgiving. The aspens are the best exterior photos of the year.

**Mid-October to late January.** The fewest buyers and the most motivated
ones — relocations, divorces, investors. A house that must sell now can,
at a price; a house that can wait should wait for February, with the winter
spent on the [pre-listing work](/answers/prepping-house-for-sale-front-range).

## Submarket differences

| Submarket | Timing note |
| --- | --- |
| Denver central, Boulder, Golden | Least seasonal; spring still best; condos slower all year in 2026 |
| Family suburbs (Cherry Creek, Douglas County, D20, Boulder Valley, Poudre) | Strongest March–June; a July listing still works for a school-year move |
| Colorado Springs | PCS season (May–August) adds military buyers; VA financing common; fall window strong |
| Fort Collins, Loveland | CSU calendar: investor buyers of student houses want to close by July |
| Mountain-edge (Evergreen, Estes, foothills) | Summer and early fall — buyers want to see the property without snow; winter access sells it or kills it |
| Pueblo, Greeley | Less seasonal; price is everything; 2026 inventory highest in the corridor |

## The three things that matter more than the month

1. **Price to the comps on day one.** In a balanced market the first
   two weeks produce most of the showings; a reduction later is a signal,
   not a strategy. See [assessed value versus market value](/answers/assessed-value-vs-market-value-colorado).
2. **Condition and documentation.** The pre-listing inspection, the roof
   and sewer paperwork, the permits — see [the prep guide](/answers/prepping-house-for-sale-front-range).
3. **The photos and the first weekend.** Launch Thursday, open house
   Saturday, review offers Monday — the Front Range standard — still works
   when the house is priced for it.
