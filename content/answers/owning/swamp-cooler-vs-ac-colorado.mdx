Look at the roofs of any Denver neighbourhood built before 1990 and a
quarter of them still carry a metal box: the evaporative cooler, which
cools air by pulling it through wet pads, and which only works where the
air is dry. The Front Range is exactly that place — until it is not. Here
is how to live with one, and how to decide what replaces it.

<Diagram name="swamp-cooler" caption="How an evaporative cooler works" />

## How it works, and where it stops

Outside air is drawn through water-soaked pads by a blower and pushed into
the house through a duct; the water evaporating off the pads absorbs heat.
At 95°F and 15% humidity — a normal Denver afternoon — the air leaves the
cooler at about 70°F. At 90°F and 50% humidity — a monsoon afternoon in
late July, or a stretch after heavy rain — it leaves at 80°F and damp.
Because the cooler is moving outside air *into* the house, a window on the
far side must be open for it to work at all; close the house on a smoke
day and it does nothing.

Operating cost is the blower and a small pump: about a quarter of a
kilowatt-hour, or $15–30 a month. Central AC on the same house draws
3–5 kW and runs $60–120 a month on Xcel's time-of-use rate, most of it at
the 5–9 pm peak price. Water use is 3–15 gallons an hour, which Denver
Water counts.

**Keep the cooler** if it is in good shape, you are in Pueblo, the Springs,
Greeley or an older Denver neighbourhood where it is normal, you are home to
open windows, and you are not selling within five years. Add a window AC
unit in one bedroom for the monsoon week and the smoke days.

**Go to central AC** if you have ducts, a furnace with ten years left, and
a house that needs to appraise with AC. Ask for a variable-speed condenser
sized by a Manual J load calculation, not by the old cooler's tonnage.

**Go to a heat pump** if the furnace is also over 15 years old. The 2026
rebate stack — Xcel's $2,250 per ton for cold-climate units, Colorado's
$1,000 credit, the federal $2,000, and Denver's, Boulder County's, Fort
Collins's or Colorado Springs Utilities' local programmes — makes a heat
pump cost about what AC-plus-furnace costs, and it replaces both. See [the
heat pump guide](/answers/heat-pump-colorado-rebates).

**Go ductless** on a house with a boiler and radiators (common in
pre-1940 Denver and Boulder) — one or two mini-split heads in the rooms
you use.

## Removing one

When you take the cooler off the roof, have the roofer patch the deck and
reshingle the spot, and have the duct drop removed and the ceiling closed
— not just capped. A capped cooler duct is an uninsulated hole in the attic
floor that causes ice dams (see [ice dams](/answers/ice-dams-colorado)).
Side-mounted coolers leave a wall opening that needs framing and siding.
Budget $500–1,200 for the removal and patch.
