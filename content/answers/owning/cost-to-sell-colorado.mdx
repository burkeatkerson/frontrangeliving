The number Colorado sellers get wrong is the commission — partly because
it changed in 2024 and partly because it was never the whole cost. The
rest is title insurance, which Colorado custom puts on the seller, a
handful of closing and HOA fees, the tax proration, and the line nobody
budgets: what the inspection objection costs.

## Commissions after the settlement

Since August 2024, under the NAR settlement, buyer-agent compensation can
no longer be advertised in the MLS, buyers sign a written agreement with
their agent that sets what the agent is owed, and the buyer then asks the
seller — in the offer — to pay some or all of it. Sellers decide case by
case. In the Front Range market of 2026:

- **Listing side:** 2–3% is the common range, negotiated with your agent
  and set in the listing agreement. Flat-fee and discount models exist.
- **Buyer side:** a buyer's agreement typically specifies 2–2.5%. Most
  sellers in a balanced market agree to pay it when requested, because a
  buyer who must pay their own agent out of pocket has less for the house;
  some offer a fixed amount; some offer nothing and price lower.
- **Total:** where both sides are paid, roughly 4.5–5.7%; where the
  seller pays only the listing side, 2–3%.

What you cannot do is ignore it: an offer that asks for 2.5% buyer-agent
compensation is an offer that is 2.5% lower in net, and you compare offers
on net.

## The lines explained

**Title insurance.** Colorado custom (and the standard contract's default)
has the seller buy the owner's policy that protects the buyer's title;
the buyer pays for the lender's policy. Rates are filed and similar across
companies — roughly $1,500–2,500 on $600,000 — with a reissue discount if
you bought within the last few years and can produce your policy.

**Documentary fee.** $0.01 per $100 of consideration, paid at recording.
Colorado has no transfer tax outside a few resort towns.

**Property tax proration.** Colorado taxes are paid in arrears — the 2026
bill is paid in 2027 — so at closing you credit the buyer for January 1
through the closing date at the most recent mill levy. On a $600,000 house
at 90 mills, closing July 1, that is roughly $1,700.

**HOA and metro district.** The status letter ($150–400), a transfer fee
($100–500), and in many Douglas County and newer communities a
working-capital contribution of one to three months' dues charged to the
buyer or seller per the covenants. The metro district itself charges
nothing at sale; its levy simply prorates with the taxes.

**Water and utilities.** Final readings; Denver Water and CSU close out at
the closing date. Some districts (Castle Rock, Parker) require a final
read and bill the seller.

**The inspection objection.** In 2026's market the buyer asks for
something on almost every resale; $2,000–10,000 in concessions or repairs
is typical on a post-war house that was not prepped. The [pre-listing
work](/answers/prepping-house-for-sale-front-range) is how you move that
number toward zero.

## Taxes on the gain

Federal: the Section 121 exclusion shelters $250,000 of gain ($500,000
joint) on a primary residence owned and occupied two of the last five
years. Gain is the sale price minus selling costs minus basis, and basis
includes capital improvements — the roof, the sewer line, the basement
finish, the heat pump, the egress window — so the folder of receipts you
kept for the buyer is also your tax file. Colorado taxes gain above the
exclusion at its flat income rate (4.4%). Front Range owners who bought
before 2013 in Denver, Boulder or Fort Collins are increasingly over the
single exclusion; talk to a CPA before listing, not after closing.

## What you net

On the $600,000 example with a 2.75% listing fee, 2.5% buyer-agent
compensation agreed, title and closing, a $1,700 tax proration, $800 in
HOA fees and a $5,000 inspection concession: about $555,000 before the
mortgage payoff — 92.5% of the price. Without the buyer-agent
compensation, about $570,000. That spread is why the commission
conversation now happens offer by offer, and why the [pricing](/answers/assessed-value-vs-market-value-colorado)
should start from the net, not the list.
