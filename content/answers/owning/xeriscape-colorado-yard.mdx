The bad xeriscape is everywhere on the Front Range: a front yard scraped to
dirt, covered in landscape fabric and red rock, three junipers, and a heat
island that kills the tree. The good one is invisible as xeriscape — it
looks like a garden. The difference is a plan that assigns water by zone,
and it is what the utilities pay for.

## The principle

Group plants by water need and give each group its own irrigation zone.
Turf where people actually walk, play or sit — often a 300–600 square foot
patch in the back — on spray heads. Shrub and perennial beds on drip, which
uses a third of the water of spray. Street strips, side yards and slopes,
where nobody goes, in natives that live on rain after the second year. The
rock is mulch between plants, not the landscape.

Three more rules from twenty years of Front Range conversions:

1. **Keep the trees.** A mature shade tree is worth more than the lawn
   under it, and it was watered by that lawn. Put it on its own drip ring
   or a bubbler before you remove the turf, and water it through the first
   two winters.
2. **Mulch with wood in beds, rock only on paths and strips.** Rock mulch
   against the house raises summer wall temperatures 10–20°F; wood mulch
   holds moisture and cools the soil.
3. **Amend the clay.** Two to three inches of compost worked into the top
   six inches before planting. Natives tolerate clay; they establish in
   loosened clay.

Water saved: bluegrass at 1,000 square feet uses roughly 20,000–30,000
gallons a summer; the same area in established natives on drip uses
3,000–6,000. At Denver Water's summer tier rates that is $150–300 a year,
more in Douglas County's district water; at Castle Rock's rates the
conversion pays back in under five years.

## The plants that work here

From Plant Select and the CSU Extension lists, for sun at 5,000–6,500
feet, USDA zone 5b–6a, clay soil, 15 inches of rain:

| Use | Plants |
| --- | --- |
| Low-water turf | Blue grama (Hachita, Alma), buffalograss (Legacy, Prestige), Dog Tuff for traffic |
| Shrubs | Fernbush, Apache plume, rabbitbrush, three-leaf sumac, leadplant, Russian sage, mountain mahogany, Mojave sage |
| Perennials | Penstemon (Rocky Mountain, pineleaf), blanket flower, chocolate flower, prairie coneflower, agastache, Kannah Creek buckwheat, salvia, winecups, red birds in a tree |
| Grasses | Little bluestem, blue avena, prairie dropseed, Indian ricegrass |
| Ground cover | Sedum, ice plant (Delosperma), creeping veronica, Turkish speedwell, woolly thyme |
| Trees (new) | Kentucky coffeetree, bur oak, hackberry, Western catalpa, hawthorn — not ash, not silver maple |

Avoid: junipers against the house in any wildfire zone, anything on the
state's noxious list (Russian olive, tamarisk), and "drought-tolerant"
plants sold for the Southwest — Texas sage and most agaves die at −15°F.

## Rules that help you

Colorado HOAs cannot ban xeriscape or backyard artificial turf. The 2024
state law against new non-functional turf applies to commercial and common
areas; Castle Rock, Aurora and Broomfield cap front-yard turf on new homes.
Denver's and CSU's summer watering rules are in [the watering guide](/answers/lawn-watering-rules-front-range);
the irrigation changes are in [the sprinkler guide](/answers/sprinkler-blowout-colorado).
