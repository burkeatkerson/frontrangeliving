The Front Range's mature shade canopy is almost entirely planted; the
shortgrass prairie had cottonwoods along the creeks and nothing else. What
the 1950s–70s planted was silver maple, green ash, elm and honeylocust,
and the ash — roughly one in six metro trees, 330,000 in Denver alone — is
now being killed by a beetle that reached Boulder in 2013 and Denver proper
in 2024. Meanwhile the city owns the strip your street tree stands in and
you own the tree.

The line is the back of the sidewalk: everything between it and the curb
is public right-of-way. Private yard trees are yours without a permit,
except in Boulder and Denver historic districts and for trees on the
state's noxious list.

## The ash problem

Emerald ash borer larvae feed under the bark, cutting off water; a tree
shows a thinning upper canopy, sprouts from the trunk, D-shaped exit holes
and woodpecker damage, and dies in two to five years. Confirmed on the
Front Range as of 2026: Boulder, Gunbarrel, Longmont, Lafayette, Louisville,
Superior, Lyons, Erie, Broomfield, Westminster, Arvada, Thornton, Denver
(four neighbourhoods first, now spreading), Littleton, Centennial, the
Berthoud area and north of Fort Collins — more than twenty cities, and the
Springs and Pueblo are expected.

Three Front Range-specific points. **Winter watering** is the one that
matters: our winters are dry and windy, the soil desiccates, and trees —
especially spruce, pine and anything planted in the last three years — die
of winter drought more often than cold. **Sunscald**: the low winter sun
heats the south-west side of a thin-barked young tree (maple, linden,
honeylocust) on a 50°F afternoon and it freezes that night; wrap trunks
from November to April for the first three years. **Do not top a tree**:
the Front Range's hard winds and wet spring snows break topped regrowth;
hire an ISA-certified arborist for crown reduction.

## Removing a tree

Private yard trees need no permit in most cities (Boulder and historic
districts excepted). Parkway trees need the city's permit and usually a
replacement. Xcel and Colorado Springs Utilities prune for line clearance
for free and will coordinate a removal near lines. Stump grinding is
$150–400. And check the sewer: a large tree over the lateral is often the
reason the roots are in it — see [the sewer guide](/answers/sewer-line-colorado-house).
