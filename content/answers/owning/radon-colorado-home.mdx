Radon is a radioactive gas produced by uranium decaying in granite and
shale — which is what the Front Range is made of. It seeps up through soil
into basements and crawlspaces, and it is the second-leading cause of lung
cancer in the United States. The Colorado Department of Public Health and
Environment's testing data put about half of the state's homes at or above
the EPA's action level; Jefferson, Boulder, Larimer and Douglas counties
and the foothills towns run highest, the plains east of I-25 lower, and no
Front Range zip code is reliably clear.

## The fix

Active sub-slab depressurisation: a 3- or 4-inch PVC pipe set into a small
pit under the basement slab, routed up through the house or along an
exterior wall, with an inline fan in the attic or outside that pulls soil
gas from under the slab and vents it above the roofline, where it disperses.
Slab cracks and the sump pit are sealed so the fan draws from the soil
rather than the room. A U-tube manometer on the pipe shows the fan is
pulling.

Installers should hold an NRPP or NRSB certification; Colorado began
licensing radon professionals in 2022 and the Department of Regulatory
Agencies lists them. Post-mitigation levels are typically under 2 pCi/L,
and the installer retests to prove it. A passive system — the pipe without
the fan — is required in new construction in Denver, Boulder, Fort Collins
and many other Front Range jurisdictions; adding the fan to one is a $300
job, and most new-build owners never do it.

## When to retest

- Every two years.
- After any work that opens the slab or foundation: a finished basement, a
  sewer line replacement, an egress window, a sump install.
- After replacing the furnace or adding central air — both change the
  pressure in the house.
- In winter, if your last test was in summer.
- Before you list. The [seller disclosure guide](/answers/colorado-seller-disclosures)
  covers what SB23-206 requires.

## The one thing people get wrong

Finishing a basement without mitigating first. You have just built a
bedroom in the room with the highest reading in the house, and the drywall
now covers the slab cracks you would have sealed. Test before the framing
goes up; see [finishing a basement](/answers/finishing-a-basement-colorado).
