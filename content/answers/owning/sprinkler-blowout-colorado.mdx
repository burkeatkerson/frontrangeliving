The irrigation system is the Front Range's most-broken home system for one
reason: it is the only one that must be deliberately shut down every
autumn, and nobody tells new owners. Water left in the pipes freezes in
October, the backflow preventer's brass body cracks, and the first sign is
a geyser in April. The whole annual routine is below.

## Autumn: the blow-out

Do it after the lawn no longer needs water and before the first hard
freeze — in practice the first two weeks of October in the metro, late
September in Fort Collins and the foothills, mid-October in Pueblo. A
single 25°F night is enough to crack a backflow preventer.

  { title: "Set the backflow preventer's two ball valves to 45°", body: "Half-open, not closed. Closed traps water in the body and it cracks; 45° lets it drain and keeps the seats from freezing in place. Open the two small test cocks a quarter turn.", tag: "key step" },
  { title: "Connect the compressor at the blow-out port", body: "A quick-connect or hose bib fitting on the downstream side of the backflow preventer, or a dedicated blow-out tee. Stop at mist — running dry air through heads for minutes overheats the gears. Do each zone twice in rotation rather than once for a long time.", tag: "2 min / zone" },

Drip zones need less pressure (30 psi) and less time. Systems with an
automatic drain valve at the low point still need air — the drains stick.

## Spring: start-up and the backflow test

After the last hard freeze, which on the Front Range is late April in the
metro and early May north and west. Opening in March for a warm week and
getting a 15°F night on April 20 is the second most common way to crack a
backflow preventer.

## The upgrades that pay

A **smart controller** (Rachio, Hunter Hydrawise, Rain Bird) that skips
watering after rain and adjusts to temperature cuts use 15–30% and most
Front Range utilities rebate $50–100 of it. **Pressure-regulated heads and
nozzles** stop the misting that wastes a third of spray-zone water in our
wind. **Converting beds to drip** is the largest single saving and is
required for new landscapes in many cities. Denver Water, CSU, Aurora and
Fort Collins each publish a rebate list; see [xeriscape and rebates](/answers/xeriscape-colorado-yard).

## If you missed it

If the first freeze came before the blow-out, do it the next warm day —
damage from one light freeze is usually limited to exposed parts. If the
backflow preventer shows a split in the brass or leaks from the body in
spring, replace it before the test; a cracked one fails the test anyway.
