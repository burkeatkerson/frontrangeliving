A semi-arid climate with wet basements sounds wrong until you watch a Front
Range summer storm: two inches of rain in forty minutes onto clay that
sheds it like pavement, every gutter overflowing, every window well a
bathtub. Or March: a foot of snow melting on ground frozen solid, with
nowhere to go but the foundation wall. The water gets in at the surface,
which is the good news — surface problems have cheap fixes.

## Where it comes in

| Symptom | Where the water came from |
| --- | --- |
| Wet floor along one wall after a storm | A downspout at that corner, or grade sloping in |
| Water through a window well | Well uncovered, full of leaves, or with no gravel drain |
| Damp at the floor-wall joint all spring | Snowmelt against the wall; clogged or no perimeter drain |
| Wet under the stairs or at a pipe penetration | Grade at that spot; a sprinkler head against the wall |
| Efflorescence (white crust) on block | Chronic moisture through the wall; usually grade and gutters |
| Water rising through a floor crack | Hydrostatic pressure — the rare groundwater case, or a high creek |
| Sewage in the floor drain | Not surface water: the sewer line (see the sewer guide) |

will tell you whether they worked.

A sump pump handles the house that has a perimeter drain discharging to a
pit, or sits in a low spot. Most Front Range sump pits are dry eleven
months a year and the pump fails from disuse — test it each spring by
pouring a bucket in, and add a battery backup because the storm that fills
the pit is the one that takes out the power. An interior perimeter drain
(a channel cut along the slab edge feeding a sump) is the usual answer to a
chronic floor-wall seep after the surface work is done; exterior excavation
is the last resort.

## Flood zones and flood insurance

The homeowners policy excludes flood — water entering from outside at
ground level — and covers only the sewer/drain backup if you bought the
endorsement. Flood insurance is a separate NFIP or private policy, required
by lenders in a FEMA Special Flood Hazard Area and a good idea within a
block or two of any Front Range creek.

Front Range flood history is creek history: the 1965 South Platte flood,
the 1976 Big Thompson, the 1997 Fort Collins Spring Creek flood, and the
September 2013 event that hit Boulder Creek, the St. Vrain, the Big
Thompson, Fountain Creek and Evans all at once and redrew the maps. Check
your address on the FEMA map; if you are in the 100-year zone the lender
already made you buy it, and if you are in the 500-year zone (shaded X)
the policy costs $400–800 a year and is worth it.

## The finished basement

Everything above matters three times as much once the basement has
carpet, drywall and a bedroom in it. Before finishing: fix the water,
[test for radon](/answers/radon-colorado-home), and read [finishing a
basement](/answers/finishing-a-basement-colorado) — the floating-wall
detail and the egress window are both about the same clay and the same
water.
