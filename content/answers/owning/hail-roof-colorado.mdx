The Front Range corridor from Fort Collins to Pueblo is one of the most
hail-prone places in the country — the 2017 storm across Lakewood, Wheat
Ridge and Golden was Colorado's costliest insured event, and the 2018
Fountain and 2023–24 east-metro storms followed. Hail is why your
insurance doubled, why your deductible is a percentage, and why a roofer
will be at your door within a day of a storm. Here is the order of
operations.

## The claim

An adjuster inspects, usually within one to three weeks after a big storm
(longer after a regional one). Have your roofer present. The adjuster's
estimate lists the replacement cost value (RCV), subtracts depreciation to
reach actual cash value (ACV), and subtracts the deductible. On a
replacement-cost policy you receive ACV up front and the withheld
depreciation when the work is done and invoiced. On an ACV roof
endorsement you receive only ACV — on a 15-year-old roof with a 25-year
shingle, about 40% of the cost — and the rest is yours.

Disputes go to the carrier's re-inspection, then appraisal (each side
names an appraiser, they pick an umpire), then the Division of Insurance's
complaint process. Colorado requires carriers to acknowledge a claim within
a set period and pay undisputed amounts promptly.

## Replacing the roof

If you are replacing, three decisions:

**Class 4.** Impact-rated shingles (Malarkey Vista/Legacy, Owens Corning
Duration Flex, GAF Armorshield, CertainTeed NorthGate) cost 10–20% more and
earn a 10–30% premium discount from most Colorado carriers; some carriers on
the plain will only write Class 4. Metal and stone-coated steel are Class 4
by nature and last twice as long at twice the price. Colorado's 2026 law
(SB26-155) created a state grant programme for FORTIFIED-standard roofs;
it prioritises owner-occupants and is worth checking before you sign.

**The contractor.** Colorado does not license roofers statewide; Denver,
Aurora, Lakewood, Colorado Springs (through the Pikes Peak Regional
Building Department) and most cities require a contractor licence and a
permit for a re-roof, with inspection. Use the permit as your quality
control — a roofer who suggests skipping it is telling you something.
Written contract, itemised scope matching the adjuster's estimate, no
payment beyond materials until the job is done, certificate of insurance
in hand.

**Ice-and-water shield and ventilation.** Code in every Front Range city
requires self-adhered membrane at the eaves; ask for it over the whole
north slope on a foothills house. Add ridge and soffit ventilation while
the roof is off — it is the cheapest ice-dam prevention there is. See
[ice dams](/answers/ice-dams-colorado).

## After

Keep the permit, the invoice and the shingle warranty registration in the
house file; your next buyer's insurer will ask for the roof's age and
class, and a transferable Class 4 warranty is a selling point. Put the date
in your calendar: in ten years the next storm's adjuster will depreciate
from it.
