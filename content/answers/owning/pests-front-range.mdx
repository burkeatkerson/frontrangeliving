The Front Range is a semi-arid grassland with a forest on one side and a
city in the middle, and everything that lives in the grassland and the
forest would like to live in your house in October. Most of it is seasonal
and predictable, which means most of it is preventable.

## The ones that matter

**Mice and rats.** House mice are universal; Norway rats have established
in central Denver and Aurora since about 2018. Mice enter through any gap a
pencil fits through — the garage door's bottom corners, the gap around the
AC line set, dryer vents, the sill plate. September is sealing month: steel
wool and caulk in every gap, a garage-door bottom seal, 1/4-inch hardware
cloth over vents. Snap traps along walls, peanut butter, and none of the
poison blocks that kill the fox that eats the mouse.

**Raccoons and squirrels.** Raccoons go down uncapped chimneys to den
(February–May), and fox squirrels chew into attic gables and soffits.
Symptoms: thumping at dusk, a smell, insulation pulled through a vent. A
chimney cap ($60–200) and metal flashing over any chewed soffit is the
prevention. Removal of a raccoon with young is a licensed-wildlife-control
job ($300–600); in Colorado you may not relocate them yourself.

**Wasps.** Paper wasps under eaves are mostly harmless; yellowjackets in a
wall void or a ground nest near the patio are not, and they are worst in
late summer. Treat paper-wasp nests at dusk with a jet spray; leave a
wall-void yellowjacket nest to a professional ($300–500) — sealing the hole
drives them into the house. Hang a false nest in April; yellowjackets are
territorial.

**Voles.** The Front Range's winter lawn pest. Surface runways in March, a
girdled crabapple in April. Mow short in October, keep mulch 6 inches off
trunks, wrap young tree trunks with hardware cloth to 18 inches, and let
the lawn grow back. No poison; the neighbourhood's Cooper's hawks and great
horned owls are doing the work.

**Bears and mountain lions.** On the foothills edge — Boulder, Golden,
Evergreen, Ken Caryl, Roxborough, the Springs' west side, Manitou, Palmer
Lake, Monument — bears are a trash-management problem from April to
November. Bear-resistant carts (required in Boulder and parts of Jeffco,
El Paso and Boulder counties), no bird feeders April–November, pick fruit
as it ripens, clean the grill, lock the garage. Mountain lions follow deer
into the same neighbourhoods; keep pets in at night. Colorado Parks and
Wildlife's rule is that a bear that gets into houses twice is destroyed,
so the stakes are the bear's.

**Prairie dogs.** Not in your yard unless you back onto open space, in
which case they are protected by most cities' ordinances and the
city's problem. Plague does circulate in Front Range colonies; keep pets
off them.

## The ones that do not

Miller moths (May): three weeks, no damage, no spray. Box elder and elm
seed bugs (autumn): vacuum them; they are on the sunny wall for warmth and
leave. Earwigs and sowbugs in the window wells: drainage, not insecticide.
Spiders: the Front Range has no recluses; the occasional black widow in a
window well or woodpile is the only one to take seriously, and a glove
handles it. Bats in the attic: protected, beneficial, and excluded with a
one-way door in August after the pups fly, never in June.

A pest-control contract at $40–60 a month is mostly perimeter spray you do
not need; a one-time exclusion job ($800–2,000) that seals the building
envelope is the version that works, and it is also [air sealing](/answers/ice-dams-colorado)
you would pay for anyway.
