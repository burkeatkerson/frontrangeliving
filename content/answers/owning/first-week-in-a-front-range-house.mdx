Every house has a first-week list. A Front Range house has a specific one,
because the things that go wrong here — the sewer lateral, the clay under
the slab, the hail roof, the sprinkler system nobody showed you, the gas
furnace that will run for seven months — are not the things that go wrong
where you came from. Do these fourteen in roughly this order.

Set the start date for closing day, not move-in day; a gap leaves the
furnace off in a house you own.

## The three that matter most

**The radon test.** Roughly half of Colorado homes test above the EPA
action level. Run a 48-hour kit in the lowest lived-in level, doors and
windows closed. Above 4 pCi/L, [mitigate](/answers/radon-colorado-home)
($1,200–2,500); the system is a pipe and a fan and it is a selling point later.

**The sewer line.** If your inspection did not include a camera scope of the
lateral, book one now. Pre-1985 lines from the house to the street are clay
or Orangeburg fibre pipe, and you own the whole run — including the part
under the sidewalk and the street. A root-choked joint found in week one is
a $300 cleaning; found in year three as a backup in the basement it is
$10,000–18,000 and a street cut. See [the sewer-line guide](/answers/sewer-line-colorado-house).

**The sprinkler system.** The controller is in the garage; the backflow
preventer is the brass assembly on the outside wall or in a box by the
meter. Most water providers require it tested annually by a certified
tester, and the whole system must be blown out with compressed air before
the first hard freeze in October. Your seller may have left it running on a
July schedule. See [sprinkler start-up and blow-out](/answers/sprinkler-blowout-colorado).

## The two facts to know about your bill

Your property tax is set by the county assessor's value and a mill levy
that varies street to street, and if you bought in a post-1995 subdivision
you are probably in a metro district that adds 30–70 mills. Pull the parcel
now, read the levy breakdown, and put June 8 in the calendar: that is the
protest deadline in a reappraisal year, and the value you are protesting
lands on May 1. [How to read and protest your tax bill](/answers/protest-property-tax-colorado).

Your insurance policy almost certainly carries a **percentage wind/hail
deductible** — 1% or 2% of the dwelling limit, so $4,500–9,000 on a typical
house — rather than the flat $1,000 you may be used to. Write the dollar
figure down. It is the amount you pay before the insurer pays anything on the
roof after the next hailstorm, which on the Front Range is a when, not an
if. [The insurance guide](/answers/homeowners-insurance-colorado-hail).

## What can wait until month two

Furnace service (October is the month), the first sprinkler adjustment,
HOA paperwork beyond confirming the dues autopay, the senior exemption
application if you qualify (the deadline is July 15, and you need ten years
of occupancy anyway), and the landscaping. The [first-year calendar](/answers/front-range-homeowner-calendar)
lays the rest out month by month.
