Between 2020 and 2026 the average Colorado homeowners premium roughly
doubled, to about $6,630 — the fourth highest in the nation. The Division
of Insurance's own analysis found that hail accounts for 26–54% of
premiums depending on county and about half along the Front Range and
eastern plains, where most of the state lives. Wildfire is a smaller share
of the average and the whole story in the specific zip codes it touches.
Carriers responded by raising rates, moving to percentage deductibles,
depreciating older roofs, and in the foothills, leaving.

## The five lines on the declarations page

1. **Coverage A (dwelling).** The cost to rebuild, not the market value —
   land is most of a Front Range price. Rebuild cost here runs $200–300 a
   square foot in 2026; a 1,800 square foot ranch is $400,000–500,000 of
   dwelling coverage. Under-insuring to save premium triggers co-insurance
   penalties on a partial loss.
2. **Wind/hail deductible.** Flat or percentage. Write the dollar figure on
   the fridge. Going from 1% to 2% typically cuts premium 10–15%; the
   question is whether you can write a $9,000 cheque after the storm.
3. **Roof surfacing endorsement.** Replacement cost, or actual cash value
   (sometimes a "roof payment schedule" by age). ACV on a roof over ten
   years old means you fund most of the next replacement. Some carriers
   impose ACV automatically at 15 years; ask.
4. **Water backup (sewer and drain).** Excluded by default; $40–100 a year
   to add; the most common non-hail claim on the Front Range. Add it.
5. **Ordinance or law.** 10–25% of Coverage A to bring a 1958 house to 2024
   code after a loss. Add it on anything pre-1990.

Plus liability ($500,000 minimum; an umbrella over it), loss of use (12–24
months), and on the foothills edge, wildfire-specific endorsements and
extended replacement cost.

## Shopping it

Every September, before the autumn renewal cluster. An independent agent
who writes with six or more carriers — in Colorado that typically includes
State Farm, American Family, Farmers, Allstate, USAA (military), Auto-Owners,
Safeco, Travelers, Nationwide and the regional mutuals — can quote the same
house across the market in an afternoon. Bring: square footage, year built,
roof age and material (and class certificate), panel type, plumbing type,
claims history (order your CLUE report free once a year), and the current
declarations page.

Ask each quote the same five questions: dwelling amount, wind/hail
deductible in dollars, roof basis, water backup included, ordinance or law
included. Quotes that are 30% cheaper are usually cheaper on one of those
lines.

## The foothills

West of Highway 93 and Highway 115 — Evergreen, Conifer, Genesee,
Nederland, the Boulder mountain parcels, Mountain Shadows and Peregrine in
the Springs, Roxborough, Estes Park — the question is not the premium but
whether a standard carrier will write the policy at all. Since the 2012–13
Springs fires, the 2020 Cameron Peak and East Troublesome fires and the
2021 Marshall Fire, carriers have non-renewed whole zip codes.

What works: a wildfire mitigation assessment from the Colorado State
Forest Service or the county (Jeffco, Boulder and El Paso run programmes),
documented defensible space, a Class A roof, ember-resistant vents, and
shopping the surplus-lines and regional carriers through an agent who does
it routinely. The FAIR Plan is the backstop. See [defensible space](/answers/wildfire-defensible-space-colorado).

## Before the storm

The policy you want is the one you have read. Dwelling at rebuild cost,
the deductible in dollars in your reserve, replacement cost on the roof,
water backup on, ordinance or law on, the roof's age and class in the
file. The [hail guide](/answers/hail-roof-colorado) covers what to do when
it comes.
