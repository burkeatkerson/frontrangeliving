Every Front Range utility is under a state or city mandate to cut gas use
and peak electric demand, and the cheapest way for them to do it is to pay
you. In 2026 the list is long enough that a homeowner who plans a year of
upgrades around it can get a third to a half of the cost back. The trick
is the order, because the audit qualifies the insulation, the insulation
qualifies the heat-pump sizing, and the heat pump qualifies the panel.

## Time-of-use first

If you are on Xcel (Denver, most of the metro, Boulder, Greeley), your
electric rate already changes by the hour. The peak — 5 to 9 pm on
weekdays — costs roughly three times off-peak. No upgrade required:

Municipal utilities — Colorado Springs Utilities, Fort Collins, Longmont,
Loveland — are not on time-of-use by default, though Fort Collins and CSU
offer optional TOU rates that reward the same behaviour.

## Claiming them

Utility rebates are applied for by the contractor or online within 60–90
days of the invoice, with the invoice and the model numbers; the heat-pump
and insulation tiers usually require a participating contractor. The state
heat-pump credit is taken as a point-of-sale discount by registered
installers. The federal 25C credit is claimed on your tax return (Form
5695) with the manufacturer's certification; it is capped per year, so
spread large projects across two tax years. Keep every invoice in the house
file — your buyer's inspector and your appraiser will want the heat pump's
model and the insulation's R-value.
