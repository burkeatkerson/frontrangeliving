## By era of house

**1890–1930 (Whittier, Baker, Old North End, Old Town Fort Collins).**
Systems first: the sewer, knob-and-tube out, a 200-amp panel, a boiler or
a ductless heat pump, the roof. Then bathrooms — these houses were built
with one. Kitchens last and sympathetically; buyers of these houses are
buying the house, not a 2026 kitchen.

**1945–1975 (the post-war ring everywhere).** The basement is the whole
game: egress, a bath, a permit, and the house goes from 2/1 to 3/2 or 4/2.
Then central air, then the panel and the sewer, then the attic. The
original kitchen in a brick ranch is smaller than any buyer wants; a
refresh, not a gut, because the wall you want to take out is usually
bearing.

**1975–2000 (Highlands Ranch, Briargate, Aurora's subdivisions, the
Springs' north).** Roof and siding (the hail years), windows if still
builder-grade, the furnace/AC pair, and the popcorn ceilings and oak
cabinets — paint and hardware transform the latter for $3,000. Polybutylene
plumbing out if it is there.

**2000–2020.** The fewest returns from improvement; the house is what
buyers expect. Spend on condition — paint, carpet, the landscaping the
builder skimped on — and on the metro-district and HOA documents being in
order.

## What the buyer's side looks at

1. **The inspection report** — roof age, furnace and water heater age,
   panel type, sewer scope, radon, foundation, grading. Every item on it
   is a negotiation; every item you fixed with a receipt is not.
2. **The appraisal** — bedroom and bath count, above-grade versus
   below-grade square footage, garage, central air, condition rating.
   A permit record for the basement is what lets the appraiser count it.
3. **The insurance quote** — the buyer's carrier asks roof age and class,
   panel type, plumbing type. A house that quotes at $2,400 beats one that
   quotes at $4,200 before anyone sees the kitchen.
4. **The listing photos** — the front elevation, the kitchen, the main
   bath, the yard. Paint, lighting, hardware and the garage door are the
   cheapest photos you can buy.

## What we tell sellers two years out

Do the inspection-report items now, with permits and receipts, while you
live with the benefit. Do the bedroom-count item (egress, basement bath)
if the house is a 2-bedroom or has one bath. Budget the kitchen refresh
for the spring you list, so it is new in the photos. Skip the pool, the
suite and the gut remodel unless you are staying ten years. And put every
invoice in one folder: [the pre-listing guide](/answers/prepping-house-for-sale-front-range)
starts with it.
