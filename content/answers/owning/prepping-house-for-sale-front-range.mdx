  - q: "Should I get a pre-listing inspection before selling in Colorado?"
    a: "Yes, almost always. A $400–600 inspection with a sewer scope and radon test tells you what the buyer's inspector will find, lets you fix it on your schedule with your contractors, and turns the inspection objection — the step where most Colorado deals are renegotiated — into a formality. Anything you learn and do not fix must be disclosed, so decide before you order it that you will deal with what it finds."
  - q: "What repairs should I make before listing my house?"
    a: "The ones that are objections, not the ones that are preferences: a Federal Pacific or Zinsco panel, a missing expansion tank or T&P discharge on the water heater, a furnace that has not been serviced, grading and downspouts, lifted or missing shingles, a cracked backflow preventer, GFCI outlets, smoke and CO alarms, the sewer line if the scope shows it, and radon mitigation if over 4 pCi/L. Then paint and the yard. Not the kitchen."
  - q: "When should I take listing photos in Colorado?"

A March listing photographed under snow reads as a March listing; if you must list in winter, use last summer's exterior shots and disclose it."

The Front Range inspection objection is where deals are renegotiated:
the buyer's inspector produces a thirty-page report on a 1962 ranch, and
the buyer asks for the roof, the sewer, the panel and $8,000. The seller
who already knows what is in that report — because they commissioned it
three months earlier and fixed the items with receipts — does not have
that conversation. That is the whole method.

## Front Range specifics

- **Photograph before hail season** if listing in spring; in autumn, shoot
  in the last week of September when the trees turn.
- **The lawn must be green in the photos**, which in a drought year means
  the allowed watering days and a pre-photo soak (within the rules). A
  well-done xeriscape photographs better than a brown lawn.
- **Mountain views** are a line item: shoot from the upstairs window and
  the back deck on a clear morning after a front.
- **The garage** is a room here — sweep it, light it, and show the
  storage.
- **Altitude light** is harsh: shoot early, blinds half-open, lights on.
- **Buyers will ask for the utility history**: Xcel and CSU provide twelve
  months; a house with a new heat pump and R-49 attic has a story to tell
  in it.

## The folder

One PDF, handed to every buyer's agent at showing: the pre-listing
inspection with the repair receipts, the sewer video and invoice, the
radon test and mitigation paperwork, the roof's age and class and permit,
the furnace and water heater invoices, the permit history, the HOA
documents and status letter, the metro district site, the insurance
declarations page (so the buyer's agent can quote it), and the survey or
ILC. It answers the inspection objection before it is written. The
[disclosure guide](/answers/colorado-seller-disclosures) covers what is
required; this folder is what is persuasive.
