Three numbers arrive before the listing conversation: the county's, the
app's, and the one the seller remembers from the neighbour's sale in 2022.
None of them is the price. Front Range houses are priced the way
appraisers price them, from closed sales, and in 2026 the market punishes
a list price that came from anywhere else.

## Front Range adjustments that surprise out-of-state sellers

- **Basement square footage is not main-floor square footage.** The MLS
  and the appraiser separate above-grade and below-grade; a 1,200 square
  foot ranch with a 1,200 square foot finished basement is a 1,200 square
  foot house with a finished basement, not a 2,400 square foot house.
- **Egress decides bedroom count.** A basement room without a conforming
  window is not a bedroom on the appraisal, whatever the listing says.
- **Central air matters in Denver and Boulder**, is neutral in the Springs
  and Pueblo.
- **Backing to open space or a greenbelt** adds 3–8%; backing to a
  four-lane arterial subtracts about the same.
- **The school articulation area**, not the district, in Cherry Creek,
  Jeffco, Douglas County and D20.
- **A metro district levy** shows up in the buyer's payment and in their
  agent's comparison; two identical houses with a $300-a-month tax
  difference are not identical.
- **Mountain views** are worth something everywhere and a great deal on
  the west side; "peek-a-boo view from the bedroom" is worth nothing.
- **Roof and systems age**: a 2025 Class 4 roof and a 2024 heat pump are
  line items the buyer's insurer and inspector will confirm.

## What the appraisal will do

After you are under contract, the buyer's lender orders an appraisal, and
the appraiser runs the same exercise with the same comps. In 2026's
market, appraisals coming in under contract price are more common than in
2021 — the contract price was set by one buyer on one weekend, the
appraisal by the last six months of sales. A gap means the buyer brings
cash, you reduce, or the deal dies. Pricing from the comps in the first
place is how you avoid the conversation; the [folder of receipts](/answers/prepping-house-for-sale-front-range)
is what supports the condition adjustments when the appraiser visits.

## The assessor's number, once more

It is useful for one thing at sale: if it is well above your realistic
price, [protest it](/answers/protest-property-tax-colorado) before June 8
so the buyer's first tax bill is not an objection. If it is well below,
say nothing; the value does not reset on sale in Colorado, and the buyer
inherits it until the next reappraisal.
