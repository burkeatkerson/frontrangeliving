The Front Range has a climate that does one thing at a time, hard: a
six-week leasing-and-moving season, a hail season, a freeze that arrives in
one night in October, a dry winter with 60°F days and −10°F nights in the
same week. The house runs on that schedule whether you do or not. This is
the year as we run it on our own houses.

## Month by month

**January.** Tax bills arrive; new state laws take effect. Run a radon test
now — winter is when readings peak and the state and several counties hand
out kits. Ice dams form after the first big storm followed by a warm spell;
see [ice dams](/answers/ice-dams-colorado). Keep hose bibs disconnected.

**February.** First half of the tax bill due by the 28th if you split it.
Chinook winds along the foothills take fences and shingles; walk the roof
line from the ground after a wind event. Prune deciduous trees while
dormant — but not ash, and not street trees in Denver without a [free
Forestry permit](/answers/trees-front-range-yard).

**March.** Denver Water and Colorado Springs Utilities announce the summer
watering status (in 2026 Denver declared a Stage 1 drought on March 25).
Order xeriscape materials and Garden in a Box kits now; they sell out.
Schedule the sprinkler start-up and backflow test.

**April.** Start the sprinklers after the last hard freeze — usually late
April in the metro, early May in Fort Collins and the foothills. The
**backflow preventer test** is due at start-up in most districts; the
tester files the report. Full tax bill due April 30. Aerate and overseed
cool-season lawns. Swamp coolers: install pads, reconnect water, check the
float.

**May.** Watering rules begin May 1. Hail season opens; keep the car in the
garage on afternoons with a storm forecast. In odd years the **Notice of
Valuation** arrives by May 1 — read it the day it comes. Treat ash trees
for emerald ash borer once the leaves are fully out. Plant after Mother's
Day; the last frost at 5,280 feet is not reliably over before then.

**June.** **June 8: property valuation protest deadline** (odd years).
Second half of the tax bill due June 15. Peak hail month. Turn the sprinkler
controller to the summer schedule; Denver Water's 2026 rules allow two days
a week, Colorado Springs Utilities three. See [watering rules by city](/answers/lawn-watering-rules-front-range).

**July.** Hottest month; monitor the AC condensate line and the swamp cooler
pads. If you protested, the assessor's decision arrives by the end of June
and the **County Board of Equalization appeal is due July 15**. Check the
roof after every storm. Senior exemption applications are due July 15.

**August.** Hail tapering. Lawns stress at 95°F; the rules still apply. The
Front Range's monsoon pattern brings afternoon rain; check that downspouts
discharge away from the foundation after a heavy one.

**September.** Book the sprinkler blow-out and the furnace service now. Shop
the insurance renewal. First frost is possible from mid-September in Fort
Collins and the foothills. Wildfire chipping programmes run their last
rounds.

**October.** **Blow out the sprinklers by mid-month.** Service the furnace,
replace the filter, test CO alarms. Disconnect every hose. Drain and cover
the swamp cooler. Watering rules end October 1 (Denver) or 15 (Springs).
Clean gutters after the cottonwoods drop. Insurance renewals.

**November.** First snow. Sidewalks cleared within 24 hours in most cities
— see [snow rules by city](/answers/snow-removal-rules-front-range). Start
winter tree watering on warm days. Check attic insulation depth before the
cold sets in.

**December.** Cold snaps to −10°F: open cabinet doors under sinks on
exterior walls, let a tap drip on the coldest nights, and know where the
main shut-off is. Photograph the roof's snow load pattern — bare patches on
a snowy roof are where heat is escaping. Plan next year's projects; Xcel and
CSU rebate cycles reset January 1.
