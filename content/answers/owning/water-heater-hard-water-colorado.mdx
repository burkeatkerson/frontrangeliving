faq:
  - q: "How hard is the water on the Front Range?"
    a: "Denver Water's mountain supply is moderately hard, about 90–120 ppm (5–7 grains). Colorado Springs, which blends Arkansas River and Colorado River water, runs 150–250 ppm. Aurora, Thornton and the Douglas County districts that draw on the South Platte and deep aquifers run 200–300 ppm, firmly hard. Fort Collins and Boulder, on snowmelt, are softest at 30–80 ppm. Harder water means more sediment and a shorter tank life."
  - q: "Do I need a thermal expansion tank on my water heater in Colorado?"
    a: "Almost certainly yes. Most Front Range water systems have backflow preventers or pressure-reducing valves at the meter, making the house plumbing a closed system; when the heater warms the water it expands and has nowhere to go, spiking pressure and lifting the relief valve. Codes in Denver, the Springs and most cities require an expansion tank on the cold inlet. Inspectors flag its absence on every sale; it is a $150–300 add."

Pull the drain valve on a Denver water heater that has never been flushed
and what comes out is milky, then gritty, then a cup of white sand. That is
the Front Range's dissolved calcium and magnesium, dropped out of solution
every time the burner fires, sitting on the bottom of the tank insulating
the water from the flame. The tank works harder, the bottom overheats, and
at year ten it rusts through. The routine below gets you to fifteen.

## Replacing it

Gas tanks last 8–12 years in hard-water areas, 12–15 in Denver and Fort
Collins. Rust at the base, rusty hot water, rumbling during heating, or a
tank over 12 years old: plan the replacement before it chooses the day.

The **heat-pump water heater** is the 2026 answer for any house with a
basement or utility room of at least 700 cubic feet: it uses a third of
the electricity of a resistance tank, dehumidifies the basement as a side
effect, and the rebates — Xcel's and Colorado Springs Utilities' $400–800
plus the federal 25C credit of 30% up to $2,000 — bring it to the price of
a gas tank. It is slower to recover, so size up a tier. Denver's
electrification code now favours it on replacement.

**Tankless** works best in Fort Collins and Boulder's soft water and on
houses with the gas capacity; in the Springs, Aurora and Douglas County it
needs a softener or it scales shut in three years. At altitude, de-rate
the output 20–25% when sizing.

## Permits

Every Front Range jurisdiction requires a plumbing permit for a water
heater replacement, and most a mechanical permit for gas; the inspection
checks the expansion tank, the T&P discharge, the venting and the gas
connection. Denver e-permits it in a day; the Pikes Peak Regional Building
Department handles the Springs. A plumber who skips the permit is skipping
the inspection that catches the flue problem that causes the CO call. See
[permits](/answers/permits-front-range-homeowner).
