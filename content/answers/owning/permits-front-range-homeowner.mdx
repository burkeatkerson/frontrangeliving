Permits are where Front Range homeowners most often get the rules wrong
in both directions — pulling one for a fence they did not need, and
finishing a basement without one that they did. The logic is the same in
every city: anything that touches structure, life safety (egress, fire
separation), electrical, plumbing or gas needs a permit and an inspection;
anything cosmetic does not. The details differ by jurisdiction.

## Who issues it

**Denver.** Community Planning and Development, almost entirely through
the e-permits portal. Simple homeowner permits (water heater, furnace,
fence, re-roof) issue same-day or in a few days; a deck or basement finish
goes through plan review in two to six weeks. Fees $50–100 for trade
permits, $150–600 for a basement or deck. Historic districts and Landmark
properties add design review. Zoning permits (fence height, setbacks,
ADUs) are separate from building permits and come first.

**Colorado Springs and El Paso County.** The Pikes Peak Regional Building
Department — one regional office for the Springs, the county, Fountain,
Manitou, Monument, Palmer Lake and Woodland Park — handles permits, plan
review, inspections and contractor licensing. Homeowner permits are
available for the primary residence you live in. Zoning sign-off comes
from the city or county first; floodplain review where applicable. PPRBD
publishes a residential handout that lists exactly what needs a permit.

**The suburbs.** Each city has its own building division (Aurora,
Lakewood, Arvada, Westminster, Thornton, Littleton, Englewood, Boulder,
Longmont, Fort Collins, Loveland, Greeley, Castle Rock, Parker), and
unincorporated areas go through the county (Jeffco, Arapahoe, Adams,
Douglas, Boulder, Larimer, Weld). Highlands Ranch is Douglas County. Most
have online portals; most issue trade permits in days and plan-reviewed
permits in two to six weeks.

## Contractors

Colorado licenses plumbers and electricians statewide; general contractors
and roofers are licensed by each city or by PPRBD. Ask for the licence
number and a certificate of insurance (general liability and workers'
comp), and verify both — Denver and PPRBD have online lookups. A contractor
who suggests skipping the permit is asking you to carry the risk they are
paid to carry. On any job over $10,000, pay in stages tied to inspections,
and never more than 10–30% up front; Colorado's mechanics' lien law lets
an unpaid subcontractor lien your house even if you paid the general, so
get lien waivers with each payment.

## When you sell

The [seller disclosure](/answers/colorado-seller-disclosures) asks about
unpermitted work; the buyer's inspector and appraiser look for it; the
city's permit record is public. The basement you finished in 2019 without
a permit is a basement you will permit retroactively, discount, or
disclose and hope. See [finishing a basement](/answers/finishing-a-basement-colorado)
and [fence rules](/answers/fence-rules-front-range) for the two jobs that
come up most.
