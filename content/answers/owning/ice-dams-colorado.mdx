The call comes in the third week of January: a stain spreading on the
ceiling near an exterior wall, or water dripping from the top of a window
frame, in a house with no plumbing anywhere near it. Outside, the eave
above that spot carries a ridge of ice and a row of icicles. That is an
ice dam, and the Front Range produces them reliably because our winters
alternate a foot of snow with a week of 45°F sun.

## Why it happens

The roof over a heated attic is warmer than the eave that overhangs the
wall. Snow on the warm part melts from underneath; the water runs down to
the eave, which is at outdoor temperature, and freezes. Each cycle adds to
the ridge. Eventually the melt behind the ridge has nowhere to go but up
under the shingles — which shed water running down, not pooled — and
through the deck into the wall and ceiling.

The heat gets into the attic three ways, in order of importance: air
leaks (recessed lights, the attic hatch, plumbing and wiring
penetrations, the top of interior walls), thin insulation (most pre-1990
Front Range houses have R-11 to R-19 of settled fibreglass; code is now
R-49 to R-60), and ductwork or a furnace in the attic. A north-facing eave,
a valley, or a roof section over a vaulted ceiling or a heated garage is
where it shows first.

Do it in that order: seal first (insulation over air leaks is a filter,
not a barrier), insulate second, ventilate third so the attic stays cold.
Xcel Energy's Home Energy Squad and insulation rebates, Colorado Springs
Utilities' insulation and air-sealing rebate, Fort Collins Utilities'
Efficiency Works and Denver's CARe programme all pay part of it, and the
federal 25C credit covers 30% of insulation cost up to $1,200 a year. An
audit first — many of the utilities subsidise it — tells you where the
leaks are and qualifies you for the rebates. See [the rebate guide](/answers/xcel-time-of-use-rebates-colorado).

## Houses that get it worst

- 1950s–70s ranches with recessed lights added in a 1990s kitchen remodel.
- Split-levels where the upper roof dumps onto the lower one.
- Anything with a vaulted ceiling and no ventilation channel.
- North and east eaves in the foothills, where the snow stays.
- Houses with the furnace and ducts in the attic (common in 1990s–2000s
  Douglas County and Springs construction).

## After

If the ceiling stained, the insulation under it is wet and has lost its
R-value; replace that section. If water came down the wall, open the
drywall a foot above the base and dry the cavity before it grows mould.
At the next re-roof, ask for ice-and-water membrane six feet up the eave
instead of the two feet code requires; see [the hail and roof guide](/answers/hail-roof-colorado).
