The Front Range does not have a cold winter so much as a mild one with
violent exceptions: a 60°F afternoon, then an arctic front, then −12°F at
6 am. Pipes that survive every ordinary night freeze on that one, and in
the same five places in every house. The fix is an October checklist and a
cold-night routine.

<Diagram name="hose-bib" caption="Why the hose matters more than the bib" />

## Where Front Range houses freeze

1. **Hose bibs with hoses attached.** The most common freeze damage in the
   corridor and entirely preventable. A frost-free sillcock drains itself
   only if nothing is connected.
2. **Supply lines in exterior walls** — the kitchen sink on the north
   wall, the upstairs bathroom over the garage, the laundry in an addition.
   Cabinet doors closed, the pipe sits at near-outdoor temperature.
3. **Crawlspaces and garages.** Pipes run through an unheated crawlspace
   with open vents, or along the garage ceiling to a hose bib.
4. **The sprinkler backflow preventer** and any irrigation pipe not blown
   out. See [the sprinkler guide](/answers/sprinkler-blowout-colorado).
5. **Swamp-cooler water lines** — the ¼-inch copper or poly line up the
   side of the house to a rooftop cooler. Drain and disconnect in October.

## On a −10°F night

Open the cabinet doors under every sink on an exterior wall. Run a
pencil-thin stream from the tap farthest from the main, hot and cold; moving
water freezes far more slowly. Keep the thermostat at least 60°F overnight
— the $4 of gas is cheaper than a ceiling. If the garage has plumbing, keep
the garage door closed and crack the door into the house. If a tap runs
slow or stops, that line is freezing: open it fully, warm the wall or
cabinet with a hair dryer or a space heater (never a torch), and leave the
tap open so the thaw has somewhere to go.

## If a pipe has frozen

No water at one tap, or a faint hiss in a wall: shut the main, open the
affected tap, and warm the section gradually — hair dryer, heat lamp,
towels soaked in hot water. Once flow returns, check every joint in the
thawed run for drips over the next hour; the split opens on the thaw. If
you cannot find the frozen section or water is already coming through
drywall, shut the main and call a plumber; a freeze call in a cold snap is
$250–500 and the queue is long.
