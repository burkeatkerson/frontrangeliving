Two cities an hour apart run their household services on opposite models.
Denver unbundles: a private investor-owned utility for gas and electric, a
separate municipal water board, and a city trash department that charges by
the cart. Colorado Springs bundles everything into one municipally owned
utility — and then leaves trash to the market entirely. Most suburbs land
somewhere between.

## Denver

**Xcel Energy** supplies gas and electricity to Denver and most of the
metro. Since 2022 the default residential electric rate is **time-of-use**:
on-peak from 5 to 9 pm on weekdays, off-peak the rest of the time, with a
price spread of roughly three to one between them. Shifting the dishwasher,
laundry, EV charging and the AC pre-cool out of that four-hour window is
worth $15–40 a month on a typical house. You can opt out to a flat rate
through your account, and Xcel has a rate case pending that would raise
average residential bills by about 10% from late 2026.

**Denver Water** bills water and, on the same statement, Denver's
wastewater and stormwater charges. Summer watering rules run May 1 to
October 1; in 2026 the board declared a Stage 1 drought with mandatory
two-day-a-week watering. See [watering rules](/answers/lawn-watering-rules-front-range).

**Trash** is the city's Solid Waste Management, and since January 2023 it
is pay-as-you-throw: $9 a month for a 35-gallon cart, $13 for 65, $21 for
95, with recycling every other week and weekly compost included. Most
households that compost fit in the 35 or 65. Large-item pickup is by
appointment. Change the cart size through 311 or the Denver Utilities
Online portal — it is the one utility decision that saves money for doing
less.

## Colorado Springs

**Colorado Springs Utilities** is the city's own four-service utility —
electric, gas, water, wastewater — on a single bill, governed by city
council sitting as the utilities board. Rates are set locally and have
historically been below Xcel's for electricity and above for water (the
city pumps most of its supply over the Continental Divide). CSU may ask
for a deposit to start service if you have no payment history with them;
it is billed on the first statement and refunded after twelve months of
on-time payment. Water Wise rules — three watering days a week, none from
10 am to 6 pm — run May 1 to October 15.

**Trash** is entirely private. Colorado Springs is the largest city in the
country with no municipal collection; you call a hauler, they deliver a
96-gallon cart, and you pay them directly, typically $20–35 a month with
recycling. Prices vary by neighbourhood and the haulers compete, so ask
neighbours what they pay. HOAs often contract one hauler for the whole
subdivision and include it in dues.

Three things to notice. Municipal electric utilities — Fort Collins,
Longmont, Loveland, Colorado Springs — price below Xcel and are not on
time-of-use by default. District water in Douglas County (Castle Rock,
Parker, Centennial Water for Highlands Ranch) is the most expensive water
on the Front Range because it is pumped from deep aquifers and the
districts are paying for renewable supply projects. And Black Hills, which
serves Pueblo's electricity, has the highest residential rates in the
state.
