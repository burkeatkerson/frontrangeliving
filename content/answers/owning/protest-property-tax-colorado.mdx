The first tax bill on a Front Range house surprises in both directions:
lower than Texans expect, higher than the listing implied if you are in a
metro district. It is worth understanding the arithmetic once, because
the one thing you can change about it — the value — has a deadline that
arrives in your first spring.

## The formula

```
taxable value = (actual value − 10% of the first $700,000) × assessment rate
annual tax    = taxable value × total mill levy ÷ 1,000
```

- **Actual value** is the assessor's estimate of market value on the
  appraisal date — June 30 of the even year before an odd-year reappraisal.
  2025 and 2026 bills use June 30, 2024 values; the 2027 reappraisal will use
  June 30, 2026.
- **The reduction** is 10% of value up to $70,000, set by the 2024 special
  session.
- **The assessment rate** for 2026 is 6.8% for local-government levies and
  7.05% for school levies. It has changed in four of the last five sessions.
- **The mill levy** is the sum of every district you sit in. One mill is $1
  per $1,000 of taxable value.

## Reading the bill

The county treasurer's bill (January) lists each taxing authority and its
mills. The lines to find:

1. **School district.** Usually the largest single levy. Adams 12, 27J and
   Pueblo 60 are high; Douglas County and Colorado Springs D11 are lower.
2. **Metropolitan district(s).** If you see one or more "Metro District"
   lines, you are repaying your subdivision's infrastructure bonds. Typical
   40–70 mills combined, for 30–40 years, and the district can raise its
   mills to hold debt service flat when values fall. The district's service
   plan and debt are public at the Division of Local Government.
3. **City, county, fire, library, water, recreation.** Smaller and mostly
   fixed.
4. **TABOR / temporary credits.** Some districts show a temporary mill
   reduction; it can be withdrawn without a vote.

## Protesting the value

You cannot protest the mill levy. You can protest the assessor's actual
value, and in a reappraisal year about a third of residential protests
succeed at least in part.

Most Front Range counties — Denver, Jefferson, Arapahoe, Adams, Douglas,
Boulder, Larimer, Weld, El Paso, Pueblo — take protests online and publish
the comps they used. Start there. A denial costs nothing; the CBOE step is
where well-documented cases most often win.

## The senior exemption

Age 65 or older on January 1; owner-occupant of the home as primary
residence for ten consecutive years; 50% of the first $200,000 of actual
value exempt. Apply once to the county assessor by **July 15**, and it
carries forward. For 2025 and 2026 only, a temporary portability law lets
seniors who had the exemption and moved between January 2020 and December
2025 claim it on the new home (apply by March 15). Disabled veterans with a
100% permanent rating, and Gold Star spouses, have a parallel exemption.
The exemption is not available on a rental.
