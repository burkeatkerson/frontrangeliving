Every Front Range family owns the same memory: the "easy" 4-mile hike
that ended with a 45-pound child on someone's shoulders at mile three.
The fix is not tougher kids; it is picking trails where the payoff —
water, rocks, a tunnel, fish — is visible early and often. These are
the ones that work, by home base.

## From Boulder and the north

**Chautauqua's first meadow** (free shuttle on summer weekends —
believe the parking warnings) gives the Flatirons at half a mile;
turn around whenever. **Wonderland Lake** is the flat 1.5-mile
preschool loop with a playground at the trailhead. **The Fowler
Trail at Eldorado Canyon** ($10/car, book a timed slot on summer
weekends) is 0.8 miles of canyon wall with climbers to watch —
binoculars turn it into an hour. **Mud Lake** and **Caribou Ranch**
above Nederland add aspen and moose odds in fall. Up north, **Devil's
Backbone** in Loveland (free) delivers its keyhole rock in the first
mile, and the **Poudre River path** through Fort Collins is the
stroller default with the whitewater park as destination.

## From Colorado Springs

Garden of the Gods before 10am is the anchor — Perkins Central paved
loop for strollers, the Siamese Twins spur (0.5 mi) for the Pikes
Peak photo through the window. Then: **Red Rock Canyon Open Space**
(free, the quarry loop's slabs are a natural playground), **the
Paint Mines** in Calhan (free, 45 min east — hoodoos in sherbet
colors, kids must stay off the formations but the trail winds right
through), **Fox Run Regional Park's** Fallen Timbers loop in the
Black Forest pines, and the two nature-center parks — [Bear
Creek](/columns/saturday-with-kids/bear-creek-nature-center) and
[Fountain Creek](/columns/saturday-with-kids/fountain-creek-nature-center)
— whose short loops come with an indoor exhibit room for the
weather-turn. **Seven Bridges** (North Cheyenne Cañon) is the step-up:
seven creek crossings, turn around at any of them.

Altitude fine print: everything above 8,000 feet halves the distance
rule and doubles the water; RMNP's Bear Lake is glorious and needs the
timed-entry permit May–October (recreation.gov, released in monthly
blocks). And afternoon lightning is the one non-negotiable — mountain
hikes are morning hikes, June through August. The step up from here is
[the mountain day](/answers/mountain-day-with-kids), and the winter
version is [the sledding list](/answers/sledding-hills-front-range).
