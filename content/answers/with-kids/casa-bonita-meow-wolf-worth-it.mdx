## Doing Casa Bonita right

Book the seating release the week it opens — follow the restaurant's
list or Mile High on the Cheap for the date. Take the earliest seating
you can get: the divers run continuously, the cave has no line at 4:30,
and a 7-year-old at an 8pm seating is a countdown. Order the flag up
for sopaipillas early and often (they are unlimited), give each kid a
set number of arcade tokens *before* entering the arcade, and walk the
whole building — the mariachi room, the puppet theater, the gorilla.
It is on West Colfax in Lakewood with its own free lot;
[Sloan's Lake](/neighborhoods/denver/sloan-lake) across the street
absorbs the post-sugar hour.

## Doing Meow Wolf right

Weekday morning tickets, eat first (there is no real food inside),
check the stroller at the door and bring the carrier for anyone under
4. Give grade-schoolers the QDOT device or just one instruction — "find
the secret passages" — and let them lead; the fridge door, the dryer,
and the fireplace are the openers they will find first. Ear protection
helps sensitive kids; the staff will point you to the quieter floors.
Two hours in, everyone is done — leave before the whining, it preserves
the magic. The site sits at the Decatur-Federal W Line stop, which for
a [train-phase kid](/answers/train-obsessed-kid-colorado) makes the
journey half the event.

## If you are choosing one

Under 8: Casa Bonita — it is aimed at them, and dinner is handled.
Nine and up, or an art-leaning crew: Meow Wolf, which rewards exactly
the age that has started calling things boring. Visiting grandparents:
Casa Bonita, no contest. And neither is a weekly habit — the weekly
habits are the [free list](/answers/free-things-kids-front-range) and
the [museum rotation](/answers/museums-with-kids-front-range); these
two are for birthdays, visitors, and the February Saturday that needs
a rescue.
