Colorado family skiing looks unaffordable from the ticket window —
$250 walk-up days exist — and is quietly near-free for children,
because every resort's business model needs your kids hooked. The
system, assembled:

Enroll in both kid programs each fall (proof of grade; the Passport
wants a report card or letter). The double-dip is legal and intended.

## The parent problem, solved by geography

The adult answer is the small hills, which are also the better kid
hills:

- **Loveland** — 60–75 min, no tunnel, the Valley learning area,
  $500-ish adult passes bought in spring, free snowcat-served
  progression later. The default Denver family hill.
- **Eldora** — Boulder County's own, RTD-bus reachable, no I-70 at
  all; on Ikon if a parent carries one anyway.
- **Echo Mountain** — under an hour from the west metro, cheap,
  night skiing; a learning hill, not a destination, which is the
  point.
- **Monarch** — the Springs/Pueblo family mountain over its own pass;
  deep snow, small crowds, sane prices.
- **Granby Ranch / Ski Cooper / Sunlight** — the far ring of cheap,
  gentle, uncrowded.
- **A-Basin and Copper** — the step-ups with credible kid deals and
  (for A-Basin) a spring season that runs to June.

Buy any adult pass in **March–May for next season** — every product
is cheapest at spring launch and most expensive in November. If the
budget is day tickets, buy online weeks ahead (half the window rate)
or grab the 4-packs sold each fall.

## Gear: lease, never buy, never rent daily

Season leases — Christy Sports, Epic Mountain Gear, local shops —
run $150–250 for a kid's full setup, swap sizes mid-season free, and
beat daily rentals by the third ski day. The Passport's two free
Christy junior rentals cover the maybe-kid. Helmets always; goggles
from the swap; and the November **ski swaps** (Loveland Ski Club,
schools, churches — every Front Range town has one) are where locals
actually buy.

## Lessons versus the magic-carpet parent

Ski school is superb and $200–300 a day at the big resorts. The
budget sequence that works: the **free Epic SchoolKids first-timer
lesson** (use it — it is a real lesson), then parent-taught
magic-carpet laps at Loveland Valley or Echo on the cheap, then —
if the kid is hooked — one multi-week program (Eldora's, Loveland's
and Monarch's weekend programs run far cheaper per hour than
day-school) to fix your bad habits. Do not teach your own kid past
the wedge-turn stage; everyone involved deserves better.

## The I-70 doctrine

The mountain day fails in the car, not on the hill. Rules: leave by
6:30 or after 9:30, never both directions in the Saturday peak; ski
**Sundays over Saturdays**, and powder-day-plus-one over powder days;
check chain-law status before leaving (traction law applies to
everyone Sept–May — real snow tires, legally and actually);
Eldora and Monarch exist so you can skip the corridor entirely. Pack
the [sledding-kit checklist](/answers/sledding-hills-front-range)
items plus goggles, and food from home — the $22 chicken tenders are
where ski budgets actually die.
