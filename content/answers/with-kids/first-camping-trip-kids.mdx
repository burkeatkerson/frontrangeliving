The first family campout fails on ambition, not on kids. A remote
national-forest site with no bathrooms and a two-hour drive is a
second-season trip. The first one wants three things: close to home,
a reservation with your name on it, and a lake or creek to burn the
daylight. Colorado's front-country state parks were practically
designed as trainer campgrounds.

## The trainer parks

| Park | From | Why it works for trip one | Fine print |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Chatfield SP (Littleton) | 30 min metro | Flush toilets and showers, swim beach, bike paths, the balloon launches at dawn | The most competitive booking; four campgrounds |
| Cherry Creek SP (Aurora) | 25 min metro | Same amenities, even closer; you can hear the city, which is the point | Shade varies — pick loops with cottonwoods |
| Golden Gate Canyon SP | 50 min from Denver | Actual mountains, aspens, Reverend's Ridge's showers, panorama point | Nights cold at 9,100 ft; book the yurts early |
| St. Vrain SP (Longmont) | 40 min north metro | Flat pond-side sites, easiest logistics anywhere, birdlife | I-25 hum; ponds are no-swim (fishing) |
| Boyd Lake SP (Loveland) | NoCo's Chatfield | Swim beach, marina, paved loops | Open sun — bring shade |
| Jackson Lake SP | 90 min northeast | Sand beach, legendary sunsets, dark skies | The 'far' trainer; summer weekends easier to book |
| Mueller SP (Divide) | 50 min from the Springs | The Springs' Golden Gate: aspens, wildlife, big sites | 9,500 ft — pack for the 30s at night |
| Cheyenne Mountain SP | 15 min from the Springs | Camp with the city lights below; excellent camper services | The closest real campground to any Front Range city |
| Lake Pueblo SP | 20 min from Pueblo | Water everywhere, long season, easier availability | Shade is the scarce resource |

Book at **cpwshop.com**: site fee ~$28–41 (electric higher, ~$36–48)
plus the $10–12 daily vehicle pass (waived with the $29 Keep Colorado
Wild pass from your vehicle registration). Every site's photo,
shade and bathroom distance is on the listing — with kids, filter for
**flush toilets** and pick a site one loop away from them: close
enough to walk, far from the traffic.

## The cheat codes

**Camper cabins and yurts.** Golden Gate, Mueller, Chatfield, St.
Vrain, Cheyenne Mountain and others rent heated camper cabins and
yurts ($90–150/night): beds, a roof, a locking door, the campfire out
front. This is the right call for a first trip with anyone under 4,
for September, and for the household's camping skeptic. They book
six months out to the minute.

**The backyard dress rehearsal.** Pitch the tent at home the weekend
before. Kids sleep in it; you find the missing pole at 4pm, not at
dusk; the actual trip starts with a practiced crew.

**Borrowed gear.** Gear swaps, Buy Nothing, REI used. The only things
worth buying new for trip one: headlamps for each kid ($10 — the
single highest-joy item in camping), and sleeping pads, because cold
comes from below.

Round two, next season: the national-forest classics — Kelly Dahl and
Pawnee above Nederland, Camp Dick and Olive Ridge up the Peak to Peak,
Rampart Reservoir's loops above the Springs, Eleven Mile's canyon
sites (recreation.gov, also six months) — where bathrooms go vault
and the stars get serious. The [fishing pond skills](/answers/fishing-with-kids-colorado)
transfer directly, and [the mountain-day rules](/answers/mountain-day-with-kids)
about storms and altitude apply from the first trip on.
