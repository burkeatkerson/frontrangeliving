The Front Range built hundreds of miles of car-free trail along its
creeks and canals, then built free bike parks on top — which means
the kid-bike years here are genuinely great if you learn two lists:
the flat paths with destinations, and the parks where skills happen.

## The paths, by home base

| Path | Best kid segment | Surface | The destination |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Highline Canal (71 mi, south metro) | deKoevend Park ↔ Writer's Vista; Eisenhower Park stretch | soft gravel, dead flat | playgrounds every 2 miles; cottonwood shade |
| Clear Creek Trail | Prospect Park (Wheat Ridge) → Golden | paved | Golden's creek beach + ice cream |
| Cherry Creek Trail | Four Mile Historic Park → Confluence | paved, below street grade | REI flagship + the kayakers at Confluence |
| South Platte Trail | Hudson Gardens → Breckenridge Brewery stretch | paved | trains, herons, root beer |
| Bear Creek Trail | Lakewood's stone house → Morrison edge | paved | Bear Creek Lake beach |
| Ralston Creek / Van Bibber | through Arvada | paved | Olde Town tacos |
| Boulder Creek Path | Eben G. Fine → 55th | paved | tubing spectators, Scott Carpenter pool |
| Coal Creek / Rock Creek | Louisville–Lafayette–Superior loop | paved | the towns themselves |
| Poudre Trail | Lee Martinez Park ↔ whitewater park | paved | Fort Collins' river scene |
| Loveland Recreation Trail | around Lake Loveland | paved | the lake beach |
| Pikes Peak Greenway | Monument Valley Park core | paved | Uncle Wilber fountain |
| Santa Fe / New Santa Fe Trail | Palmer Lake ↔ Monument | gravel | train-watching at Palmer Lake |
| Fountain Creek Trail | to the nature center | paved/gravel | the boardwalks |
| Pueblo River Trail | along the Arkansas + levee art | paved | the riverwalk and whitewater park |

The pattern for a first ride: flat, water on one side, a payoff in
the middle, six miles max. The Highline Canal's gravel is the most
forgiving crash surface in the metro, which is not a small thing
with a new rider.

## The bike parks (all free)

**Valmont Bike Park, Boulder** is the flagship — dirt tracks, slopestyle
lines, tot loops, glory laps for every ability, and the reason a
seven-year-old suddenly corners like a BMX kid. **Ruby Hill's Railyard**
in Denver doubles as the winter terrain park; **Ulysses Skate & Bike
Park** in Golden, **Erie's SPACE bike park**, **Berthoud's** and
**Frederick's pump tracks**, **Sandstone Ranch** in Longmont,
**Louisville's Davidson Mesa-adjacent track**, and the Springs'
**Bear Creek bike park** and **Memorial Park pump track** round out
the circuit. Etiquette is the curriculum: one rider per feature,
ride the direction of the arrows, session small before big.

Strider-age (2–4): the tot loops at Valmont and most pump tracks are
exactly balance-bike scaled, and an empty school parking lot on
Sunday remains undefeated.

## Skills, rules, and the trail-a-bike question

Colorado has no statewide bike helmet law — every family treats it as
one anyway. Path rules that keep the peace: kids ride right, call
passes ("on your left" is a toddler's favourite phrase within a
week), stop fully at every road crossing even where the path has the
underpass — the one arterial crossing is where [the bike-to-school
test](/answers/kid-bike-to-school) gets decided. Gear progression
that works here: balance bike → 16-inch with hand brake (skip
training wheels entirely; the balance-bike generation doesn't need
them) → 20-inch with gears for the canal rides → the trail-a-bike or
cargo e-bike for the gap years when their legs quit at mile four and
the family still wants ten.

When the pump track stops being enough: green singletrack. **Bear
Creek Lake Park**, **Marshall Mesa's** doubletrack (Boulder), **Lory
State Park's** valley trails (FoCo), and **Red Rock Canyon's** lower
loops (Springs) are the standard first real dirt — morning rides,
per [the storm rules](/answers/mountain-day-with-kids).
