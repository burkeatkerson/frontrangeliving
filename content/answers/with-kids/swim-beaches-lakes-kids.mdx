faq:
  - q: "Is it safe to swim in Colorado reservoirs with kids?"
    a: "At the designated beaches, yes, with open-water rules: most are unguarded (Boulder Res staffs lifeguards in its swim season; the rest post swim-at-your-own-risk), bottoms are sandy-to-gravel with gradual drop-offs, and swimming outside marked areas is prohibited and enforced. Life jackets are legally required for anything floatable beyond the swim line, and the parks loan kid jackets free at several beaches. The real hazards are sunburn at altitude and cold water — not depth."
  - q: "What is the blue-green algae problem?"
    a: "Warm, shallow reservoirs grow cyanobacteria blooms in late summer that can be toxic to kids and lethal to dogs. Parks test weekly and post closures — Cherry Creek and Chatfield's swim beaches close for stretches most Augusts. Check the park's algae status page the morning of, keep dogs out of still water in August–September regardless, and rinse everyone after any lake day."
  - q: "Can we bring floaties and paddleboards to the swim beach?"
    a: "Inside the buoyed swim area: small floaties generally yes, at your own risk. Nobody moves to Colorado for the beaches, and then the first 95°F July
Saturday arrives and the whole metro discovers that the state parks
have them: real sand (trucked), real swim areas (buoyed), and water
that was snow in April. Managed expectations — cold water, afternoon
wind, algae Augusts — make for genuinely great kid days. Here is the
list.

## The metro three

**Cherry Creek State Park** (Aurora, $11/vehicle) has the biggest
beach scene: a long sand crescent, shallow entry that stays
waist-deep for fifty yards, bathrooms and showers, rentals nearby,
and the metro's warmest water by August. It is also the busiest —
before 10 on weekends or don't. **Chatfield State Park** (Littleton,
$12) has two swim beaches on its gravel-pond swim area (separate from
the main reservoir), calmer water, better shade trees, and the
balloon launches over the dam as a bonus show. **Bear Creek Lake
Park** (Lakewood, ~$10 city-run) is the sleeper: a smaller beach on
Big Soda Lake, paddleboard rentals on Little Soda, warm shallow
water, and lighter crowds — the best under-6 beach in the metro.

## North

**Boulder Reservoir** (city-run, ~$7 walk-in/kid rates, parking fee)
is the only beach up here with actual lifeguards in season, plus a
swim-lesson program and boat rentals; north Boulder's summer
institution. **Union Reservoir, Longmont** (~$10/vehicle) is
wakeless — no motor chop — with a mellow beach, cottonwood shade and
the best paddleboard-with-kids water in the region. **St. Vrain State
Park** ponds are fishing-first (no swimming) but the wading birds
and playground make it the backup picnic. Up north, **Windsor Lake**
runs a sandy swim beach walkable from downtown Windsor, and
**Horsetooth Reservoir's** swim beach at South Bay puts foothills
cliffs behind the sand ($10/vehicle, Larimer County).

## The Springs and south

**Prospect Lake** in Memorial Park (Colorado Springs) is the in-town
beach: free parking, sand, a swim season with posted hours, pedal-boat
rentals, and Pikes Peak over your shoulder. **Lake Pueblo State
Park's Rock Canyon** swim beach ($10/vehicle plus a small beach fee
some seasons) is the south's big one — attached to a paddle-craft
cove and, next door, the **Pueblo Whitewater Park** where big kids
surf the Arkansas under supervision. **Palmer Lake** (the town) allows
no swimming — it is a watershed — but the adjacent playground and
train-watching make it the picnic consolation.
