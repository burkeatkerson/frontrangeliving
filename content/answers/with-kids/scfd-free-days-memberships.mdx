The Front Range hides a genuinely unusual deal: since 1989, a penny on
every $10 spent in the seven metro counties goes to the Scientific and
Cultural Facilities District, and the price of that tax is that the big
institutions open their doors free on scheduled days. Families who learn
the calendar do the zoo, the gardens, the art museum and the history
museum every year for parking money. Families who do not pay $90 a visit.
Here is the system.

**The January ritual:** pull the year's list, put every date your family
can make into the calendar with a two-week-earlier reminder for the
zoo lottery, and you have scheduled eight to twelve outings for $0.

## The library layer

Most Front Range library districts lend **culture passes** — free family
admission checked out like a book: Denver Public Library, Arapahoe,
Jeffco, Douglas, Anythink (Adams), Boulder, Lafayette (WOW! passes),
High Plains and Poudre River (FCMOD and more), and PPLD in the Springs.
Inventory is limited and they go like holds on a bestseller — check the
app on Sunday night for the week ahead. The same buildings run free
storytimes (babies through preschool, weekly, no signup at most
branches), Lego clubs, maker spaces and summer reading programmes with
actual prizes.

## The one-membership rule

Buy one membership a year: the place your kids have asked for twice.
For most metro families that is DMNS (~$150, breaks even in two visits,
and its ASTC reciprocity covers FCMOD, WOW and 350 science museums when
you travel) or the Denver Zoo (~$190 if you are a zoo family). In the
Springs it is Cheyenne Mountain Zoo, full stop. Everything else comes
from the free layer, and the [museum guide](/answers/museums-with-kids-front-range)
ranks what is worth even that.

**CityPASS** (~$62 adult for three attractions) is for visiting
grandparents, not residents — free days beat it.

## Income-qualified programmes

Nearly every SCFD venue honours SNAP EBT admission ($1–3 a person) —
DMNS, the zoo, the Children's Museum, the Gardens, the Aquarium does
not. Colorado's Check Out State Parks programme lends a parks pass
through libraries, and CPW's Independence Pass gives income-qualified
families a $14 annual state parks pass. The rec districts all run
scholarship rates for camps and lessons; ask, they are not advertised.

## The honest math

A family of four that pays walk-up prices for the zoo twice, DMNS twice,
the Gardens twice and the Children's Museum once spends about $560 a
year. The same year on this system — one DMNS membership, the zoo
lottery twice, free-day Gardens, library passes for WOW and the
Butterfly Pavilion — is about $160 plus parking. The kids cannot tell
the difference. That is the whole point of the tax you are already
paying.
