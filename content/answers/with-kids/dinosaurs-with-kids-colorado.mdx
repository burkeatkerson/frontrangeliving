The Denver metro sits on the Morrison Formation — the rock unit that
defined the golden age of American dinosaur discovery, named for the
town where the C-470 crowd now walks its dog. For a kid in the dinosaur
phase this is home-field advantage: within an hour you can stand where a
herd of ornithopods actually walked, watch a real preparator clean a
real bone, and see the results in one of the country's great fossil
halls. Run it as a circuit.

## Rainy-day and gift-shop tier

The **Morrison Natural History Museum** (~$10) is tiny and lets kids
touch actual baby-stegosaurus-era finds with a docent one-on-one — the
best under-6 dinosaur hour in the metro. **Dinosaur Ridge's Discovery
Center** has the touchable bone wall. The **Best Western Denver
Southwest** in Lakewood is, absurdly and wonderfully, a
dinosaur-themed hotel with a fossil-filled lobby, which settles where
the grandparents stay. And the town of **Dinosaur, Colorado** is seven
hours away and mostly a sign — the actual Dinosaur National Monument
wall of bones is worth the trip once, but it is a Utah-line vacation,
not a Saturday.

## Rules that keep it fun

- **No collecting** at any track or bone site — look, touch where signs
  allow, photograph, leave it. Rangers at the Ridge will end a visit
  over a chisel.
- **The hogback has no shade.** The whole Ridge walk is exposed; before
  11 or after 4 in July, water regardless.
- **Trains and dinosaurs share a station:** the C-470 trailhead is ten
  minutes from the [Colorado Railroad Museum](/columns/saturday-with-kids/colorado-railroad-museum),
  which resolves the sibling dispute.
- The Ridge's **Dinosaur Discovery Day** events (monthly-ish, summer)
  add fossil casting and junior-paleontologist stations for a few
  dollars; the calendar is on dinoridge.org.
