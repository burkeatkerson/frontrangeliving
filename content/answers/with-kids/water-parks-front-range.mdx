There are three price tiers of getting wet on purpose here, and the
mistake is living at the top one. Water World is genuinely one of
America's great water parks; it is also $150 a family, which is why
the rec-district pool exists. Sort your summer accordingly.

## Tier 1: the rec-district outdoor pools ($6–12 a kid)

Every district built a leisure pool in the last 25 years, and most
have a slide, a lazy river or a zero-entry spray end — the water park
minus the branding:

Denver's own outdoor pools are free for city kids with a [MY Denver
card](/answers/scfd-free-days-memberships) and cheap for everyone
else; Congress Park and Berkeley are the standouts. Punch cards and
resident rates cut every number above — you rarely pay the listed
walk-up more than twice before buying the card.

## Tier 2: Water World (the big day)

Seventy acres in Federal Heights, run by the Hyland Hills district
since 1979, and still priced like a public amenity if you buy right:
online pick-a-date tickets ($33–50 general, $23–43 child by date,
under 40 inches free), free parking, coolers allowed. The 2026
addition — a drop-capsule slide in the new Summit Canyon — extends
the teen end; Wally World and the zero-entry pools hold the toddler
end; the raft rides in between are the reason it works for a whole
family at once. Strategy: gates at opening, hit Voyage and the Storm
before 11, claim a shaded table early, picnic from the cooler, and
leave at the 3pm wind or stay for the empty last hour. Season passes
(~$120–150 by tier) break even at three visits; district residents'
$22–26 day rate makes Federal Heights and Westminster addresses
quietly great for exactly one demographic.

**Lakeside and Elitch's** are amusement parks with water wings — see
them for coasters, not slides. Elitch's water park is included with
park admission if you are there anyway.

## Tier 3: Great Wolf and the winter problem

**Great Wolf Lodge Colorado Springs** keeps its indoor park at 84°F
all year and sells capped-quantity **day passes from about $30**
(towels included, attraction bundles more) — the strongest answer to
a Springs February that anyone sells. The wave pool and slides suit
6–13; Cub Paw covers the toddler; the arcade will eat whatever you
allow it to. Book the pass online ahead; weekends sell out.

The metro's winter equivalents are humbler and fine: the indoor
leisure pools ([rec-center guide](/answers/rec-centers-swim-lessons-kids))
at Apex, Paul Derda, Westminster City Park and Buck run slides and
warm shallow ends every day of the year at day-pass prices, and
Denver's 13 indoor pools are free for card-holding city kids.
