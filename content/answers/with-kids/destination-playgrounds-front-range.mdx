Somewhere in the 2010s the Front Range's cities got into a quiet arms
race, and the result is a set of playgrounds that justify a
half-hour drive and a packed lunch. They are all free. This is the
circuit, with the intel that matters — shade, fencing, bathrooms —
because the equipment is never the reason a playground day fails.

## How locals run a playground day

**The two-park move.** Destination playgrounds peak 10–noon. Arrive at
9, own it for ninety minutes, lunch from the cooler when the crowd
arrives, then either the adjacent water feature (Centennial,
Westminster, Ralston, Discovery) or a second, sleepier neighborhood
park for round two. One park stretched past nap has a known ending.

**Claim shade first.** Before anyone touches a ladder: drop the
blanket under the shelter or the one good tree. July slides burn bare
legs by 10:30 — the sunscreen-and-water-bottle rules from [the hiking
guide](/answers/hikes-kids-actually-finish) apply verbatim on rubber
surfacing at 5,600 feet.

**Know your fencing.** Fully fenced with one gate (Ralston Central,
Clement's toddler area) means a phone glance is survivable. Open-plan
flagships (Paco Sanchez, Miller) are magnificent and require zone
defense with a runner. Pick by your current roster.

**Bathrooms decide the length of the day.** Real bathrooms: Ralston,
Centennial, Venezia, Westminster, City Park FoCo, Miller. Portalets or
nothing: assume 90 minutes max with anyone under 5.

The wet versions of this list are [the splash pads](/answers/splash-pads-front-range),
the winter versions are [the rec centers](/answers/rec-centers-swim-lessons-kids),
and when the playground stops being enough, [the bike parks](/answers/biking-with-kids-front-range)
are the next arena.
