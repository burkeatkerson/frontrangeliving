The splash pad is the load-bearing institution of the Front Range
toddler summer: free, fenced-ish, and open exactly the months the
playground slides become griddles. Every city has several; these are
the ones locals cross town for, judged on shade, bathrooms and what
else is within a hundred yards.

Stage 1 left the purpose-built pads running but check denvergov.org

## West metro and Jeffco

**Ralston Central Park, Arvada** (5850 Garrison St) is the model:
shade sails over the pad, clean bathrooms, a fenced site, a big
playground, and Olde Town's tacos ten minutes away; timers run 10–8,
Memorial Day on. **Discovery Park in Golden** puts a small pad on
Clear Creek — pad for the toddler, creek for the big kid. **Belmar's
plaza fountains** (Lakewood) handle a shopping-trip meltdown, and
**Crown Hill** and **Morse Park** cover the neighborhood tier.

## North metro and Boulder County

**Westminster Center Park** (City Hall) is a genuine water plaza with
a huge adjacent playground; **The Orchard Town Center** (Westminster)
and **Streets at SouthGlenn** (Centennial) are the retail pads —
shaded, clean, surrounded by lemonade. **Broomfield County Commons**,
**Thornton's Carpenter Park**, and **York Street Yards** fill the
grid. Boulder's **Scott Carpenter Park** pairs its pad with the
rocket-ship playground and the outdoor pool; **Longmont's Kanemoto
Park** and **Roosevelt Park** both run pads next to real pools;
Lafayette's **Great Bark Park** adjacency means the dog can come.

## South metro and Douglas County

**Centennial Center Park** (13050 E Peakview) is the south side's
day-trip pad: a lighthouse water plaza, an amphitheater lawn and a
huge themed playground — shade is thin, so go before 11. **Founders
Park in Castle Rock** and **Philip S. Miller Park's** splash pad (with
the incline and zip lines above it) give Douglas County two strong
options; **Cornerstone Park in Englewood** and **Clement Park in
Littleton** cover the older suburbs; **David A. Lorenz** (South
Suburban) adds shade structures.

## Colorado Springs and south

The city opens its fountains and spray grounds Memorial Day weekend:
**Deerfield Hills** (southeast; 50-plus nozzles, 10–5 daily) and
**Julie Penrose Fountain** in America the Beautiful Park (downtown —
a sculpture that is also a sprinkler) are the two anchors, with
**John Venezia Community Park** (Briargate) the north side's
shaded, bathroom-equipped standout and **Uncle Wilber Fountain**
(Acacia Park, downtown) the beloved pop-up-trumpet classic. **Monument
Valley Pool** and **Wilson Ranch** add paid pool days ($10 youth via
the YMCA partnership). **Fountain's Metcalfe Park** serves the south
corridor, and **Pueblo's** riverwalk splash fountain plus **City
Park's** pool cover the far south.
