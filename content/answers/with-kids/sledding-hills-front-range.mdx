  - q: "When is sledding actually good here?"
    a: "The storm day and the morning after, full stop. Front Range snow falls dry, then 50-degree sun crusts it to ice by afternoon two and strips the hill to grass-and-mud by day three. A foot falls Tuesday night: Wednesday is powder, Thursday morning is fast, Thursday afternoon is luge, Saturday is dirt. The season is really fifteen great days scattered from November to March — take the snow-day morning off and go."
  - q: "What sled works best?"
    a: "A cheap plastic toboggan with rope, or a foam slider — steerable-ish, survivable, $10–20. Snow-day sledding is the Front Range's fastest-expiring perishable:
the same sun that gives you a 55°F January also deletes the hill by
Friday. The locals' whole system is a sled bought in October, a list
of close hills, and a willingness to blow off Wednesday morning. The
list:

## Denver

**Ruby Hill Park** (Florida & Platte River Dr) is the institution —
a genuinely big hill with skyline views, plus **Ruby Hill Rail Yard**,
the free Winter Park-built terrain park where kids lap boxes and
rollers on skis and boards when there's base. **Robinson Park**
(Montview, near City Park) is the east side's generational hill with
multiple pitches. **Barnum Park** (west side) has the shallow slopes
for the 3-to-6 crowd, and **Prairie Meadows** (Green Valley Ranch)
gives the far northeast its own 30-footer without the crowds.
Governor's Park downtown and the Cheesman slopes fill in for the
apartment-dwelling toddler set.

## Boulder, the north, the foothills

Boulder: **Scott Carpenter Park's** dedicated sled hill (lit some
evenings) and **Tantra Park** south. **Foothills Community Park**
adds the view. Longmont sleds **Rothrock Dell** and the **Clark
Centennial** slopes; Fort Collins uses the **Old Town library park**
hill, **Edora**, and **City Park's** nine; Loveland's **Mehaffey
Park** built its hill on purpose. In the foothills the snow lasts:
**Evergreen's Buchanan Park fields**, Nederland's everything, and
**Meyer Ranch Park** off 285 — the closest hill that stays powdery
after Denver melts — where the lower meadow is a beloved unofficial
run (pull off legally; the lot fills).

## The Springs and south

**Cottonwood Creek Park** (north) is the city's best-known hill —
playground at the bottom, multiple pitches. **Mountain Shadows
Park** runs short-and-sweet next to its playground; **Palmer Park's**
meadow slopes and **Monument Valley Park's** downtown banks fill the
middle; **Fox Run Regional Park** in the Black Forest holds snow
longest and adds the pines. Monument and Palmer Lake, a thousand
feet higher, sled a full extra month — **Limbach Park's** edges and
the school hills. Pueblo's rare snow goes to **City Park** within
hours of falling; go immediately.

## The paid tier, when the hills are brown

Mid-winter brown spells are normal at 5,300 feet. The groomed
answer: **Frisco Adventure Park** (the best-run tubing lanes in the
state, book online), **Echo Mountain** (45 min from Denver, the
closest), **Snow Mountain Ranch** and **Copper**. $25–45 a head,
height minimums around 36 inches, sessions timed. It is not the
same sport — it is a conveyor belt and a cashier — but in a snowless
January it delivers, and pairs with [cheap kid skiing](/answers/skiing-with-kids-cheap)
weekends up the same highway.
