There is a day when the [creek-and-payoff hike](/answers/hikes-kids-actually-finish)
stops being impressive and somebody in a booster seat asks to see a
glacier. The Front Range's answer is genuinely rare: alpine terrain —
tundra, snowfields in July, lakes the color of antifreeze — reachable
between breakfast and a late lunch. It just runs on two clocks
nobody tells newcomers about: the reservation clock and the storm
clock.

## The two clocks

**Reservations.** The famous alpine trailheads all metered
themselves: RMNP timed entry, Brainard Lake, the Mount Blue Sky
highway — all on recreation.gov, all released in advance blocks that
sell out, all with small night-before tranches for the flexible.
Book when your month opens or plan the unmanaged alternatives.

**Storms.** Summer convection builds over the peaks by noon nearly
every day; lightning above treeline is the one genuinely
non-negotiable hazard. The family version of the rule: on the trail
by 8, at the lake by 10:30, heading down by 1, ice cream by 3.
Thunder means down, now, mid-sentence.

The free, no-reservation bench: **Guanella Pass** (Georgetown —
tundra walks from the lot, bighorn odds), **Loveland Pass** (snow
until July on the Divide itself), **Kenosha Pass** in late September
(the aspen event), and **Rampart Reservoir** above the Springs.

And the two graduation markers, for later: the first walk-up summit
(Genesee's paths, then Chief Mountain's 1.5-mile top at 11,700 — the
metro's best kid summit), and eventually a real fourteener at 10+
(Bierstadt from Guanella, started at 6am, with a summer of practice
behind it). The mountains aren't going anywhere; the storm clock is
the only deadline.
